# Section 4.1 and Appendix C.2 — Observable algebra, ordering, and contact terms

**Physical question.** How does the paper turn the classical null-cone action into a quantum observable algebra, handle operator ordering and source contact terms, and construct the gravitational clock as a quantum operator?

**Outcome.** The algebraic composition laws for unitary source deformations and the sign and factor of two in the second-order contact formula check out in any regular Hamiltonian representation. At finite mode resolution, bounded-source time evolution is well defined, and the monotone-limit construction rigorously defines a limiting clock resolvent on the closure $M$ of its form domain, given well-defined positive focusing and normalized area-density inputs. Two key continuum steps remain open: showing that the abstract unitary assigned to the gravitational action is realized as a continuum propagator generated by the Weyl-ordered gravitational Hamiltonian (with control over nonlinear canonical changes of variables), and proving at finite $\hbar$ that the domain closure $M$ coincides with the full incoming Hilbert space rather than a proper subspace—a step the paper itself flags as heuristic.

The equation numbers below refer to printed pages 25–26 and 65–67, with cross-checks on pp. 30–33 and 35–40. [Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=25) · [Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=65) · [TeX source archive](https://arxiv.org/src/2611.34924v1)

## Source relations and amplitudes

Rather than quantizing fields directly in one stroke, §4.1 builds a dynamical algebra out of unitary operators $S_I(F)$ labeled by deformations $F$ of a reference action $I$, following the Buchholz–Fredenhagen framework. The table summarizes which relations are exact algebraic consequences of that setup and which require extra analytic input in a representation.

**Interior phase check.** To verify how the shift Ward identity (4.2d) works in ordinary quantum mechanics, shift a single canonical pair $(q,p)$ by smooth functions $(a(t),b(t))$ that vanish near both endpoints. Integration by parts gives the action change

$$
\Delta I_0=\int\big(p\dot a-q\dot b+b\dot a\big)\,dt .
$$

Define the time-dependent unitary $W(t)=e^{-ib(t)\widehat q/\hbar}e^{ia(t)\widehat p/\hbar}$. Differentiating yields $\dot W W^{-1}= (i/\hbar)(\widehat p\dot a-\widehat q\dot b+b\dot a)$, while both endpoint operators reduce to the identity. The scalar term $b\dot a$ accounts exactly for the Weyl phase. The same check makes clear why a displacement whose parameters do not vanish at the boundaries leaves nontrivial endpoint operators behind.

**Universal norm and regularity.** Because every unitary has norm $1$, the triangle inequality guarantees $\|\sum c_jg_j\|\le\sum |c_j|$ in every unitary representation, so the universal $C^*$-seminorm exists. Representing the gravitational model, however, demands more: the prescribed scalar phases must survive the quotient, and a chosen representation must actually realize the canonical operator products and source derivatives. A universal-norm bound supplies neither property. Even in the ordinary Schrödinger representation, $\|e^{ia\widehat q}-1\|=2$ for every real $a\ne0$, so unitaries are not norm-continuous in the source parameter. Source derivatives must therefore be taken weakly on sufficiently regular vectors or strongly on generator domains; differentiating an abstract universal relation without specifying the representation and domains does not establish a distributional Schwinger–Dyson identity. Buchholz and Fredenhagen explicitly draw this same boundary between constructing a scalar-field algebra and proving that physically distinguished states exist. [Buchholz and Fredenhagen, §§3–5 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.06062)

## Ordering, local terms, and covariance

Differentiating the source unitaries twice produces time-ordered two-point functions together with same-time contact terms. For $j$-differentiable bounded interactions with norm-integrable derivatives near $j=0$, set $O_r(t)=-\partial_j^rH[j](t)|_0$. In the interaction picture the exponent has generator $(i/\hbar)(jO_1+j^2O_2/2+\cdots)$, whose second derivative at $j=0$ is

$$
\frac{i}{\hbar}\int O_{2,I}(t)\,dt-
\frac{2}{\hbar^2}\int_{t>s}O_{1,I}(t)O_{1,I}(s)\,dt\,ds .
$$

This reproduces both the signs and the factor of two in (4.28) and (C.7–C.8). When an independent source is coupled at each time, the first term is diagonal in the two source times whenever the Hamiltonian depends locally on the sources. Holding the reduced coordinates fixed while differentiating automatically captures source dependence entering through the embeddings $\iota_{j,t}$ and $\chi_{j,t}$.

A simple mechanical check is the velocity-coupled Lagrangian $L_j=m\dot q^2/2+j a(t)\dot q$. Its canonical Hamiltonian is $(p-ja)^2/(2m)$, giving $O_1=a\widehat p/m$ and $O_2=-a^2/m$. Direct time evolution on Schwartz vectors produces the phase $-ij^2\int a^2/(2m\hbar)$ in the exponent, reproducing the contact term exactly. By contrast, a source entering linearly in a fixed reduced Hamiltonian has $O_2=0$. In continuum field theory, additional contact terms can arise when extending singular time-ordered products to coincident points. The cited Hollands–Wald theorems treat renormalized perturbative scalar fields on fixed curved backgrounds; the reduced gravitational composites require their own renormalization prescriptions and Ward-identity checks. [Hollands and Wald 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103074) · [Hollands and Wald 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0404074)

**Nonlinear changes of canonical variables.** Equation (C.9) writes the Weyl–Moyal star product using a constant symplectic matrix in a chosen Darboux chart. For suitable finite-dimensional symbols it is an exact oscillatory integral, and for general symbols a formal differential series that terminates on polynomial pairs. Smoothing a linear field before multiplying and forming a local product before smearing are distinct operations, and (C.10) works explicitly order by order—it provides no convergence or domain theorem for the exponential of a positive unbounded field composite.

Moreover, Weyl ordering does not commute with nonlinear canonical transformations. Consider the canonical shear $P=p+q^2$, implemented unitarily by $e^{-iq^3/(3\hbar)}\widehat p\,e^{iq^3/(3\hbar)}=\widehat p+\widehat q^2$. Evaluating the Moyal product (C.9) exactly gives

$$
P\star P=P^2,\qquad P^{\star 3}=P^3-\hbar^2/2,
\qquad P^{\star 4}=P^4-2\hbar^2P .
$$

More generally, right-multiplying any smooth function $F(P)$ by $P$ yields $F(P)P-(\hbar^2/4)F''(P)$. Conjugating $\widehat p^4$ therefore produces the Weyl operator of $P^4-2\hbar^2P$, whereas substituting $p\mapsto P$ classically in $p^4$ and reimposing Weyl ordering gives a different operator. Because the discrepancy depends on the operator $P$, no scalar phase at the endpoints can absorb it. While the linear translation check above remains exact, this finite-dimensional example isolates what must be proved when (C.1) and cap independence involve nonlinear changes of reduced variables: one needs a quantized transition rule, a restricted class of admissible transitions, or a fixed polarization with a proved intertwiner. This is a structural check on the ordering rule; an anomaly for a specific gravitational transformation has not been calculated here. [Gotay, polynomial quantization obstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/9809015)

## Clock form and finite-resolution estimates

Appendix C.2 constructs the quantum gravitational clock by first capping the null focusing functional $\Xi_y(v)$, forming a closed quadratic form, and then removing both the focusing cap and a spectral resolvent cutoff $L$. Each step is clear to audit separately:

- **Limiting resolvent, (C.11–C.14).** Assume positive self-adjoint focusing operators $\Xi_y(v)$, measurable quadratic forms, and the normalized area-density identity $\int\|\widehat\varrho_S(y)\xi\|^2d\mu(y)=\mathscr A\|\xi\|^2$ on its stated domain. Positivity and this normalization force $\ker\widehat{\mathsf a}=0$ whenever $\mathscr A>0$. The capped quadratic form is bounded above and below by positive multiples of $\|\widehat{\mathsf a}^{-1/2}\psi\|^2$, so its form norm is equivalent to the complete graph norm and the form is closed. As the cap is removed, monotone convergence of closed forms yields the limiting resolvent $L(L+U)^{-1}P_M$, where $M=\overline{\mathrm{dom}\,Q}$, with convergence in vector norm for each fixed $\psi$ (strong operator convergence). Completing the square in (C.12) and using $\sup_{u\ge0} uL^2/(L+u)^2=L/4$ gives (C.14). Sending $L\to\infty$ then recovers $P_M\psi$. [Behrndt et al., Theorem 4.2](https://www.numerik.math.tu-graz.ac.at/~behrndt/BehrndtHassiSnooWietsma09.pdf)
- **Wigner expansion at finite resolution, (C.15a–e).** For finitely many Gaussian pairs, the Wigner function integrates to $1$ over each $(q_j,p_j)$ and has momentum variance $\hbar^2/[4(\Delta q_j)^2]$. Assuming the classical clock reading is finite almost surely at that mode resolution, monotone convergence in the cap followed by dominated convergence in $L$ proves (C.15b). Expanding $(L+u)\star f=u$ gives $f_0=u_0/(L+u_0)$ and $f_1=Lu_1/(L+u_0)^2$ because $\{u_0,f_0\}=0$. Both the local coefficient recursion and the Gaussian Wick factor $1/(2^m m!)$ are exact, but bounding each coefficient on a fixed regular ball supplies no remainder estimate uniform in expansion order, mode resolution, or focusing cutoff at fixed $\hbar$.
- **Classical versus quantum focusing tails, (C.16a–d).** Operator monotonicity of $x/(L+x)$ proves the capped quantum bound (C.16a). The conformal-metric contraction $\frac12\operatorname{tr}[(\bar q^{-1}d\bar q)^2]=(dz^1)^2+e^{2z^1}(dz^2)^2$ verifies the $1/4$ focusing coefficient, and integrating $-\partial_v\log\partial_u\varrho$ gives the log ratio in (C.16b). However, the indicator in (C.16c) and the compact-set estimate (C.16d) bound only a *classical* probability at fixed resolution; what is missing is the corresponding quantum bound when removing the focusing cap, a step the footnote to (5.16) explicitly calls heuristic.
- **Coherent displacements, (C.17).** Finite differences of the unnormalized displaced vacuum converge in Hilbert norm to its occupation-state derivatives. If every sufficiently small displacement (including joint displacements of each finite set of modes) belongs to $M$, closedness of $M$ implies that all occupation vectors belong to $M$. This implication is valid, but its premise still requires finite-$\hbar$ domain control.

**One-mode check of the proposed probability comparison.** To test whether positive Gaussian Wigner densities automatically bound quantum resolvent expectations from above, take the ground state of a single harmonic oscillator $\widehat u=(\widehat p^2+\widehat q^2)/2$. It is an exact eigenstate with eigenvalue $\hbar/2$, whereas its positive Wigner density gives the classical variable $u=(p^2+q^2)/2$ a nonconstant exponential distribution of the same mean $\hbar/2$. Strict concavity of $u\mapsto u/(L+u)$ implies, for every $L>0$,

$$
\int W\frac{u}{L+u}\,dq\,dp
< \frac{\hbar/2}{L+\hbar/2}
=\left\langle\frac{\widehat u}{L+\widehat u}\right\rangle .
$$

Gaussian Wigner positivity alone therefore cannot furnish the classical upper bound on a quantum resolvent expectation invoked in (5.16). This toy model tests that inference step; it does not determine the exact gravitational clock spectrum.

**A finite-mode clock with zero form domain.** The gap between classical and quantum tails can persist even in the limit $L\to\infty$. In units $\hbar=1$, take one canonical pair $X=x$, $P=-i\partial_x$, constant area weights, and two positive focusing operators $2X^2$ and $2P^2$, defining the uncapped clock form

$$
Q[\psi]=\|e^{X^2}\psi\|^2+\|e^{P^2}\psi\|^2 .
$$

Its leading classical symbol $e^{2x^2}+e^{2p^2}$ is finite everywhere and has vanishing high-reading probability under every Gaussian Wigner distribution, and both capped summands are bounded operators. Yet finiteness of the uncapped form requires both $e^{x^2}\psi(x)$ and $e^{\xi^2}\widehat\psi(\xi)$ to lie in $L^2$ (with Fourier convention $e^{-ix\xi}$). By the Gaussian-weighted $L^2$ uncertainty theorem, the only such vector is $\psi=0$. Thus $M=\{0\}$, (C.13) vanishes at every $L$ once the cap is removed, and the exact overlap loss is $1$ even as $L\to\infty$. This one-pair model isolates the missing noncommutative estimate; whether the actual gravitational focusing operators avoid this collapse depends on their joint domains. [Cowling et al., statement (A2)](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~ponce/co-es-ke-po-ve.pdf)

**Radial ultraviolet check.** Even before noncommuting conjugates enter, removing the radial mode cutoff challenges finite-resolution concentration. In a linear one-polarization model, expand $z_N=\sum_{k\le N}q_k e_k(v)$ in normalized Dirichlet sine modes on $[\epsilon,1]$ with the independent Gaussian variance $1/(2sk)$ from (4.32). Direct summation gives

$$
\mathbb E\!\int_\epsilon^1 |\partial_v z_N|^2\,dv
=\frac{\pi^2}{2s(1-\epsilon)^2}\sum_{k=1}^N k .
$$

For $\epsilon>0$, the radial weight $v$ in the positive shear square leaves this divergence intact. Worse, the unweighted integral diverges almost surely as $N\to\infty$: the independent events $q_k^2\ge 1/(2sk)$ all have the same positive probability, so infinitely many occur, each contributing a term proportional to $k$. A finite-resolution Gaussian concentration bound therefore cannot be assumed uniform in the number of radial modes. This check diagnoses the bare quadratic linearization; settling the nonlinear gravitational clock requires a continuum ultraviolet definition that simultaneously preserves the positivity used in the clock construction.
