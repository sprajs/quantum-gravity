# Recovering the curved clock from probes inside its diamond

On the symmetric plane wave there is a prescription for approaching the distributional clock source by conserved smooth sources strictly inside the measured diamond. It gives a joined cap-and-sheet pairing for smooth extendible gravitational perturbations. The construction uses local homogeneity of the plane-wave background and a new, locally calibrated emitter for each smaller experiment. [Curved clock and emitter calibration](weak-curved.md) · [Flat closed-diamond construction](weak-boundary.md)

**Preparation.** Use the regular spherical diamond with tips $p,q$ on the same timelike geodesic, before any conjugate points or generator crossings, in the globally hyperbolic Rosen strip specified in the curved calculation. Make the transverse periods large enough that a neighborhood of the closed diamond lifts injectively to the covering plane wave. Use the first-slot convention $\Omega(X,Z)=j[Z]$. The local tidal calibration fixes the emitter's longitudinal velocity component $V^u$ at launch. The angular mean is stationary under the remaining variations of its launch marks at this background. The resulting source $j$ is compactly supported in the closed filled diamond, including its launch and return data, and obeys $\nabla_\mu j^{\mu\nu}=0$ as a distribution.

## An interior regularization that respects the constraint

1. Move the past and future tips a small proper time toward the midpoint along the background geodesic. The smaller closed diamond lies strictly inside the original open one, by timelike transitivity and compactness. At each new past tip introduce a fresh local launch mark and impose the same tidal calibration there. Use the shorter proper duration. Its source $j_\epsilon$ is obtained by differentiating this smaller experiment at the original plane-wave metric. Any radius-matched version uses the background radius of the smaller experiment and requires a nonzero derivative of that radius with respect to return time. Refer all perturbed launch data to the new mark. Transporting them from the old launch could retain source support at the old vertex.

2. Each $j_\epsilon$ is conserved by the same infinitesimal isometry argument as $j$. The optical transport, observer propagation and endpoint constraint derivatives converge on every smooth metric test as $\epsilon\to0$. Their orders as distributions remain uniformly bounded on a common compact set because the waist remains transverse and the cap slope stays positive. This proves convergence of the sources in the sense needed here.

3. Average the pushed-forward tensor distributions over a small smooth unit-mass bump on the local plane-wave isometry group. Take its support small enough that every translated smaller diamond remains strictly inside the original one, and let that support shrink to the identity with $\epsilon$. Isometries preserve the divergence constraint. The group action is transitive on spacetime, so its orbit map at any point is a submersion. In local group coordinates the map separates into four spacetime coordinates and stabilizer coordinates. Integrating the smooth group density over the latter gives a smooth kernel in the source and target points, including the smooth transport of tensor indices. The resulting $\tilde j_\epsilon$ is therefore smooth. This argument uses local isometries on the injectively lifted compact set; the full group need not preserve the global torus identifications. Extending by zero outside the support gives a global smooth conserved source. Its pairing with any fixed smooth test converges to $j[Z]$ by the preceding uniform bounds.

## Which boundary values the response needs

Write $S$ for the waist sphere. It is acausal in this preparation. A causal link between two distinct points on it, combined with a generator from $p$, would either place the second point in the chronological future of $p$ or put both points on the same generator. Regularity and the once-per-ray intersection exclude both possibilities. Choose a smooth spacelike Cauchy surface $\Sigma$ containing $S$. Such extension is available for a compact acausal spacelike submanifold in a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Choose a complete timelike flow that follows the observer between the tips. Each compact achronal null face projects injectively along this flow onto a topological closed ball in $\Sigma$. The two balls share their boundary $S$ and the interior point to which the observer's two tips project. Every future generator from $p$ hits $S$ before a cut point and every past generator from $q$ does so as well. Thus the two causal shadows on $\Sigma$ are the same closed ball $B=\Sigma\cap J^+(p)=\Sigma\cap J^-(q)$. Points of $\Sigma$ exterior to $B$ cannot be causally related to the filled diamond. This argument relies on the regular two-cone geometry and the exclusion of wrapped null paths. [Bernal and Sánchez, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0512095)

For every interior approximation the smooth field $X_\epsilon=-E_{\rm EH}\tilde j_\epsilon$ has Cauchy data supported strictly inside $B$. Green's identity gives $\Omega_B(X_\epsilon,Z)=\tilde j_\epsilon[Z]$ for smooth extendible vacuum $Z$. Conservation gives the same number as the sum of the cap current and the sheet current, with a common geometric marking. Taking the limit gives $j[Z]$ without separately restricting a product of singular fields to a null face. The causal field $X=-E_{\rm EH}j$ has well-defined distributional data on $\Sigma$, since its characteristic singular covectors are null and $\Sigma$ is spacelike. The equality also follows by testing the Cauchy evolution against smooth data. The curved calculation supplies smooth probe sequences for which this pairing tends to $-0.060534188\ldots$.
