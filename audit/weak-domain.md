# Which probes can measure a weak spherical clock generator?

A sharp clock on a regular spherical light cone has a distributional causal response on a specified globally hyperbolic continuation, provided its preparation defines a compact, covariant metric differential. The response pairs with every smooth compactly supported conserved source. Its position and momentum on a spacelike Cauchy surface are distributions with canonical restrictions. Taking its value on a null generator or at a sharp cut requires a further argument. In four-dimensional linearized vacuum gravity, two positive time averages of the same spherical curvature reading, with identical mean and variance, can have a mutual bracket proportional to the inverse fourth power of their timing width. The calculation below concerns the ambient classical field and Chandrasekaran's clock at linear order about a fixed solution. [Chandrasekaran, arXiv:2611.34924v1, Eqs. (3.26)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E27)

## Construction on smooth probes

Take a smooth globally hyperbolic vacuum spacetime without boundary and a compact, pre-caustic diamond inside it. Assume that the two cones intersect transversely, the optical radius is increasing with nonzero slope at the fractional cut, and each implicit observer or radius condition has a nonsingular linearization. Require the complete marking prescription to depend differentiably on finitely many metric derivatives on the sampled compact set, or on finite-time smooth transport of such data. This includes the path of any clock or frame used to select the tips. With these hypotheses the clock is defined off shell near the chosen geometry. Variations of the geodesic, Jacobi and frame equations obey linear inhomogeneous transport. On their compact parameter intervals their solutions are bounded by a finite metric seminorm. Implicit differentiation of the endpoints gives the same bound. Smooth angular integration preserves it. Consequently the differential extends to a symmetric tensor distribution density $J$ supported on the closed set sampled by the preparation. This support can include both cones, the rim, the waist, tips and observer data. A prescription that selects an emitter using data extending over a whole Cauchy surface must establish compactness separately. [Geometric differential including moving endpoints](joint-jacobi.md)

Let $E$ be retarded minus advanced response for the formally self-adjoint gauge-fixed Einstein Hessian, with the action normalization and trace reversal included in the source-to-metric map. The de Donder equation has normally hyperbolic principal part after the invertible algebraic trace reversal. For a smooth compact tensor source $f$, define the distributional causal response $Y_J$ by transposition,
$$
   \langle f,Y_J\rangle=-\langle J,Ef\rangle.                         \tag{1}
$$
Skew adjointness of $E$ makes $Y_J=EJ$. Both sides of (1) have meaning for every compact finite-order $J$. The retarded and advanced extensions have the respective causal supports. Their difference solves the homogeneous gauge-fixed equation and has spacelike-compact support. This proof never restricts the kernel to a cone. With the first-slot convention $\Omega(X_J,h)=J(h)$ and $\Omega=\int\delta\pi\wedge\delta q$, the weak Hamiltonian field is $X_J=-Y_J$. Thus $\{T,F_f\}=J(Ef)=\langle f,X_J\rangle$. [Bär, Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.0738#page=18) · [Gravitational source normalization](joint-scaling.md)

For the bulk gauge quotient require $J(\mathcal L_\xi g)=0$ for all compactly supported vector fields $\xi$. Equivalently, $\nabla_a J^{ab}=0$ distributionally, with the density pairing understood. Diffeomorphism-equivariant metric marks give this identity by differentiating the observable. With a conserved source the de Donder constraint of each fundamental solution obeys the homogeneous vector wave equation with vanishing past or future data. Distributional uniqueness gives zero, so $Y_J$ and $X_J$ solve the linearized Einstein equation. Smooth conserved probes pair equally with gauge-equivalent representatives of $X_J$. Changes in a smooth probe's representative $Ef$ by a pure gauge field drop out of (1). The compact support of $J$ permits a local cutoff on the gauge vector. If two choices of off-shell current agree on all smooth physical solutions, their mixed brackets agree. With independent external tips or frames the gauge identity instead differentiates the metric and the marks together. Freezing their coordinate components generally fails the bulk conservation test. [Fewster and Hunt, §§2.2–3.3 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.0261#page=10) · [Marked scaling and intrinsic alternatives](joint-scaling.md)

The wavefront set $\mathrm{WF}(X_J)$ records the covectors where the response fails to be smooth. It is contained in the null covectors, because $X_J$ solves the homogeneous hyperbolic equation. The same inclusion holds after one covariant derivative. On a spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$ the normal covectors are timelike. The pullback theorem therefore defines both $X_J|_\Sigma$ and $\nabla_n X_J|_\Sigma$ as distributions. The Einstein Cauchy current with a smooth second solution uses each of these against smooth data. On a complete Cauchy surface the first-slot pairing of $X_J$ with a smooth solution $h$ equals $J(h)$. This follows by the Green identity for smooth sources and duality, or by testing against solutions constructed with smooth Cauchy data. Restricting further to the **boundary** of a cap or pairing with a discontinuous indicator for half a Cauchy surface is an additional operation. A rim delta in the complete initial momentum can disappear on restriction to the open cap. [Hörmander, Theorem 8.2.4](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61497-2) · [Explicit cap momentum impulse](joint-peierls.md)

There is a Poisson algebra for **one** sharp linearized reading $L_J(h)=J(h)$. On a fixed background take the polynomial algebra generated by $L_J$ and smooth conserved linear probes on smooth solutions modulo gauge. Use the usual brackets between smooth probes, (1) for a mixed pair and $\{L_J,L_J\}=0$. These constants define a skew form on the generators. If $L_J$ is equal on solutions to a smooth probe $F_g$, the mixed formula agrees with $\{F_g,F_f\}$, including $J(Eg)=g(Eg)=0$. For the smooth source quotient take the usual causal-kernel quotient, or a reduction on which the vanishing smooth probes have pure-gauge causal response. The bracket thus respects linear relations. Leibniz gives a Poisson bracket, since the Jacobi identities on the generators have constant inner brackets. It gives an algebraic Hamiltonian derivation even when the response leaves the smooth tangent space. This construction uses one differential at one background. A nonlinear Poisson bracket would additionally differentiate the clock current and the metric-dependent inverse.

## A domain for two sources

Write $\mathcal C$ for parallel transport of null cotangent vectors along null geodesics, including zero propagation distance. The wavefront set of $EK$ is contained in $\mathcal C(\mathrm{WF}(K))$. A sufficient domain for the pairing $J(EK)$ of two compact currents is
$$
 \mathrm{WF}(J)\ \cap\ [-\mathcal C(\mathrm{WF}(K))]=\varnothing.       \tag{2}
$$
This makes their contracted product a distribution and compact support permits integration. For currents on smooth strata, one can often check (2) with their conormals. Check the ends of each stratum as well as its interior. The conormal to a null sheet is its own null generator covector. A spacelike two-sphere has a two-dimensional Lorentzian normal plane with two null directions. Angular averaging alone leaves those normals possible. If (2) fails, a tensor contraction or a separately specified limiting procedure can still give a value. [Duistermaat and Hörmander, propagation along bicharacteristics](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02392165) · [Hörmander, distribution products and pullbacks](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61497-2)

There is a simple sufficient finite-resolution prescription. Push a compact current $K$ by the smooth flow $\Phi_\tau$ of a vector field $V$ timelike throughout the swept support, and integrate against a smooth compact timing density $w(\tau)$. In a flow chart this convolves the current in the time variable. Equivalently the pushforward under $(\tau,x)\mapsto\Phi_\tau x$ can carry an output covector $k$ only if $k(V)=0$, since $w$ is smooth. Such nonzero covectors are spacelike, so $E\int w(\tau)\Phi_{\tau *}K\,d\tau$ is smooth. The averaged current can still be supported on a spatial surface. Every compact sharp current can act on its response. For an actual family of measurements the same argument applies to a sum of smooth weighted finite derivatives of currents on swept strata if the parameter velocity on **each** stratum, including endpoints, is timelike. Each gravitational reading must satisfy its Ward identity. A timelike isometry translating a conserved family is one realization. Averaging only the fractional cut radius while retaining the same fixed null face gives no such transverse smoothing of that face. [Wavefront bounds for pushforward, Brouder, Dang and Hélein](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.7662) · [Finite-resolution cylindrical clock](joint-resolution.md)

## A spherical curvature measurement at the boundary of that domain

In Minkowski space write $\langle\cdot\rangle$ for probability measure on the unit sphere. Let $K(\tau;r,s)$ be the causal scalar wave kernel of $\partial_t^2-\Delta$ averaged over two concentric spheres of radii $r,s>0$ separated by time $\tau$. Averaging $\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\delta(\tau^2-|rn-sn'|^2)/(2\pi)$ over the uniform cosine $n\cdot n'$ gives
$$
 K(\tau;r,s)=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}\tau}{8\pi rs}
      \mathbf1_{|r-s|<|\tau|<r+s}                                       \tag{3}
$$
as a distribution in $\tau$. Equivalently the spherical Fourier multiplier is $\sin(kr)/(kr)$ for $k=|\mathbf k|$. At $r=s$ the two time limits at zero differ. For round cuts on opposite faces of a flat radius-$R$ diamond, $\tau=2R-r-s$ with $0<r,s<R$. The threshold $r+s=R$ places antipodal points on a null geodesic orthogonal to both cut spheres. Approaching through $r+s<R$ gives zero. Approaching through $r+s>R$ gives $1/(8\pi rs)$ in the limit. An ordinary radial smearing across that threshold integrates a step function. Evaluation at the exactly critical pair of cuts needs a prescription. [Opposite-face restriction on the sheet interiors](joint-scaling.md)

There is a physical gravitational probe of this singularity. Use an inertial tetrad and a constant spatial symmetric trace-free tensor $S^{ij}$. Read the electric Weyl curvature on a sphere,
$$
 Q_r(t)=\big\langle S^{ij} C^{(1)}_{i0j0}(t,rn)\big\rangle .
$$
The background Weyl tensor vanishes. The reading therefore annihilates pure-gauge metric variations, and a first-order movement of the sphere or tetrad leaves its linearization unchanged. In vacuum TT gauge $C^{(1)}_{i0j0}=-\ddot h^{\rm TT}_{ij}/2$. The ADM kinetic term gives the momentum $\dot h^{\rm TT}_{ij}/(32\pi G_N)$, with the bracket projected to TT tensors. For a spatial Fourier direction $m$ put $P=1-mm^T$. The TT projection is $PSP-\tfrac12P\operatorname{tr}(PS)$. Its contraction with $S$, averaged over $m$, is $(2/5)S:S$ by the spherical second and fourth moments. The sphere multipliers in (3) depend only on $k$. The canonical ordering $\{h(0),\dot h(0)\}=32\pi G_N\Pi^{\rm TT}$ therefore gives
$$
 \{Q_r(0),Q_s(\tau)\}=\frac{16\pi G_N}{5}(S:S)\,\partial_\tau^4 K(\tau;r,s).
$$
For $s=r$ and $|\tau|<2r$, the exact result as a distribution in the time delay is
$$
 \{Q_r(0),Q_r(\tau)\}=c_r\,\delta'''(\tau),
 \qquad c_r=\frac{4G_N(S:S)}{5r^2}.                                    \tag{4}
$$
It has a finite value against each smooth time response. An antisymmetric bracket for a single sharp symbol always assigns its self-bracket zero. [TT waves and the angular projection on the round screen](joint-flat-hessian.md)

Choose smooth compact probability profiles $a,b$, rescaled to $a_h(t)=a(t/h)/h$ and $b_h(t)=b(t/h)/h$. For small enough $h$ all time differences lie within $(-2r,2r)$. Equation (4) gives their cross-bracket exactly as $-c_r h^{-4}\int a b'''$. One can match the two centers and variances while keeping this coefficient nonzero. Take $a$ even, smooth, compactly supported and positive on an interval where $a^{(6)}$ has a strict sign. Choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth bump $g$ inside that interval and put $b=a+\lambda g'''$. Its first three moments of orders $0,1,2$ agree with $a$ by integration by parts. A sufficiently small nonzero $\lambda$ keeps $b$ nonnegative. Now $\int a b'''=\lambda\int a^{(6)}g\ne0$. Both readouts converge to $Q_r(0)$ on every smooth metric variation. Identical profiles have zero mutual bracket for every $h$. Thus even positive, centered angular and time averaging gives no regulator-independent *simultaneous sharp limit for all spherical curvature probes*. The spherical clock Hessian has its own finite, timing-dependent contact in a calibrated quadrupole experiment. [Translated clock calculation](weak-clock-contact.md)

An isolated diamond also requires the regional gauge and boundary equations. Equation (1) specifies an ambient metric response. A regional inverse must say which cap and relative-frame fields vary, how flux at the waist is accounted for and on which test space a rim distribution acts. Green operators restrict from an ambient spacetime to a causally compatible globally hyperbolic open set for interior sources. A current supported on the boundary lies outside that uniqueness statement. These are proof gaps for the isolated finite-cap theory and its quantum projector. [Regional versus continued geometry](match-reduction.md) · [Original moving-boundary potential, Eq. (B.21)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A2.E21)

## Reproduce the spherical calculation

Run `python3 -B checks/weak_domain.py` from the repository root, then `make check` for the full suite. The focused check uses the independent Fourier integral with multiplier $e^{-\eta k}$, takes both sides of the antipodal threshold, integrates the curvature contact against a compact cubic test and checks the time profiles and their moments by exact polynomial integration. The numerical profiles are compact piecewise polynomials with sufficient continuous derivatives. The proof above gives smooth profiles. Expected output follows.
