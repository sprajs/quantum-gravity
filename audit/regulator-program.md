# A regulator test for the clock and the dimension of a causal diamond

**The physical question.** *How can we test—both numerically at finite resolution and analytically as the cutoff is removed—whether the quantum causal diamond contains any allowed states, and whether the number of allowed states stays finite?*

**The short answer.** *Two distinct criteria settle the two claims in §5, and finite-matrix stability alone cannot decide either one without continuum estimates.* Producing even one state with controlled quantum focusing and nonzero overlap with the incoming Gaussian would prove the diamond is nonempty. Bounding **every** state admitted by the clock—including corner excitations and states reached by area-changing insertions—would prove finite dimension. The program below lays out separate tests for both claims and includes an exactly solvable corner benchmark with reproducible numerical counts (`python checks/corner_clock.py`). The full interacting gravitational regulator remains to be constructed. [§5.1, definition of the diamond](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS1.p2.1) · [§5.2, area bound](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2.p2.1)

## 1. Specify the limits and the physical Hilbert space

Fix the capped diamond, with normalized spherical reference measure, $0<\epsilon<1$, a round reference radius $b$, and physical threshold $\ell$. Freeze $G_N,\hbar$ and $s=\pi b^2/(G_N\hbar)$ as resolution increases. The interval $v\in[\epsilon,1]$ and the angular sphere already have finite extent. Sending $\epsilon$ to zero changes the physical boundary problem. For a first run choose $\epsilon=1/4$, $s=10^4$, and sweep $\ell/[(1-\epsilon)b]$ below, at and above one. These are test parameters. Repeat separately at other fixed values. The round classical clock is $u_0=(1-\epsilon)b$. [§3.2, cap and affine normalization](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS2.p6.1) · [§5.1, spherical preparation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS1)

| Control | What to vary |
|---|---|
| Physical field resolution | Angular degree $J$, radial degree $K$, corner harmonic degree $J_c$. The round background gives radial scale of order $b(1-\epsilon)/K$ and transverse scale of order $vb/J$ at cut $v$. Refine $K$ at fixed $J$, $J$ at fixed $K$, and then both, including unequal rates. |
| Numerical resolution | Quadrature in $v,y$, matrix arithmetic precision, and Hermite occupation cutoff $M$ per retained pair or a specified total occupation budget. Remove the numerical oscillator cutoff at **fixed** field content and fixed focusing ceiling before interpreting any field refinement. |
| Focusing ceiling and clock filter | Write $c=\Xi_*$ and $L$ for the length in (5.5). At fixed $L/b$ control the limit $c\to\infty$. Only afterwards increase $L/b$. Removing the field resolution before or after $c$ requires its own convergence argument. |
| Representation | Record Gaussian widths, canonical coordinate map, and how states at successive cutoffs are embedded. Start with the same low-mode Gaussian, embedding absent modes in their reference vacua. (4.32) specifies example widths for a particular radial source. Other radiative modes and the corner covariance need an explicit choice. Changing widths at each refinement can change the representation. |

A target continuum prescription is to construct the bounded resolvents at fixed $c,L$ on a common fixed-area space, then take $c\to\infty$ followed by $L\to\infty$. Compare against removing $c$ first at fixed $J,K,J_c$. Different results identify a missing uniform estimate. Let $T$ be the limiting clock on $\mathcal M=\overline{D(Q)}$, with the resolvent extended by zero on $\mathcal M^\perp$. Then the surviving Gaussian weight is $\lim_{L\to\infty}\langle\Omega,L(L+T)^{-1}P_{\mathcal M}\Omega\rangle=\|P_{\mathcal M}\Omega\|^2$. Positive weight establishes support at some finite reading. The weight below a *prescribed* $\ell$ is a separate measurement. [Appendix C.2, monotone forms and the filter](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p3.1)

## 2. Build the constraints before counting states

1. **Linear radiative calculation.** Start with a single real trace-free tensor harmonic of degree $j=2$. Then include both electric and magnetic parity and all $m=-j,\ldots,j$ through $J$. For fixed end metrics use $x(v)=vh(v)=\sum_{k=1}^K x_k\sqrt{2/d}\sin[k\pi(v-\epsilon)/d]$, $d=1-\epsilon$. Form the antisymmetric matrix of $\int(x'y-y'x)dv$ and the symmetric matrix of the quadratic cost $Q_{\rm rad}$ in (D.40). For each trial function compute
   $$
   \varpi(v)=v^{-2}\!\left[\epsilon^2\varpi(\epsilon)-\tfrac{t_j}{2}\int_\epsilon^v w^2h'(w)\,dw\right],
   \qquad t_j^2=(j-1)(j+2)/2 .
   $$
   Use the prescribed normal-connection boundary condition. If both endpoint values are fixed, restrict coefficients by the resulting linear endpoint condition before diagonalizing. Do the same for any additional fixed shear or cap data. Evaluate the full constraint residual on an independent quadrature grid. Bring the restricted symplectic matrix to canonical pairs, recording its kernel and the boundary condition or gauge symmetry responsible for each removed direction. An odd number of real trial directions can produce a spurious kernel. The real profile coefficients do not each supply an independent oscillator. [Appendix D.3, constraints and pairing](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS3.p2.1) · [Appendix A.3, boundary terms in the inverse](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A1.SS3.p4.1)
2. **Measure the one-loop cost.** For a cost written $Q_{\rm rad}=\tfrac12 x^{T}Ax$ and reduced symplectic matrix $B$, the positive excitation costs are $\hbar/\sigma_i$, one per canonical pair, where $\sigma_i$ are the nonzero paired singular values of $A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}$. Include the physical prefactor of $B$ from the symplectic potential and the chosen tensor normalization. Record the counts below fixed positive costs, including $m$ and parity multiplicities. Compare to the $O((s\nu)^3)$ upper bound and the radial/angular competition $|k|/s+j(j+1)/(s|k|)$ at large mode numbers. Keep the occupation cost relative to the oscillator ground state distinct from the absolute zero-point contribution to the positive clock. The transverse cost $\int|\varpi|^2dv$ used here depends on fixed transverse expansion data. Equating a clock bound with a bound on this cost for variable cap and corner data would require a new inequality. [Appendix D.3, mode count and occupation budget](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS3.p2.2)
3. **Restore the corner and the nonlinear reduction.** Use scalar harmonics for the area profile $f_S$ (its additive constant is redundant) and the relative normal boost, and vector harmonics for the relative angular identification and its conjugate momentum. Keep $j=0,1$ whenever their charges or conjugates survive the specified quotient. A common change of frame is a gauge symmetry, whereas the paper retains relative frame data. Reconstruct the conformal metric on regular dyad patches. Angular tensor harmonics give linear perturbations in these patches. For large fields use a positive unit-determinant metric parametrization and check agreement across patches. Solve Raychaudhuri, angular momentum transport, cross-focusing and transverse shear transport with the correct end data. For two joined cuts check the pullback of $q$, the two boost-rescaled null extrinsic curvatures and the gradient shift in $\varpi$. On spacelike caps impose the vacuum Hamiltonian and momentum constraints and corner compatibility. Track the expansion, metric determinant and occurrence of a turning point or intersection. Quantify any excluded configurations and their probability before drawing a conclusion about the Gaussian. [§3.2, phase space](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS2.p3.1) · [§3.5, transverse transport](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS5.p2.2) · [§3.6, matching](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS6.p3.1)
4. **Obtain the quantum fields.** Pull back the complete symplectic potential to the constrained coefficients, including the bulk–corner mixed terms and cut contributions. Fix the simultaneous gauge directions. Construct canonical coordinates in overlapping regular charts, verify their Poisson brackets on the retained modes and specify how to transport the state on overlaps. Quantize the pulled-back symbols for $z^1,z^2,f_S$ with the paper's Weyl prescription. For a few pairs the midpoint Weyl integral yields matrix elements by quadrature in a Hermite basis. In a nonlinear chart a guess that the shear coefficients are independent commuting position operators changes the null symplectic bracket. The paper gives an implicit reduction $\iota$. Until a usable map with boundary conditions and quantum domains is supplied, a calculation using only its linearization tests that linearization. [§3.3, constraint pullback](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS3.p5.4) · [Appendix A.2, corner coupling](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A1.SS2.p3.3) · [§4.3, ordering](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS3.p2.1)

## 3. Compute the quantum clock

Use positive angular weights $w_i$ summing to $4\pi$ and a radial mesh on which **each prefix integral** has positive quadrature weights. For example, integrate each radial cell with Gauss nodes and place the outer clock quadrature on cell endpoints with positive trapezoid weights. At an angular node $i$ form $D=\sum_iw_i e^{f_i}$, $\rho_i=\sqrt{\mathscr A}\,(D^{-1/2}e^{f_i}D^{-1/2})^{1/2}$ and $a=\sum_iw_i\rho_i$. At each radial step compute $\Xi_i(v)$ as the prefix integral of the positive Gram matrices of $\partial_vz^1$ and $e^{z^1}\partial_vz^2$, with factor $v/4$. Apply $\min(\Xi_i(v),c)$ by spectral calculus at that node. Then assemble the angular and radial sum of
$$
 a^{-1/2}\rho_i\exp[\min(\Xi_i(v),c)]\rho_i a^{-1/2}
$$
with the outer quadrature. For matrices too large to exponentiate explicitly, apply the exponential or capped exponential to a vector with a controlled error and use an iterative positive resolvent solve. Increasing the cap orders its value for each fixed matrix $\Xi_i(v)$. Matrices at different nodes need not commute. [§4.3, exact ordering and area normalization](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E22)

At each refinement check $\sum_iw_i\rho_i^2=\mathscr A I$, positivity, $T_0=(1-\epsilon)\mathscr A a^{-1}$, $T_0\le T_c\le e^cT_0$, and $T_c\ge u_0 I$. At fixed $\mathscr A$ the clock commutes with total area identically. Test strong commutation across the full representation when a model for area-changing sources is available. Resolve small eigenvalues of $a$, the condition number of the normalization and the weight of the chosen state near small $a$. Discarding small positive eigenvalues changes the clock. Increase angular and radial quadrature independently beyond the field degrees because the exponentials generate higher harmonics. [§5.2, lower bounds](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2.p2.1) · [Appendix C.2, capped form](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p3.1)

For each $(J,K,J_c,M,c)$ store the following quantities.

- The filtered overlap $F(L)=\langle\Omega,L(L+T_c)^{-1}\Omega\rangle$, low-clock probability $p(\ell)=\|\mathbf1_{[0,\ell]}(T_c)\Omega\|^2$, several low eigenvalues, and the full threshold count where feasible. Repeat the overlap for a low-mode coherent displacement and selected occupation states. The classical Wigner integral under the same cutoffs is a comparator. Report the difference between quantum and classical changes from $c_0=\tfrac12\log(L/b)$ to $c>c_0$ for $L\ge b$. Equation (5.16) specifically needs control of this tail at finite $\hbar$. [§5.1, finite-$\hbar$ estimate and its footnote](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS1.p12.1)
- A norm error for each solve. For $(I+T_c/L)\psi=\Omega$, the residual norm bounds the solution error because the inverse has norm at most one. To count eigenvalues of a small finite matrix use inertia of $T_c-\ell I$. If a certified operator error is $\eta$, the computed counts at $\ell-\eta$ and $\ell+\eta$ bracket the true regulated count. Print both values at a crowded threshold. A single eigenpair residual certifies proximity to *some* eigenvalue, with no guarantee of a complete count.
- Physical discretization error, separately from solver precision. Double quadrature, increase $M$, compare matrix elements on fixed low states using a larger intermediate oscillator buffer, and change the radial basis. In general $g(PzP)\ne Pg(z)P$. Finite matrices also fail the exact canonical commutator on their highest occupation levels. Monitor weight there. Estimate convergence orders from at least three refinements. Fit both a plateau with a decaying correction and a growing model where appropriate, then check the next refinement. These differences are empirical errors until supported by an analytic bound. Vary $K/J$, $J_c$, and the proximity of $\ell$ to a measured level. A joint diagonal sequence can hide a cutoff dependence.

## 4. An exactly solvable corner benchmark

Take shear zero, positive fixed area $\mathscr A=4\pi b^2$, and $f_S(\theta)=q\cos\theta$. As an auxiliary corner model put $q$ on $L^2(\mathbb R,dq)$ by multiplication. The two integrals needed are $\int e^{q\cos\theta}d\Omega=4\pi\sinh(q)/q$ and the same expression with $q/2$ for $\int e^{q\cos\theta/2}d\Omega$. Thus (4.26) gives exactly
$$
 \frac{T(q)}{u_0}=\sqrt{\frac{q}{2}\coth(q/2)},\qquad T(0)=u_0.
$$
For $\ell/u_0>1$ the allowed interval $[-q_*,q_*]$ has positive length. At or below the spherical reading the projection is zero, since the singleton $q=0$ has zero Lebesgue measure. Above it, splitting the allowed interval into arbitrarily many disjoint subintervals gives infinitely many orthogonal allowed wavefunctions. For the oscillator vacuum $|\Omega(q)|^2=e^{-q^2}/\sqrt\pi$, the probability is $\operatorname{erf}(q_*)$. This realizes the paper's classical area and clock formulas in a corner Schrödinger model. Whether the nonuniform area profile and its conjugate produce such a physical sector after both cones, cap matching and quantum radiation are included is precisely the issue to investigate. [§4.3, angular area normalization](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS3.p4.1) · [Appendix A.2, corner pairs](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A1.SS2.p3.3)

I tested the threshold count using the $n$-dimensional Hermite matrix for $q$, with zero diagonal and off-diagonals $\sqrt{k/2}$, $1\le k<n$, applying $T$ by spectral calculus to this matrix. The counts below were computed by bisection for $q_*$ and tridiagonal inertia at $\pm q_*$. For reproduction, start $d_0=-x$, iterate $d_k=-x-k/(2d_{k-1})$ and count negative pivots. The difference of the two inertia counts is the number of eigenvalues in the interval. Both thresholds lie between eigenvalues. A physical test suggested by this model is to compute the generator $K[g]=\int_Sg(y)\rho_S^2(y)d\Omega/(8\pi G_N)$ for one nonconstant smearing referenced to physical angular labels. Contract the full reduced symplectic form with the corresponding local frame shift, including endpoint terms. Determine whether it preserves the data defining the diamond, acts as a physical charge, and has a unitary quantum implementation commuting strongly with the clock. **If** an allowed vector has a nonatomic spectral component for this charge, take disjoint charge intervals of positive probability. Their spectral projections commute with the clock selection and give infinitely many orthogonal allowed vectors. An embedding of the benchmark would require an isometry into the physical representation intertwining its clock spectral projections, or the independent charge argument just given. A classical shear-free solution for each $q$ does not produce such a quantum subspace. The spectrum in the selected sector and the exact commutation are both substantive hypotheses. Total-area commutation alone establishes neither. ## 5. Analytic results that would decide the extrapolation

**Existence.** Seek normalized trial states converging to $\chi$ on the same fixed-area Hilbert space with $Q[\chi]<\infty$ and $|\langle\Omega,\chi\rangle|=h>0$. For $P_\ell=\mathbf1_{[0,\ell]}(T)$ extended by zero on $\mathcal M^\perp$, Cauchy–Schwarz and the spectral tail bound give
$$
 \|P_\ell\Omega\|\ge h-\sqrt{Q[\chi]/\ell}.
$$
A positive right-hand side proves existence at that threshold. For a sequence of regulators establish the continuum form first, for example by a weak lower-limit estimate on form energies and a strongly convergent recovery sequence for each vector in its domain. A uniform trial energy with states escaping weakly to zero is insufficient. A uniform large-$L$ bound on $1-F(L)$ tending to zero would establish the paper's stronger full-Gaussian-weight claim. Test a positive lower bound on the quantum clock's minimum as well. A minimum that diverges under a proved form comparison would rule out any finite-reading states in the specified representation. Growth of an unbounded vacuum mean alone leaves the low-reading probability undetermined. [Appendix C.2, filter limit](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p4.2)

**Finite rank.** Seek explicit finite-rank projections $E_N$ on the *full* physical representation for which all allowed states obey $\|(1-E_N)P_\ell\|<1$ for some finite $N$. Then $E_N$ is injective on the allowed subspace and $\operatorname{rank}P_\ell\le\operatorname{rank}E_N$. A sufficient uniform estimate is $\|(1-E_N)\psi\|^2\le\delta_N(\|\psi\|^2+Q[\psi]/b)$, $\delta_N\to0$. The D.3 radiative mode costs suggest candidates for $E_N$. The estimate must also control the edge excitations, any continuous area measure and the pure extension reached by general sources. Refining a quadrature grid of area values cannot establish the microscopic trace measure. For the reverse conclusion construct arbitrarily large continuum trial spaces for which the **maximum** form energy on the unit sphere is $\le\ell$, or construct the commuting-charge spectral projections above. Bounded energy of separate, nonorthogonal candidate vectors leaves the dimension open. [§4.2, extension of the fixed-area representation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p6.3) · [Appendix D.3, fixed-data scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS3.p4.1)

**Independent trace test.** Within the physical clock subspace define $W=e^{-\hat s}f_b(\hat s)$, $m=\min_{[0,s_*]} e^{-s}f_b(s)>0$. For a finite-rank projection $P$ in that subspace compute $Z_P=\operatorname{Tr}(PWP)$ and purity $R_P=\operatorname{Tr}[(PWP)^2]/Z_P^2$. Since $mP\le PWP\le P$, one has $m\operatorname{rank}P\le Z_P\le1/R_P$. A proved positive lower bound on $R_P$ along an exhaustive sequence of microscopic subspaces gives finite rank. A numerical comparison should use ordinary state counts and the same cap with angular and radial insertions fixed. Compare a natural mode exhaustion with cycles biased toward one narrow area band as in §5.2. Record both the signed **unnormalized** closed boost response and $Z_P$ at each step. At fixed area alone the boost is a scalar, so this response needs an actual representation of area-changing sources. A source-normalized correlator or finite saddle integral supplies no bound on $Z_P$ without a sewing theorem identifying its ordinary trace. [§5.2, preparation and cut](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.F26) · [Appendix D.3, replica and saddle measures](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS3.p7.1)

A practical first milestone is the single-harmonic cost matrix, a converged resolvent for one or two reduced pairs, and the independent corner benchmark above. Constraint failure, a violated fixed-cutoff identity, or a replicated nonzero discrepancy larger than a **claimed quantitative uniform error** can falsify that construction or that estimate. Stable small matrices alone cannot select a continuum representation, certify the domain of the unbounded exponential, or rule out new orthogonal states as the regulator is removed. Those questions have explicit mathematical decision criteria above.
