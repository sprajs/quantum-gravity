# Section 6.1 and the introduction to Section 6 — The classical solution

**Physical question.** How does classical vacuum general relativity re-emerge from coherent quantum states of a causal diamond, and how much of that derivation is an independent dynamical prediction versus a consistency check that the quantization preserves its classical input?

**Outcome.** At fixed $G_N$, $\Lambda$, mode regulator, smearing, and finite propagation time, equations (6.1)–(6.5) consistently recover the local Hamiltonian flow of vacuum general relativity and $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$ on the invertible, oriented gravitational branch, along with classical composition $E_{2\circ 1}=E_2\circ E_1$ across compatible regular saddles. This is a consistency check on the input dynamics rather than an independent derivation of Einstein's equations: equation (3.19) already defines the diamond phase space on vacuum solutions $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$, and interior reduced variations span directions tangent to the solved constraint surface rather than rederiving the eliminated simplicity and torsion multipliers or ruling out other branches. Extending these fixed-regulator, controlled-symbol estimates to an exact continuum semiclassical limit still requires cutoff-uniform control of the nonlinear gravitational Hamiltonian, the coherent states, and their propagation.

## Hamiltonian evolution and the source phase, (6.1)–(6.2)

Work in a regular reduced Darboux chart with $J^{q^k p_l}=\delta^k_l$, holding $G_N$, $\Lambda$, smearings, and propagation time fixed. Writing $A P B=\partial_I A\,J^{IJ}\partial_J B$, antisymmetrizing the Weyl star product (C.9) gives

$$
\frac{i}{\hbar}[\mathcal Q(H_c),\mathcal Q(O)]
=\mathcal Q\!\left(O P H_c-\frac{\hbar^2}{24}O P^3 H_c+\cdots\right).
$$

This verifies both the sign and the symbol order in (6.1a). For linear coordinates $O=q^k,p_k$, third and higher derivatives vanish, leaving the principal flow $\dot q^k=\partial_{p_k}H_c$ and $\dot p_k=-\partial_{q^k}H_c$ as in (6.1b). Explicitly time-dependent observables carry an extra $\partial_t O$ term, and moving the cap requires the full reduced Hamiltonian (3.53b), including $-\Theta_t(\partial_t\iota_t)+\partial_t\chi_t$. Note also that this expansion assumes the classical $H_c$ is the exact Weyl symbol of the quantum generator; any subprincipal term $\hbar H_1$ would add $\hbar\{O,H_1\}$. [Moyal's phase-space formulation](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100000487)

For the Gaussian state (4.7), set $(\Delta q_j)^2=\hbar c_j$ with fixed $0<c_j<\infty$, so that $(\Delta p_j)^2=\hbar/(4c_j)$ and the symmetrized covariance $\Sigma$ of the retained modes is $O(\hbar)$. Around a fixed center $z_i$, averaging a smooth back-transported observable $a(z)=O(E_{t,i}z)$ against the Gaussian Wigner function yields

$$
\langle \mathcal Q(a)\rangle
=a(z_i)+\tfrac12\Sigma^{IJ}\partial_I\partial_J a(z_i)+O(\hbar^2)
$$

for fixed modes and bounded derivatives. Thus a nonlinear observable's expectation value can shift away from its classical value at order $\hbar$ even though the Weyl commutator agrees with the Poisson bracket through $O(\hbar)$. Moreover, localization is needed over the entire observation interval, and estimates for each fixed smearing do not supply uniform bounds on field derivatives or sums over infinitely many modes.

For an admissible time-local deformation $f=\int f_t\,dt$, passing to the interaction picture of $H_c$ gives each $\widehat f_{t,I}$ the leading symbol $f_t(E_{t,i}z)$. In the time-ordered exponential for $R_V(\hbar f)$, ordinary multiplication produces the leading phase $\exp(i\int f_t(E_{t,i}z)\,dt)$, while star-product corrections between insertions and Gaussian averaging enter at $O(\hbar)$, yielding (6.2). As an exact single-pair check, set $H_c=0$ and insert $a q$ before $b p$; the coherent-state expectation is

$$
\left\langle e^{ib\widehat p}e^{ia\widehat q}\right\rangle
=e^{i(aq_i+bp_i)}
  e^{\,i\hbar ab/2}\,
  e^{-\frac12(a,b)\Sigma(a,b)^T},
$$

which cleanly separates the ordering phase from the state-width suppression. Equation (6.2) scales sources as $\hbar f$ at fixed $f$. Because Appendix C.2 constructs the time-ordered exponential for a stated class of bounded generators and treats general products formally at the symbol level, these estimates apply to controlled symbols or order by order at a fixed regulator. The paper does not prove a cutoff-uniform propagation bound for the nonlinear gravitational Hamiltonian.

## Interior stationarity and the Einstein equation, (6.3)–(6.4)

Setting $F=0$ in the shifted-action Ward identity of Appendix C.1 gives $R_V(\Delta_{rs}I_{\mathscr M})=1$ for an admissible interior translation supported away from the boundaries. In a differentiable representation, the first derivative at $r=0$ yields $(i/\hbar)\langle\delta_s I_{\mathscr M}\rangle=0$, whose leading symbol on a single localized branch is the classical variation of the action. Alternatively, setting $r=\hbar\lambda$ (with $\lambda$ in inverse-action units) gives $\Delta_{rs}I_{\mathscr M}=\hbar\lambda\delta_s I_{\mathscr M}+O(\hbar^2)$ by smoothness; applying the local source estimate then yields $\exp(i\lambda\delta_s I_{\mathscr M}[\phi])=1$ in the classical limit for all $\lambda$, forcing $\delta_s I_{\mathscr M}[\phi]=0$ without having to differentiate a pointwise asymptotic remainder in (6.2).

Directly varying the reduced action (3.53a) gives

$$
\delta I_{\mathscr M}
=[p_k\,\delta q^k+\delta\chi_t]_i^f+
\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\!\!dt\,
\big[(\dot q^k-H_{c,p_k})\delta p_k
       -(\dot p_k+H_{c,q^k})\delta q^k\big].
$$

For interior variations as in (6.3), the endpoint bracket vanishes. Notice the contrast between a fixed-endpoint transition kernel—which pins both initial and final configurations and can receive contributions from multiple classical branches—and an incoming-state expectation value, which pairs forward evolution with its adjoint. In a coherent packet, initial localization and phase select both position and momentum, so on that single branch the common outgoing data cancel the two final boundary variations. Outside the localized regime, (6.2) selects no single classical history, and any variation reaching an external cap, null boundary, or joint also varies the boundary state, phase, or charge, so the interior Ward identity does not apply there.

Because the variation above acts only on independent reduced coordinates, recovering the covariant Einstein equation relies on the classical reconstruction of §§3.1–3.5 and Appendices A–B. On the regular gravitational branch, pick an invertible oriented tetrad with $B^{IJ}=\tfrac12\epsilon^{IJ}{}_{KL}e^K\wedge e^L$. Before reduction, varying the trace-free multiplier imposes simplicity, varying $B$ gives $F_{IJ}=\Phi_{IJKL}B^{KL}$, and varying $\omega$ gives $D_\omega B=0$, which forces zero torsion ($\omega=\omega(e)$) on this branch. With $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$ and signature $(-+++)$,

$$
B^{IJ}\wedge B^{KL}=\epsilon^{IJKL}\,\mathrm{vol}_e,\qquad
-\tfrac12\Phi_{IJKL}B^{IJ}\wedge B^{KL}=-2\Lambda\,\mathrm{vol}_e ,
$$

as checked by $B^{01}=-e^2\wedge e^3$ and $B^{23}=e^0\wedge e^1$. With the curvature term becoming $R\,\mathrm{vol}_e$ via (3.7), varying $(16\pi G_N)^{-1}\int(R-2\Lambda)\mathrm{vol}_e$ with respect to $g^{ab}$ gives $G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=0$; tracing gives $R=4\Lambda$ and hence $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$, verifying (6.4).

This recovery presupposes the constraints, their propagation, and the classical reconstruction map from a reduced history. An interior shift $\delta\phi$ moves strictly tangent to the solved constraint surface and provides no normal variation that could rederive the eliminated multiplier equations; indeed, (3.19) already restricts $\mathcal P(\mathscr D)$ to vacuum solutions. The classical check therefore holds on the selected sector without constraining degenerate tetrads, alternative simplicity branches, or their weights in another quantization.

## Outgoing data, boundary matching and the scope of reconstruction, (6.5)

When $f_+$ is supported strictly after $V$, causal factorization gives $S_0(V+\hbar f_+)=S_0(\hbar f_+)S_0(V)$, so multiplying on the left by $S_0(V)^{-1}$ yields $U^\dagger S_0(\hbar f_+)U$ identically. Its principal symbol on incoming data is $e^{if_+(E_{\mathscr M}z_i)}$, giving (6.5) under the same control assumptions as (6.2). Note that (6.5) evaluates a final observable in the evolved incoming state rather than fixing a second independent final configuration.

For a regular stationary branch joining two consecutive kernels with Hamilton principal functions $S_1(q_m,q_i)$ and $S_2(q_f,q_m)$, integrating over the intermediate configuration $q_m$ gives the saddle condition

$$
0=\partial_{q_m}(S_1+S_2)=p_m^{(1)}-p_m^{(2)}.
$$

Since $q_m$ is already identified in the integral, the conjugate momenta match as well, and endpoint phases $\chi_m$ cancel in a shared framing. Given unique smooth development for compatible data, this proves $E_{2\circ 1}=E_2\circ E_1$ on their common domain.

Geometrically, gluing identifies the cap embeddings and internal Lorentz frames, matching induced metric and extrinsic curvature with a shared future normal; on a null corner, the two null second fundamental forms and normal connection transform with the relative boost as in §3.6, and measuring the metric at a dressed event also requires matching the transported frame and geodesic anchor on both sides. Compatible cap data guarantee agreement inside their domain of dependence. On overlaps extending farther, §3.2 imposes direct matching of tetrads and connections together with the Lorentz cocycle on triple overlaps. Intrinsic data on a truncated null sheet alone do not determine compatible cap or transverse characteristic data; furthermore, an area coordinate can hit a turning point before a caustic, and singular spacetimes need not admit smooth continuation. Continuation across a cover of regular diamonds is therefore conditional on that cover and its matched data, since established characteristic existence theorems are local. [Rendall, intersecting null sheets](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1990.0009) · [Chruściel, smooth light-cone data](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1971)

The introduction to Section 6 also previews the order-$\hbar$ mean metric shift. In (6.9), the mean shift involves $\tfrac12\Sigma^{IJ}\partial_I\partial_J g$ while the fluctuation covariance involves $(\partial_I g)\Sigma^{IJ}(\partial_J g)$, so the quadratic Einstein source can be assembled from that covariance at fixed smearing.
