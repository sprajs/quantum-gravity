# Sections 3.5 and 3.6 — Reconstructing the metric and matching gravitational data

**Physical question.** How is the four-dimensional spacetime metric rebuilt from null boundary data, how is an interior event located without coordinate gauge ambiguity, and what data must agree across an interface so that two regions describe the same geometry?

**Outcome.** The null transport equations (3.57), the linearized geodesic-dressing equation (3.58), the gauge invariance of relational shear (3.59), and the corner boost and charge-reduction identities (3.61)–(3.66) all hold in the stated conventions. One concrete qualification affects the claim right after (3.62): matching the listed metric, null extrinsic curvatures, and normal connection on the two-dimensional corner $S$ identifies the ambient connection *along directions tangent to $S$*, but does not determine the gravitational radiation away from $S$. Below, an explicit vacuum plane wave agrees with Minkowski space on every listed corner datum—and has every Christoffel symbol vanishing at $S$—yet has nonzero Riemann curvature at $S$ and different shear for arbitrarily small $u>0$. The paper's stronger prescription (3.30), which matches fields on an open overlap, avoids this counterexample. As throughout Section 3, these classical calculations leave the quantum definition of a shear operator and of a sewing amplitude to later sections.

## Null transport, equations (3.56)–(3.57)

Write $K=K^{(\ell)}$, $N=K^{(n)}$, $\theta=\operatorname{tr}K$, $\nu=\operatorname{tr}N$, and $\omega_A=\varpi_A$, and let $D$ be the Levi-Civita derivative of the cut metric $q_{AB}$. In a screen basis $E_A$ Lie-dragged along $\ell$ ($[\ell,E_A]=0$) with $\ell\cdot n=-1$ and $\nabla_\ell\ell=0$, differentiating the inner products reproduces (3.56) and yields

$$
\nabla_A\ell=K_A{}^B E_B+\omega_A\ell,\qquad
\nabla_A n=N_A{}^B E_B-\omega_A n,\qquad
\nabla_\ell n=\omega^B E_B,\qquad
\mathcal L_\ell q_{AB}=2K_{AB}.
$$

In the paper's curvature convention (3.23), contracting the curvature commutator on $\ell$ gives

$$
R_{\ell A}=(\mathcal L_\ell+\theta)\omega_A+D^B K_{AB}-D_A\theta.
$$

Imposing the vacuum equation $R_{\ell A}=0$ and splitting $K=\sigma+\theta q/2$ gives (3.57a,c). Because $R=q^{ab}R_{ab}-2R_{\ell n}$, we have $(G+\Lambda g)_{\ell n}=q^{ab}R_{ab}/2-\Lambda$, verifying (3.57b) including the sign of $\Lambda$.

For the transverse components, differentiate $N_{AB}=g(E_B,\nabla_A n)$. The commutator produces $g(E_B,R(\ell,E_A)n)$; symmetrizing in $A,B$ and using the Gauss equation to eliminate the tangential Riemann contraction gives

$$
\mathcal L_\ell N_{AB}=
D_{(A}\omega_{B)}+\omega_A\omega_B+2K_{(A}{}^C N_{B)C}
+\tfrac12\big(R^{(4)}_{AB}-R[q]_{AB}-\nu K_{AB}-\theta N_{AB}\big).
$$

Because the trace of the left-hand side is $\ell(\nu)+2K^{AB}N_{AB}$, the vacuum condition $R^{(4)}_{AB}=\Lambda q_{AB}$ yields the cross-focusing equation

$$
(\mathcal L_\ell+\theta)\nu=-\tfrac12R[q]+D_A\omega^A+\omega_A\omega^A+\Lambda .
$$

To verify (3.57e), split $N=\tau+\nu q/2$. In two dimensions the symmetrized product of two trace-free symmetric tensors is pure trace, and $R[q]_{AB}=R[q]q_{AB}/2$. Taking the trace-free part and differentiating $q$ inside $\nu q/2$ gives

$$
(\mathcal L_\ell\tau)^{\rm TF}=
\tfrac12\theta\tau-\tfrac12\nu\sigma+
(D_{(A}\omega_{B)}+\omega_A\omega_B)^{\rm TF}.
$$

As a quick check on round Minkowski spheres of radius $r$ with $\ell=\partial_t+\partial_r$ and $n=(\partial_t-\partial_r)/2$, one has $\theta=2/r$, $\nu=-1/r$, and $\omega=\sigma=\tau=0$, making both sides of cross-focusing equal to $-1/r^2$. More generally, since $\mathcal L_\ell dA=\theta dA$, equations (3.57c,d) transport $dA\,\omega_A$ and $dA\,\nu$ in turn **once the intrinsic metric history $q_{AB}(u,y)$** and corner values are supplied, and (3.57e) then transports the transverse shear $\tau$. The intrinsic conformal metric on the null sheet provides two freely specifiable functions. [Hopfmüller and Freidel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06135) · [Luk, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0898#page=7)

## Local reconstruction and geodesic dressing, equations (3.58)–(3.59)

To test relational localization, let $X_\lambda(s)$ be a smooth family of affinely parametrized geodesics of $g_\lambda$, and write $t=\partial_s X$, $\chi=\partial_\lambda X$, and $h=\partial_\lambda g_\lambda|_0$. Differentiating $\nabla^{(\lambda)}_t t=0$ at fixed $s$ relates the geodesic displacement to the metric perturbation:

$$
\nabla_t\nabla_t\chi^a+R^a{}_{bcd}\,t^b\chi^c t^d
=-\delta\Gamma^a{}_{bc}t^b t^c,\qquad
\delta\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=
\tfrac12 g^{ad}(\nabla_b h_{cd}+\nabla_c h_{bd}-\nabla_d h_{bc}).
$$

This establishes (3.58) once the boundary embedding and frame fix the initial values of $\chi$ and $\nabla_t\chi$. For a pure gauge variation $g_\lambda=\phi_\lambda^* g$, the dressed embedding transforms as $X_\lambda=\phi_\lambda^{-1}\circ X$, so $X_\lambda^* g_\lambda=X^* g$ identically. By uniqueness of parallel transport, the parallel-transported frame moves with the map, and transporting the entire congruence with a fixed test tensor in relational coordinates leaves $X^*(du\,dA\,f^{AB}\sigma_{AB})$ unchanged—yielding $\delta_\xi\sigma_f=0$ in (3.59). Physical radiative variations generally change $\sigma_f$. Note two structural requirements: measuring shear requires neighboring rays, and defining a geodesic chart for a smeared observable requires a regular tube containing its support. Promoting this classical functional to a quantum operator requires a representation and control of its smearing.

Standard existence theorems also restrict which boundary surfaces can carry independent data. Two compatible sets of characteristic data on transversely intersecting *future* null sheets determine a local development in their common future. At the equator $S$ of the paper's causal diamond, however, one boundary segment runs to the past and the other to the future; the standard double-null theorem does not permit prescribing both exterior histories freely as initial data for the interior. Section 3.2 instead places data on the past cone together with a compatible spacelike cap, restricting reconstruction to their domain of dependence so that the outgoing cone carries induced data. A smooth-vertex light cone has its own regularity and local existence theorem. [Rendall](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1990.0009) · [Luk, introduction and §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0898) · [Chruściel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1971)

## Open overlap and corner, equations (3.60)–(3.62)

When base points are identified across an open overlap, a local Lorentz transformation $e_i=L e_j$ preserves $g=\eta_{\mu\nu}e^\mu e^\nu$, and the torsion-free spin connections are related by

$$
dL+\omega_i L-L\omega_j=0,\qquad
\omega_i=L\omega_j L^{-1}-dL\,L^{-1}.
$$

This verifies (3.60) and equivalence of parallel transport throughout the overlap. Matching along a single geodesic tests only the contraction with $t^a$; agreement along all directions requires the full overlap identification (or sufficient initial data), with the triple-overlap cocycle ensuring consistent descent. The Schwarzschild photon-sphere illustration checks directly in $G=c=1$ units: at $r=3M$, a circular null geodesic orbits a great circle with angular speed $1/(3\sqrt3 M)$ in Schwarzschild time, so every orbital plane through the launch point meets at the antipode after $\Delta t=3\sqrt3\pi M$. A second chart can continue describing that regular event once the first congruence ceases to be injective.

On a two-dimensional cut $S$, substituting $\ell'=e^\gamma\ell$ and $n'=e^{-\gamma}n$ into (3.56) projects out normal-derivative pieces in $K$, giving $K'=e^\gamma K$, $N'=e^{-\gamma}N$, and $\omega'_A=-n'\cdot\nabla_A\ell'=\omega_A+D_A\gamma$. Combined with $q_+=Y^* q_-$, these verify (3.61)–(3.62) in matched normal bundles and identify the ambient connection **along tangent directions to the cut** through the Gauss–Weingarten formulas.

**Exact vacuum counterexample to propagation from the corner alone.** Compare flat Minkowski space with the vacuum plane wave

$$
ds^2=-2\,du\,dv+a(u)^2dx^2+b(u)^2dy^2,\qquad
a=\cos(ku),\qquad b=\cosh(ku),\qquad k\ne0 .
$$

Work on a patch with $a>0$, taking $S=\{u=v=0\}$, $\ell=\partial_u$, and $n=\partial_v$. The only potentially nonzero Ricci component is $R_{uu}=-(a''/a+b''/b)=0$, so the metric is exact vacuum. At $S$, $a=b=1$ and $a'=b'=0$, giving $q=dx^2+dy^2$, $K=N=0$, and $\omega=0$—identical to Minkowski space. Yet at $S$ itself, $R_{uxux}=-aa''=k^2$ and $R_{uyuy}=-bb''=-k^2$, and the null-sheet metric and shear differ for every arbitrarily small $u>0$. Computing all Christoffel symbols and contracting the full Riemann tensor independently in SymPy confirmed that every Christoffel symbol vanishes on $S$, the Ricci tensor vanishes identically on the patch, and the curvature components equal $\pm k^2$. The same free-data construction works on a spherical cut with nonzero initial expansions by perturbing the unimodular conformal metric at order $u^2$ inside a small angular patch while fixing its value, first derivative, and corner data. [Luk, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0898#page=7)

Equation (3.57) *does* propagate identical transverse data once the two descriptions already agree on the free conformal profiles along the sheet, the initial area and expansion, and the remaining corner data in a common gauge. Likewise, smooth matched Cauchy data on a spacelike cap force equality throughout the cap's domain of dependence, and for regions reaching farther, §3.2 imposes full overlap matching in (3.30). Those stronger prescriptions suffice whenever two descriptions refer to the same spacetime solution.

## Charges and symplectic reduction, equations (3.63)–(3.66)

Writing $\bar\Gamma=(\Gamma_R+\Gamma_L)/2$ and $\gamma=\Gamma_R-\Gamma_L$, equation (3.63) splits the unintegrated boost potential identically as

$$
\mathscr K_+\delta\Gamma_R-\mathscr K_-\delta\Gamma_L
=\mathscr K\,\delta\gamma+\Delta\mathscr K\,\delta\bar\Gamma .
$$

Matching pulled-back area two-forms sets $\Delta\mathscr K=0$, proving (3.64) in common angular coordinates with a shared reference measure. On the matching surface $\delta\Delta\mathscr K=0$ along tangent variations, making a simultaneous boost a degenerate direction of the pulled-back boost symplectic form and leaving $\delta\mathscr K\wedge\delta\gamma$ as the local corner term (alongside its couplings to radiative and angular data in the full reduced form).

For $s_-=Y\circ g_S$, the chain rule gives $\delta s_-=\delta Y\circ g_S+dY\,\delta g_S$; multiplying by $ds_-^{-1}$ yields (3.65). Pulling the momentum densities back to the same cut collects $(g_S^{-1})_*\delta g_S$ with coefficient $J_++J_-$, while the relative piece pairs $J_-$ with variations of $Y$, reproducing (3.66) up to the exact variation $\delta B_S$ that drops out of the symplectic form. Setting the total outward moment map to zero and quotienting by common transformations performs the classical symplectic reduction. Opposite orientations cancel internal action and charge terms whenever fields, displacements, frame conventions, and variations match across the entire shared interface. This establishes classical additivity under those hypotheses; quantum sewing and its normalization require further input. [Donnelly and Freidel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04744)
