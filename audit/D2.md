# Appendix D.2 — Corner boosts and two-sided graviton correlators

**Physical question.** Can a one-sided corner boost acting on two separated graviton shear operators—together with the sharp short-distance singularity of the flat-null vacuum—force the causal diamond's Hilbert space to be finite-dimensional?

**Outcome.** All of the linearized flat-null calculations in (D.13)–(D.36) reproduce cleanly: the negative separated-point shear correlator, its positive shift under a relative boost, the finite-rank area-band bounds, and the free-field interval inequality with every constant and scaling exponent check out. Two decisive inputs are still missing before finite dimensionality follows. First, under the infinite-dimensional alternative, the unnormalized finite-rank vector norms diverge at least like $c_0\operatorname{rank}P$, so open-vector sewing cannot justify passing to an uncut closed trace. Second, obtaining the quantitative bound (D.36) requires proving that the exact uncut gravitational preparation has uniformly bounded local normal-ordered shear of size $O(c/b^2)$—a state-specific bound that smoothness of the subtracted two-point function alone does not provide. The checks below cover (D.13)–(D.36), pp. 70–73, and the corresponding steps in §5.2. [Manuscript source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2) · [Paper, p. 70](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=70)

## Real boosts, imaginary boosts, and state matching

Put $s=A/(4G_N\hbar)$ and write a real corner boost as $U(\eta)=\exp(i\eta s/(2\pi))$. In (D.13), the intermediate phase is $\exp[i\alpha(s_L-s_R)/(2\pi)]$; the Fourier identity $\int e^{i\alpha x}\,d\alpha=2\pi\delta(x)$ supplies $4\pi^2\delta(s_L-s_R)$, verifying both the printed measure and the surviving relative phase. This is a distributional kernel for Lebesgue-normalized continuous spectral labels. In a discrete area sector, the unnormalized integral over equal labels diverges by the infinite boost volume, so an actual matching projector requires a normalized group average (such as $(2T)^{-1}\int_{-T}^T d\alpha\,e^{i\alpha(s_L-s_R)/(2\pi)}$, which converges pointwise to the Kronecker delta on point eigenvalues rather than the Dirac distribution) or an equivalent gauge quotient. Equation (D.13) by itself carries no information about area discreteness or multiplicity. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p2.1)

Continuing to the imaginary half-turn used in (5.23) gives $U(i\pi)=e^{-s/2}=e^{-A/(8G_N\hbar)}$, and reflection combined with unitary cap evolution yields $C_{\mathscr D}^\dagger C_{\mathscr D}=P_{\mathscr D}e^{-s}P_{\mathscr D}$. The decaying sign corresponds to continuation into the upper rapidity half-plane, which spectral calculus defines whenever the boost generator is positive and self-adjoint. Identifying that operator with the physical half-cap—including its boundary conditions and radiative modes—is a separate physical prescription. In the sharp-point approximation below, the real-rapidity correlator hits a pole at $\eta=i\pi$ where the continued right insertion collides with the left one, so substituting imaginary rapidity into the linearized correlator cannot independently justify the half-cap prescription. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2.p3.1)

## Gaussian source and separated correlations

Let $J_I=\sum_a j_a\delta_I\sigma_{f_a}$. Completing the square in $i\phi K_0\phi/(2\hbar)+iJ\phi$ shifts $\phi\mapsto\phi+\hbar K_0^{-1}J$ and leaves the exponent $-i\hbar J K_0^{-1}J/2$. Under a common convergent regulator, matched boundary conditions, and a centered Gaussian on both sides of the ratio, the determinant cancels. Differentiating the logarithm twice at zero source yields minus the time-ordered connected covariance, whose negative real part is the symmetrized covariance of Hermitian insertions; since $\sigma^{(1)}_{AB}=\partial_u h_{AB}/2$, the symmetrized derivative in (D.14) carries the printed $1/8$. This verifies (D.14)–(D.18) inside the regulated quadratic integral, while an unnormalized divergent exact integral cannot be canceled by this formal ratio. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p2.2)

For a single real transverse profile with vacuum spectral coefficient $c>0$ and affine separation $x=u-u'$, the positive-frequency two-point function with damping $\tau>0$ is

$$
W_\tau(x)=c\int_0^\infty \omega e^{-\tau\omega-i\omega x}\,d\omega
=\frac{c}{(\tau+ix)^2}
=-\frac{c}{(x-i\tau)^2}.
$$

For $x\ne 0$, $\operatorname{Re} W_\tau=c(\tau^2-x^2)/(\tau^2+x^2)^2\to -c/x^2$. As a distribution, its real part is the Hadamard finite part $-c\,\mathrm{Pf}(1/x^2)$ and its commutator is $W(x)-W(-x)=2\pi i c\delta'(x)$. This verifies the negative sign in (D.19) and ensures that disjoint affine smearings commute in the linearized algebra. For a smooth real test function $h$, the diagonal variance is strictly positive, $c\int_0^\infty k|\widetilde h(k)|^2\,dk$, while the negative separated-point kernel cannot be integrated across $x=0$ as an ordinary function. The radiative symplectic form fixes the commutator, whereas the positive-frequency condition selects the vacuum covariance. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p2.7) · [Ciambelli, Freidel and Leigh, *Quantum Null Geometry and Gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11132)

Set $t=e^\eta$. Because the right shear has boost weight $1$, boosting it gives $G_t(-d,d)=-ct/[d^2(1+t)^2]$, compared with the unboosted $G_1(-d,d)=-c/(4d^2)$. Their ratio and difference are

$$
\frac{G_t}{G_1}=\frac{4t}{(1+t)^2}=\operatorname{sech}^2(\eta/2),\qquad
G_t-G_1=\frac{c}{4d^2}\tanh^2(\eta/2).
$$

Splitting a putatively boost-invariant full correlator into its vacuum singularity plus a regular part reproduces the positive sign on the right of (D.21), because finite coincidence limits make the $d^2$-weighted regular difference vanish as $d\to 0$. At fixed Planck length, however, the effective-field hierarchy (5.26) places a lower bound on the smearing widths and separations available to the semiclassical argument. Changing variables in the smeared right insertion gives $p_{R,\eta}(u)=p_R(u/t)$, keeping $G_{RR}$ invariant in the scale-invariant flat vacuum. For even profiles of nonzero integral, the leading finite-width correction to $G_t/G_1$ is quadratic in $\Delta u/d$. As a numerical check, taking the normalized compact profile proportional to $e^{-1/(1-v^2)}$ on $|v|<1$ with $t=2$, $d=1$, and $320$ midpoint samples per integral yields ratios $0.88912370$, $0.88894749$, and $0.88890353$ at widths $0.10$, $0.05$, and $0.025$—approaching $8/9$ with the predicted quadratic convergence. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E28.4)

For commuting bounded insertions $X=j_L\sigma_L$ and $Y=j_R\sigma_R$, the identity $2\sin X\sin Y=\cos(X-Y)-\cos(X+Y)$ combined with the centered Gaussian expectation $\langle\cos(X\pm Y)\rangle=e^{-[\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y)\pm 2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)]/2}$ reproduces the positive hyperbolic sine in (D.20). For equally oriented nonnegative profiles and $j_Lj_R>0$, a nonzero boost makes the negative cross-covariance less negative, so the shift in $\langle\sin X\sin Y\rangle$ is strictly positive in the narrow-profile limit. Exact commutativity of the nonlinear, gravitationally dressed insertions requires a separate argument. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p2.8)

## Finite projections and the trace limit

Write $W=C_b^\dagger C_b$, $Z_P=\operatorname{Tr}(PW)$, and $\rho_P=PWP/Z_P$, with conjugate-side marginal $\rho_P^T$ in the chosen basis of (D.22). Bounding $|e^{ix}-1|\le |x|$ mode by mode and comparing the two unit-vector expectations gives

$$
|\langle\mathcal O\rangle_{\eta,P}-\langle\mathcal O\rangle_{0,P}|
\le \frac{|\eta|}{\pi}\|\mathcal O\|
      \sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\rho_P(\hat s-s_0)^2},
$$

verifying (D.22)–(D.23) and (5.32). Setting $c_0=e^{-s_*}m_{b,\mathscr D}>0$, equation (5.30c) implies $c_0 I\le W\le I$, so $Z_P\ge c_0\operatorname{rank}P$. On a narrow spectral band $I$ of width $\Delta s$ around $s_0$, the squared displacement is at most $(\Delta s)^2$ inside $I$ and at most $s_*^2$ outside; since $PWP\le P$, adding $N$ vectors from $I$ to $k$ arbitrary outside basis vectors yields (D.24). In an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, any finite spectral partition contains a band of full Hilbert dimension, so allocating at most one outside vector per $r$-cycle gives (D.25) with $k/(k+N)\le 1/r$ (including over a directed net of finite sets). All of these area-band bounds hold strictly for finite-rank traces. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p4.1)

The obstruction appears when taking the limit $P\to I$ in the unnormalized numerator of (D.22). Its underlying state vector has squared norm

$$
\left\|\sum_{i\in P}C_b|i\rangle\otimes|i^*\rangle\right\|^2
  =Z_P\ge c_0\operatorname{rank}P.
$$

Under the infinite-dimensional hypothesis being tested for contradiction, these vector norms diverge along every exhaustion in the proof—vectorizing $C_b$ on the doubled cut would already require $C_b$ to be Hilbert–Schmidt. While strong convergence $P\to I$ controls matrix elements between *fixed* Hilbert vectors (the open-vector sewing proved in (4.30)–(4.31)), here both external vectors in the unnormalized expression vary with $P$. The same gap persists for the signed observable $X$ in (5.38). Tightening the band and $r$ so that $|\langle\Delta_\eta\mathcal O\rangle_P|\le\delta_\eta/4$ (with $\delta_\eta>0$) gives $Z_P[X]\le -(\delta_\eta/4)c_0\operatorname{rank}P$. That produces a genuine contradiction *if* an independently defined, finite, positive uncut amplitude $Z[X]$ exists and a closed-sewing theorem guarantees $Z_P[X]\to Z[X]$ along these finite-rank exhaustions. Neither the Gaussian ratio nor open-vector sewing establishes those premises—indeed, independent finiteness and tracial sewing of the *identity* operator alone would already imply $c_0\dim\mathcal H_{\mathscr D}\le Z[I]<\infty$. [Open-vector sewing](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS4.p4.1) · [Closed-trace step](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2.p13.2)

## Interval inequality

To control how far a general state can deviate from the flat-null vacuum across the measurement interval, Appendix D.2 derives a chiral quantum energy inequality. Using $\widetilde g(k)=\int g(u)e^{-iku}\,du$, $A_k=\sigma_\perp[g e^{iku}]$, and $c=c_f$, the normal-ordered two-point function is symmetric in the free current algebra; integrating $e^{ik(u'-u)}$ over $k>0$ yields $\pi\delta(u-u')$ from its real part while the antisymmetric part drops out, leaving the vacuum remainder

$$
\frac c\pi\int_0^\infty dk\int_0^\infty d\xi\,\xi|\widetilde g(k+\xi)|^2
=\frac{c}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty dq\,q^2|\widetilde g(q)|^2
=\frac c2\|g'\|_2^2.
$$

Hence $E_g=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty\langle A_k^\dagger A_k\rangle\,dk\ge 0$, reproducing (D.26)–(D.29) for states with finite Wick-square expectations and giving the negative lower bound $\int g^2\langle:\!\sigma_\perp^2\!:\rangle\,du\ge -c\|g'\|_2^2/2$. Note that $\sigma_\perp^2$ involves two separate transverse smearings, so connecting this single smeared channel to a local Raychaudhuri energy density requires its own transverse contraction and subtraction. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p7.1)

For each of the four real profiles $h=j_Lp_L\pm j_Rp_{R,\alpha}$ supported where $g=1$, we have $gh=h$ and hence $a_h+a_h^\dagger=\sigma_\perp[h]$. The central commutator of $a_h=\sigma_\perp[gh_+]$ depends on $\int \operatorname{Im}(\overline{gh_+}(gh_+)')\,du$; wherever $g\ne 1$, $h=0$ and $h_+$ is purely imaginary, so the window $g$ does not alter this integral. Evaluating it with the uncut positive frequencies gives $[a_h,a_h^\dagger]=v_h I$ with $v_h=c\int_0^\infty k|\widetilde h(k)|^2\,dk$, confirming (D.30)–(D.33). The localized oscillator vacuum and the global vacuum share the same characteristic function $e^{-v_h/2}$ for the quadrature $a_h+a_h^\dagger$, though they can differ as states on the localized mode because $gh_+$ need not remain purely positive-frequency. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p8.1)

In any regular one-mode representation, $1-p_0\le\langle a_h^\dagger a_h\rangle/v_h$ and the trace distance to the oscillator vacuum is at most $\sqrt{1-p_0}$ (in the half-trace-norm convention), giving the first inequality in (D.34c) via the cosine norm bound. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to $a_h=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^\infty\widetilde h(k)A_k\,dk$ with $\int_0^\infty|\widetilde h(k)|^2\,dk=\pi\|h\|_2^2$ gives $4\langle a_h^\dagger a_h\rangle\le \|h\|_2^2 E_g$, and summing the four bounds and squaring reproduces the factor of $4$ in (D.35). Under the rescaling $h(u)=c^{-1/2}H(u/d)$, $v_h$ is dimensionless and $d$-independent, $\|h\|_2^2\propto d/c$, and $\|g'\|_2^2=B^{-1}\|g_0'\|_2^2$, confirming every scaling exponent and constant. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p9.1)

Finally, passing from (D.35) to (D.36) requires the uniform quantitative bound $\langle:\!\sigma_\perp^2\!:\rangle_{C_b}=O(c/b^2)$ across the window, which gives $E_g=O(cB/b^2+c/B)$ and $|\langle\Delta_\eta\mathcal O\rangle_{C_b}-\delta_\eta|^2\lesssim dB/b^2+d/B$. Mere smoothness of the subtracted two-point function ensures a finite coincidence limit but does not supply this state-specific bound: a smooth coherent excitation of the free field (or an equal mixture of opposite excitations with zero mean) can raise the smooth covariance or second moment arbitrarily high. Because the area weight $f_b(\hat s)$ does not fix the radiative state within each area sector, and neither the free-field coefficients nor higher-order gravitational corrections are bounded for the exact uncut preparation, establishing its local state and norm remains necessary to link this verified free-field inequality to the claimed nonperturbative finite-dimension theorem. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p10.1)
