# §5.3 — Entropy of marginally trapped surfaces

**The physical question.** *Does the null-cone construction derive the Bekenstein–Hawking black-hole entropy—and its one-loop logarithmic correction—by counting gravitational microstates?*

**Verdict.** The boost continuation gives the displayed Gibbs weight $e^{-\hat s}$ for the specified complex corner preparation, and the entropy identity, clock-integral relations, and Schwarzschild one-loop arithmetic all check out. What does not follow automatically is a microscopic state count. Deriving the leading area-law entropy for a large equilibrium family requires both a normalizable closed trace and control of its clock statistics. Likewise, the local conical heat-kernel coefficient alone does not identify the density of states of the clock-restricted Lorentzian theory or match it to the asymptotic fixed-mass ensemble; the reported fixed-mass coefficient agrees with Sen's mixed-ensemble result only once that identification is supplied. [Manuscript, pp. 45–49](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=45)

## Corner continuation, entropy and horizon geometry — (5.50)–(5.52)

1. Write $s=A/(4G_N\hbar)$ and $K=A/(8\pi G_N)$. A real rapidity $\eta$ acts by $\exp(i\eta s/2\pi)$. The specified continuation $\eta=i\pi$ gives $e^{-s/2}$. Its adjoint is the same positive operator. For unitary evolution $U$, $C=Ue^{-\hat s/2}P_\ell$ therefore has $C^\dagger C=P_\ell e^{-\hat s}P_\ell$. The orientation and half-turn matter. With positive area the chosen continuation is contractive. Spectral calculus justifies evaluating the exponential. Identifying this continuation with the gravitational cap amplitude requires the prescribed normal-plane contour and gluing. Unitarity on real boosts alone does not select a Euclidean angle, a vacuum, or those boundary conditions. Only the corner is continued in this argument. [Eqs. (5.22)–(5.25), (5.39)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E22)
2. Suppose $P_\ell$ commutes with $\hat s$, the physical trace is the ordinary trace, and $0<Z_\ell=\operatorname{Tr}_{P_\ell\mathcal H}e^{-\hat s}<\infty$. Then $-\log\rho=\hat s+\log Z_\ell$ on its support, giving (5.50). With a variable angular multiplier $t$, $-\partial_t\log Z_\ell(t)=\langle s\rangle_t$ and $S=(1-t\partial_t)\log Z_\ell(t)$ at $t=1$. This is the canonical entropy relation for the boost. At a single area of degeneracy $d_s$, $Z=d_s e^{-s}$, so $S=\log d_s$ exactly. Even a one-state sector has an area term in (5.50) and zero entropy. The geometric boost charge and the choice of state fix a modular weight. Establishing macroscopic entropy requires control of the degeneracies or the normalization.
3. On a round section with $q_{AB}=r^2\Omega_{AB}$, $\Theta_{(k)}=2k(r)/r$. Using $\ell\cdot n=-1$ and vanishing tangential derivatives of $r$, $(\nabla r)^2=-2\ell(r)n(r)=-r^2\Theta_{(\ell)}\Theta_{(n)}/2$. For Schwarzschild, $(\nabla r)^2=1-2G_NM/r$, giving $r=2G_NM$ on (5.51), with negative ingoing expansion on a regular future horizon cross-section. The bifurcation sphere itself has both expansions zero. Shear vanishes on the round radial null congruence. The affine normalization at $r=\epsilon r_S$ gives $u_*=(1-\epsilon)r_S$, which proves (5.52b) there. Marginality can be imposed locally on a classical cut. The paper supplies no quantum marginality projector or equivalent constrained trace showing that the states counted in (5.39b) have this boundary condition. The Einstein action fixes the coefficient used here. Higher-curvature theories have their own corner charges.

## Clock cutoff and the growth of the trace — (5.53)–(5.65)

Let $u_i$ and $s_i$ be the joint clock and area readings in one nested family of projections, with multiplicities. Define $w_i=e^{-s_i}$, $Z(L)=\sum_{u_i\le L}w_i$, and $m(L)=\sum_{u_i\le L}u_i w_i/Z(L)$. One may use the corresponding positive spectral measure in these calculations.

* **(5.53)–(5.55).** The bound $0\le w=(1-\epsilon)\sqrt{A/4\pi}\le u\le L$ comes from the earlier focusing result. Then $\langle w^2\rangle=(1-\epsilon)^2r_h^2\le\langle u^2\rangle\le Lm(L)$. If $\langle u-w\rangle=o(r_h)$, concavity gives $m(L)\le(1-\epsilon)r_h+o(r_h)$. Thus for $x=(1-\epsilon)r_h/L$ held in $(0,1)$, every limiting ratio lies between $x^2$ and $x$. The small delay is exact classically on a single Schwarzschild geometry. Its validity for the trace over all the admitted microstates requires dominance or a uniform estimate. The relation $4\pi r_h^2=\langle A\rangle_L$ also makes $r_h/L$ a statistic of this ensemble.
* **(5.56)–(5.59).** Integrating each step function gives $I(L)=\int_0^L Z(t)dt=Z(L)(L-m(L))$. Almost everywhere on $I>0$,
  $$
       \frac{d\log(I/L)}{d\log L}=\frac{LZ(L)}{I(L)}-1=\frac{m(L)}{L-m(L)}.
  $$
  If $m(L)/L\to q\in(0,1)$, integration gives $\log Z(L)=[p+o(1)]\log(L/L_0)$, $p=q/(1-q)$. Since $I/LZ\to1-q$, integrating between $L$ and $cL$ also gives $Z(cL)/Z(L)\to c^p$ for each fixed $c>0$. For bounded clock-to-radius ratio, $\log L=\tfrac12\log\langle s\rangle+O(1)$ in fixed units. The logarithmic-growth bound in (5.62) still follows if the ratio is uniformly bounded away from one without a limit. A numerical log coefficient needs more information. The integral identity holds when varying the cutoff of the same spectral measure. Changing the exterior, the representation, or further boundary conditions with $L$ requires a separate comparison.
* **(5.60)–(5.61).** Given the small delay and fixed $x$, $0\le\langle u^2-w^2\rangle\le2L\langle u-w\rangle=o(L^2)$, so $\langle (u/L)^2\rangle=x^2+o(1)$. The displayed identity $u=u^2/L+\int_u^L(u/t)^2dt$ follows by integration. One can establish the first-moment limit without assuming it. Put $J(L)=\int_0^L 2tZ(t)dt=L^2Z(L)[1-\langle(u/L)^2\rangle]$. Then $d\log J/d\log L\to2/(1-x^2)$ almost everywhere in the sense of the limiting bound on the derivative, hence $Z(cL)/Z(L)\to c^{2x^2/(1-x^2)}$. The limiting cumulative distribution of $u/L$ is $c^p$ for $0<c<1$. Taking its mean gives $q=p/(p+1)=2x^2/(1+x^2)$, as claimed.
* **(5.63)–(5.65).** Set $s_*=\pi L^2/[(1-\epsilon)^2G_N\hbar]$. The small mean of the nonnegative gap implies $u/L-\sqrt{s/s_*}\to0$ in probability. The preceding distribution then gives $\Pr(s\le a s_*)\to a^{p/2}$. The number of states $N_{\le a s_*}$ is at most $e^{a s_*}Z(L)$. For fixed $0<\delta<a$ the band $((a-\delta)s_*,a s_*]$ has limiting probability $>0$, giving $N_{\le a s_*}\ge e^{(a-\delta)s_*}Z(L)\Pr(\text{band})$. Taking the large-area limit and then $\delta\downarrow0$ gives (5.64). Thus the leading area entropy and exponential *integrated* count follow under these ensemble assumptions. They give no degeneracy for each exact area. Equation (5.65) follows with log coefficient $p/2$. [Clock and trace derivation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS3.p6.2)

## The area window, replica term and fixed-mass ensemble — (5.66)–(5.69)

**Window resolution.** For $b=s_b>0$, write $f_b(s)=\exp[-(s-b)^2/(4b)]$. The integral of $b^{-1/2}e^{-b/4-s^2/(4b)}$ from zero to infinity is $2\sqrt\pi e^{-|s|/2}$. Including the other factors in (5.66) gives $e^{(s-|s|)/2}=1$ for physical $s\ge0$, including $s=0$. Positive summation yields $Z=\int db\,T_b/\sqrt{4\pi b}$, $T_b=\operatorname{Tr}(f_b(\hat s)e^{-\hat s}P_L)$. This identity is exact with any multiplicities. It does not give their values.

**Local one-loop coefficient.** In four-dimensional pure Einstein gravity the known gauge-fixed graviton-minus-ghost heat coefficient on a smooth Ricci-flat background is $53R_{abcd}R^{abcd}/[45(4\pi)^2]$ for the logarithm of length. At a stationary round Schwarzschild section the trace and shear along the horizon vanish. The Gauss projection gives the surface invariant in (5.67b), and $\int_S R[q]dA=8\pi$. The Euler defect term linear in $1-n$ is $8\pi(1-n)\int_S R[q]dA$. With $r_h\propto\sqrt b$, the resulting local logarithm is $(53/45)(64\pi^2)/(16\pi^2)\log\sqrt b=(106/45)\log b$. A direct cross-check on complete Euclidean Schwarzschild uses $R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=12r_h^2/r^6$ and period $4\pi r_h$, giving the same integrated invariant $64\pi^2$. The cited smooth and regulated cone calculations concern the local term. [Bastianelli, Bonezzi and Melis, eq. (46)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13287) · [Fursaev, Patrushev and Solodukhin, eqs. (1.3)–(1.6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.4000)

**Missing identification in (5.67c).** A replica derivative computes $-\partial_n\log[\operatorname{Tr}(W_b^n)/T_b^n]|_{1}$, where $W_b=f_b(\hat s)e^{-\hat s}P_L$. Exactly, $S_b=\langle s-\log f_b\rangle_b+\log T_b$. If $d_s$ states occupy area $s$, the same entropy equals $H(p_s)+\sum_s p_s\log d_s$ for $p_s=d_s f_b(s)e^{-s}/T_b$. Evaluating the geometric response at each fixed $s$ does not by itself account for $H(p_s)$ or the measure of the varying area. Consider unit-spaced $s=k$ with $d_k=\lceil e^k k^\gamma\rceil$, $k\ge1$, and a cutoff well above the packet. At fixed area $\log d_k-k=\gamma\log k+o(1)$. The Gaussian sum gives $T_b\sim\sqrt{4\pi b}\,b^\gamma$, hence $\log T_b=(\gamma+1/2)\log b+O(1)$, even though $-\langle\log f_b\rangle_b=O(1)$. This tests the inference from fixed-area data, without claiming this spectrum describes gravity. A calculation of the normalized replicated packet, including the area collective coordinate, ghost and graviton zero modes, and cap/corner measure, or a proof that those factors cancel or are already included, is needed. Appendix D.3 gives bounds and uses a Euclidean disk factor $e^s$ for regular saddles. It does not calculate the Gaussian packet's collective-coordinate normalization. The disk factor records a semiclassical action and supplies no independent microscopic multiplicity. [Eq. (5.67c)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E67.3) · [Appendix D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS3.p8.1)

**Power counting and ensemble comparison.** Suppose one establishes $T_b\asymp b^c$ uniformly in a band with endpoints fixed fractions of $s_*$ and with nonvanishing limiting share of the trace. Then $Z(L)\asymp s_*^{c+1/2}$ at the level of the log exponent. Matching to $Z(L)=L^{p+o(1)}$ gives $p=2c+1$. With $c=106/45$, this yields $p=257/45$, $q=257/302$, and the coefficient of $\log\langle s\rangle$ in the clock ensemble is $257/90$. Equation (5.61) then requires $x^2=257/347$ for this nested ensemble. These algebraic relations check.

If the weighted area measure has a smooth local density $e^{-s}dN/ds\sim C s^{c-1/2}$, the packet calculation and $s=4\pi G_N M^2/\hbar$ give $dN/dM\propto e^{s(M)}s(M)^c$, because $ds/dM\propto\sqrt{s}$. It is important to undo $e^{-s}$ in the spectral measure before coarse-graining. A polynomial integrated asymptotic or a broad Gaussian convolution does not prove the requisite local density. The physical identification also requires an ADM mass observable or an interior-to-exterior matching for the fluctuating states and consistent treatment of angular momenta. The classical Schwarzschild relation fixes a background. In the conventional energy ensemble one instead writes $Z(\beta)=\int dM\,g(M)e^{-\beta M}$ and formally inverts it. A classical Legendre transform keeps the extremal exponent, while the fluctuation determinant and the measure govern logarithms. In asymptotically flat Schwarzschild the canonical mass integral has negative-heat-capacity saddle issues. The clock trace here has a boost weight and a size cutoff. Deriving a fixed-energy density by an inverse Laplace transform would require a partition function for ADM energy.

For the fixed-mass density with all angular momenta summed, Sen gives $(212/45)\log r_h=(106/45)\log s+O(1)$ in four-dimensional pure gravity. His fixed Cartan-angular-momentum and spin-singlet ensembles have different coefficients. This confirms the numerical comparison in (5.69) and the need to specify the ensemble. An independent Lorentzian derivation of the coefficient would require the identifications above. [Sen, eqs. (1.2)–(1.5), (3.13)–(3.15) and footnote 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0971)
