# 5.1 Existence of states in a causal diamond

**The physical question.** *If you restrict a four-dimensional quantum causal diamond to a finite geometric clock reading, does even a single physical state survive?*

**Verdict.** On a smooth expanding classical light cone, gravitational-wave focusing gives a finite clock reading. Given the positive cutoff forms, the paper's quantum variational calculation and spectral tail bound also follow. What remains open is whether the paper's spherical Gaussian state has *any* overlap with the domain of the unregulated quantum clock. Equation (5.16) estimates a quantum resolvent using a classical phase-space probability—a step Footnote 18 itself flags as heuristic. Proving that allowed states exist for some finite clock bound requires a genuine quantum domain result; the stronger assertion (5.19) requires the entire Gaussian to lie in the closure of that domain. [Paper §§4.3 and 5.1, pp. 30–31 and 35–38](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1) · [Appendix C.2 source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p3.1)

## Reproducing the focusing and spectral calculations

1. **Classical focusing, (3.25)–(3.28) and (5.8).** With affine tangent, zero twist and vacuum Einstein equations, including a cosmological constant, Raychaudhuri gives $\rho''=-\rho\|\partial_u\bar q\|^2/8$. The norm uses the positive metric on the two-dimensional cuts. On a generator with $p=\partial_u\rho>0$, use $v=\rho/\rho_S$. Dividing the equation by $p^2/\rho_S$ gives $\partial_v\log p=-v\|\partial_v\bar q\|^2/8$, so $p=p_\epsilon e^{-\Xi_y(v)}$. Integrating $du/dv=\rho_S/p$ gives $u_*(y)=\rho_S(y)\int_\epsilon^1e^{\Xi_y(v)}dv$ after the cap normalization. Thus $(1-\epsilon)\rho_S\le u_*(y)\le(1-\epsilon)\rho_S e^{\Xi_y(1)}$. The positive angular weight in (3.28) gives (5.8). A round shear-free corner gives $u_*=(1-\epsilon)b$, with $\mathscr A=4\pi b^2$. Smooth data on the compact cut and interval justify the finite maximum. A generator which turns before $S$ lies outside this coordinate chart, as §3.2 acknowledges. [Classical reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS2.p6.1)

2. **Cutoff form, (5.5)–(5.7), (5.15) and (C.11)–(C.14).** Write $c=\Xi_*$ and $K=(1-\epsilon)\mathscr A$. Assume the operators $\Xi_y(v)$ are positive self-adjoint with measurable spectral calculus, and the positive densities satisfy $\int\rho_S(y)^2 d\Omega=\mathscr A I$. Then $a=\int\rho_S(y)d\Omega$ has trivial kernel at positive area. Positivity gives $K\|a^{-1/2}\eta\|^2\le Q_c[\eta]\le K e^c\|a^{-1/2}\eta\|^2$ on $D(a^{-1/2})$. The form norms are equivalent there, hence $Q_c$ is closed and densely defined. For its operator $T_c$, completing the square in each spectral fiber gives the unique minimizer $L(L+T_c)^{-1}\Omega$ of (5.5). [Cutoff form and minimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p3.1)

3. **Exact content of the two limits.** Set $Q=\sup_cQ_c$, $M=\overline{D(Q)}$, and let $T$ be the positive self-adjoint operator of $Q$ on $M$. Monotone convergence of closed forms gives $\Psi_L=L(L+T)^{-1}P_M\Omega$. All finite-reading projections $P_\ell$ extend by zero on $M^\perp$. Applying the spectral theorem on $M$ yields
   $$
   \lim_{L\to\infty}\Psi_L=P_M\Omega,\qquad
   \lim_{\ell\to\infty}\|P_\ell\Omega\|^2=\|P_M\Omega\|^2.
   $$
   These statements include $M=\{0\}$. The bound $uL^2/(L+u)^2\le L/4$ gives $Q[\Psi_L]\le L/4$ and (5.17) by Markov's inequality. The triangle inequality gives (5.18). Equation (5.19) is equivalent to $\Omega\in M$. Nonzero projection for *some* finite $\ell$ is equivalent to $P_M\Omega\ne0$. Neither criterion gives a nonzero projection for a prescribed ratio $\ell/b$. The operator area bound (5.21c), when available, rules it out for $\ell<(1-\epsilon)b$. [Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p4.2) · [Behrndt, Hassi, de Snoo and Wietsma, theorem 4.2](https://www.numerik.math.tu-graz.ac.at/~behrndt/BehrndtHassiSnooWietsma09.pdf)

4. **One sufficient test for existence.** A normalized $\chi\in D(Q)$ with $\langle\chi,\Omega\rangle\ne0$ suffices. Since $\|(1-P_\ell)\chi\|^2\le Q[\chi]/\ell$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\|P_\ell\Omega\|\ge |\langle\chi,\Omega\rangle|-\sqrt{Q[\chi]/\ell}$. This supplies an explicit positive lower bound once $\ell>Q[\chi]/|\langle\chi,\Omega\rangle|^2$. A construction with control as the angular and radial regulators are removed would settle the weaker state-existence claim. Strong convergence to the Gaussian or density of the entire form domain is a larger claim.

## Testing the finite-Planck-constant inference

For $r_L(t)=t/(L+t)$, the resolvent identity gives operator monotonicity. Applying it to $T_c\le Ke^c a^{-1}$ gives $\langle r_L(T_c)\rangle\le\langle K(K+Le^{-c}a)^{-1}\rangle$. At $c=\frac12\log(L/b)$ this tends to zero by bounded convergence and $\ker a=0$. This proves the corner estimate even with correlated radiation. The remaining term compares this cutoff with arbitrarily large $c$ at the **same** $L$. For ordinary functions the difference in $r_L$ lies between zero and the indicator of $\max_y\Xi_y(1)>\frac12\log(L/b)$. Classical Gaussian averaging now works if the reduced chart is regular almost surely. The quantum expectation requires the Weyl symbol of the *full resolvent*. Gaussian Wigner positivity supplies no order comparison between that symbol and the classical indicator for noncommuting factors. [Equations (5.15)–(5.16) and footnote 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E15)

The local formal calculation checks. Putting $f_0=1-L/(L+u)$ makes the first Poisson bracket vanish. The second Moyal term gives $f-f_0=-\hbar^2 L J^{IJ}J^{KM}u_{,IK}[(L+u)^{-1}]_{,JM}/[8(L+u)]+O(\hbar^3)$ when the clock symbol itself is classical. Two derivatives of $f_0$ give (5.14). For a fixed finite list of modes in a regular ball, the recursion in (C.15d) gives $O(1/L)$ for each fixed Taylor coefficient. Taking $\hbar$ finite needs control of the remainder with the focusing cutoff removed, and the continuum needs estimates uniform in the mode list. The paper supplies neither estimate. [Equation (5.12)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E12) · [Recursion (C.15d)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.E15.4)

**A one-pair test of (5.16).** Work in dimensionless canonical units with $[X,P]=i\hbar$, let $V(t)=t^2+t^4$, and give two focusing terms equal weights. Take
$$
T_c=\tfrac12\left(e^{\min(V(X),c)}+e^{\min(V(P),c)}\right).
$$
Each cutoff operator is bounded and positive, and increases in form order. The classical reading $[e^{V(x)}+e^{V(p)}]/2$ is finite at every phase-space point and has a smooth minimum at the center. Every Gaussian Wigner measure assigns vanishing probability to $\max(V(x),V(p))>\frac12\log L$ as $L\to\infty$. Each fixed local order about the center has the same large-$L$ suppression used above. Yet finite $Q[\eta]$ requires both $e^{V(x)/2}\eta(x)$ and $e^{V(p)/2}\widehat\eta(p)$ in $L^2$. The quartic weights imply Gaussian $L^2$ bounds with *arbitrarily large* exponents in both conjugate variables. The $L^2$ Hardy uncertainty theorem then forces $\eta=0$ for every $\hbar>0$ (rescale $p=\hbar\xi$). Here $M=\{0\}$, all limiting finite-reading projections vanish, and the exact loss of overlap is one. This counterexample to the classical-to-quantum inference uses an auxiliary pair. Deciding the domain for the gravitational shear and corner operators requires their particular dynamics. [Cowling et al., implication (A2) and theorem 4](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~ponce/co-es-ke-po-ve.pdf)

**Removing the mode cutoff adds a distinct regularity question.** The Gaussian Wigner integral in (C.15a) is explicitly for a finite list of modes. To illustrate the dependence on resolution, take one angular harmonic with a linearized radial profile $z_N(v)=\sum_{k\le N}q_k e_k(v)$ in normalized Dirichlet sine modes on $[\epsilon,1]$ and the paper's example widths $\operatorname{Var}(q_k)=1/(2sk)$ at fixed $s=\mathscr A/(4G_N\hbar)$. Orthogonality gives
$$
\mathbb E\int_\epsilon^1|z_N'(v)|^2dv
 =\frac{\pi^2N(N+1)}{4s(1-\epsilon)^2}.
$$
In this linearized model the sum also diverges almost surely. Its summands are proportional to $kZ_k^2$ for independent unit normals, and $Z_k^2>1$ occurs infinitely often with probability one. For $\epsilon>0$ the $v$ weight in the focusing form keeps this divergence. This tests the assumed support on smooth configurations at increasing resolution. Applying it to the full nonlinear canonical map or to the exact spectral clock would require an additional argument. Likewise, subtracting a vacuum divergence would require a fresh proof of the positivity used in (4.27). ## Preparation, compression and physical scope

If $P_\ell\Omega\ne0$, its normalization gives a state of the original sharp area provided the joint clock/area projections strongly commute. In a fixed-area sector this is immediate since the area is scalar. Extending the calculation to all of $\mathcal H_\omega$ requires compatible clock operators on the area sectors reached by general insertions. Formula (5.3) then follows from the joint spectral probabilities when the conditioning event has positive probability. [Equations (5.1)–(5.3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E1)

For an irreducible representation of the full observable algebra, every nonzero vector is cyclic because the closure of its algebra orbit is reducing. Applying the bounded projection to this dense orbit gives (5.20). Compression of $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_\omega)$ then yields $\mathcal B(P_\ell\mathcal H_\omega)$. External projections allow excursions through $1-P_\ell$ between insertions, as (C.21) explicitly shows. These conclusions apply to the chosen pure extension in §4.2. They hold for Hilbert spaces of arbitrary dimension, so cyclicity alone gives no dimensional bound. The coherent-displacement argument after (C.16) would establish density in the fixed-area Fock space if the exact full-weight estimate held for all nearby finite-mode displaced Gaussians. Nonzero overlaps for nearby Gaussians alone give a weaker statement.
