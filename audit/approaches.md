# Which approaches to quantum gravity can answer the same question?

A beam of light can focus in a classical spacetime, in a semiclassical state of quantum gravity or in a theory with a fluctuating classical metric. Measuring its mean focusing tests an equation shared by those descriptions. Counting how many distinguishable preparations fit inside the beam's causal diamond asks a second question. The choice of a clock, the treatment of the boundary and the meaning of “distinguishable” are then part of the physics. Venkatesa Chandrasekaran's four-dimensional vacuum proposal makes unusually specific choices for all three. This research map compares representative programs through calculations that could bear on those choices. A missing translation between two observables leaves the comparison open. [Chandrasekaran, arXiv:2611.34924v1, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1)

## Four comparisons with specified units and preparations

### How much does a weak pulse delay the clock?

Choose a smooth expanding vacuum cone, fixed cap fraction $0<\epsilon<1$, and round corner density $\varrho_S=R$, where $R$ is a length. Use the paper's affine normalization. Send a smooth small shear on the incoming data with $\partial_v\bar q=aH(v,y)+O(a^2)$, where $v=\varrho/\varrho_S$ and amplitude $a$ are dimensionless. The trace-free tensor $H$ is compared in the same angular boundary frame. The norm below uses the unperturbed unit sphere metric. Hold the corner density fixed across preparations. Equations (3.26)–(3.28) give, by expanding the exponential and reversing the triangular order of integration,

$$
\frac{\bar u_*(a)-\bar u_*(0)}{R}
 =\frac{a^2}{8}\int\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}
   \int_\epsilon^1 dv\ v(1-v)\,\lVert H(v,y)\rVert^2 +O(a^3),
 \qquad \bar u_*(0)=(1-\epsilon)R .
$$

This is a dimensionless prediction for a specified classical preparation. The weight $v(1-v)$ favors the midpoint $v=1/2$ when it is inside the integration interval. The factor $1-v$ suppresses pulses near the corner, while $v$ suppresses pulses near a smooth vertex. To compare a quantum calculation, prepare narrow coherent packets for the same profile and background, use identical angular and radial resolution, subtract the zero-pulse mean, and take the semiclassical limit at fixed $a$ before extracting the quadratic coefficient. Curvature scales and physical pulse widths must permit a smooth classical expansion. A disagreement surviving both limits would directly challenge the claimed recovery of vacuum GR in that regime. [Manuscript, Eqs. (3.26)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E26) · [Classical and quantum focusing audit](05-1.md)

### How many physical preparations pass a fixed clock bound?

Fix $G_N,\hbar$, the cap prescription, boundary frame, physical clock bound $\ell$ and the representation or specified superselection sector. With $c=1$ and $\ell_P^2=G_N\hbar$, report the dimensionless ratio $\ell/\ell_P$. Define $P_\ell=\mathbf 1_{[0,\ell]}(\widehat{\bar u}_*)$ and $N_\ell=\operatorname{rank}P_\ell$ on the physical Hilbert space. There is no thermal averaging in this count. First test a single fixed total-area sector, then account for every sector reached by the physical preparations. One surviving infinite family in any such sector suffices to disprove the finite dimension asserted for the whole space. To establish a positive finite result for the whole space requires control both within and across sectors. [Manuscript, §§5.1–5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2) · [Physical corner and one-particle tests](finite-kinematics.md)

For a comparison that can start below the level of a state count, prepare one identified incoming state and record the dimensionless probability of an area interval $J$ conditional on a clock interval $I$, using the physical area $A/\ell_P^2$ and time $\bar u_*/\ell_P$ as the readouts. Specify a nonzero conditioning probability. In v1 this is a joint spectral measurement under strong commutation. A classical stochastic theory can give a distribution for the two readouts without assigning a Hilbert-space rank to classical geometries. Agreement on one distribution is informative about that preparation. Establishing $N_\ell$ requires a result for the full space of preparations. [Manuscript, Eq. (5.3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E3)

### How does low-energy radiation scatter?

Choose $\Lambda=0$, a four-dimensional asymptotically flat vacuum, and normalized graviton wave packets with specified helicities, center-of-mass energy $E\ll M_P$ and scattering angle away from the forward pole. In $\hbar=c=1$ units $M_P=G_N^{-1/2}$. Match massless species, packet shapes and infrared prescription. A shared theoretical target is the dimensionless four-graviton amplitude with the momentum delta function removed. For the all-plus two-loop hard amplitude compare its explicit scale logarithm at fixed renormalized cubic-curvature coupling in the same convention. Pure Einstein gravity gives $-1/4$ in units of the kinematic factor $K$ defined in the linked derivation. Running of the coupling cancels the arbitrary scale in physical predictions. A detector comparison requires a probability inclusive over unresolved radiation or a compatible dressing of asymptotic states. Neither test is performed by taking the overlap of two quadratic sources on a single incoming cone. [Bern, Chi, Dixon and Edison](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02422) · [Normalization, subtractions and $K$](scattering.md)

### Which black-hole states are being counted?

Choose a large four-dimensional, uncharged, asymptotically flat Schwarzschild saddle with pure gravity as the massless field content. Use intrinsic rest mass $M$, sum over spins, and define the dimensionless entropy $\log[M_0\rho(M)]$ for a fixed reference mass $M_0$ and a density of states per unit mass. With $k_B=c=1$ and $s=A_H/(4G_N\hbar)$, the one-loop coefficient of $\log s$ is $106/45$ in Sen's mixed ensemble. Specifying the mass density matters even for a nonrotating saddle. At leading order $s=4\pi G_NM^2/\hbar$, so a density per unit $s$ gains a factor $ds/dM\propto\sqrt{s}$ in conversion to a density per unit $M$. A projection onto a definite spin is another change. A microscopic derivation must implement the mass and spin conditions, remove exterior and translational contributions consistently and exhibit its measure. The area-packet arithmetic in v1 agrees with Sen. The measure for its proposed trace is consequential to that comparison. [Sen, Eqs. (1.1)–(1.5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0971#page=6) · [Ensembles and zero modes](sen.md) · [Area packet](ensemble-crosscheck.md)

## What each construction contributes

**Classical GR and low-energy quantum gravity.** Smooth classical vacuum Einstein evolution on a regular characteristic patch fixes the clock-delay coefficient above, with the null expansion kept positive on the interval. Quantizing the two massless helicities as an effective field theory (EFT) gives a systematic expansion of amplitudes at $E/M_P\ll1$. Long-distance nonanalytic effects and the specified two-loop scale dependence are calculable. Finite local couplings can require ultraviolet matching. A failure of the clock-delay or scattering target under its shared assumptions would locate a wrong low-energy limit. Extrapolating an EFT state count past its cutoff cannot determine the physical rank of the diamond. [Classical Peierls brackets for null data](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.2541) · [Donoghue's EFT calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9405057) · [Two-loop scale dependence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02422)

**Reduced phase space and Dirac quantization.** The former solves constraints before quantization. In the latter, quantum constraints define physical states and the inner product must assign probabilities to gauge-invariant measurements. Their comparison needs the same clock, corner charges, gauge quotient and inner product. For the same prepared packet, compare the conditional area probability above, or exhibit an inner-product-preserving map intertwining its area and clock projections. A difference would distinguish the specified reductions, domains or orderings. There are calculable precedents. Solving the disk problem in pure $2+1$ gravity yields a boundary twist with an explicitly quantized spectrum. Pressure-free dust in $3+1$ gravity permits a relational Hamiltonian and physical area observables, with the dust present in the theory. The $2+1$ bulk has no local gravitational waves, and removing the dust while retaining a measurable null clock requires a separate construction. [Andrade e Silva and Jacobson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10084) · [Relational Dirac observables](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411031) · [Dust-time quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0119)

**Loop quantum gravity, spin foams and group field theory.** Quantizing spatial holonomies and fluxes gives a self-adjoint kinematic area operator with discrete spectrum. Constraint imposition and relational localization can change a spectrum. In a related sum over labeled discrete geometries, the large-label Lorentzian EPRL four-simplex amplitude has two opposite Regge phases for suitable nondegenerate boundary data. These supply explicit comparisons at the level of geometry and semiclassical propagation. A finer common test would use a marked boundary sphere, impose the physical constraints, refine the foam at fixed $R$ and pulse width, and recover the weak-pulse coefficient. Physical multiplicities at bounded clock would test $N_\ell$ directly. Failure of a single-simplex approximation diagnoses that approximation. A continuum discrepancy after all these identifications would concern the chosen dynamics. Isolated-horizon loop counts fix their own horizon data. Comparing their logarithms to Sen requires the spin sum, mass measure and exterior modes specified above. Group-field condensates give effective homogeneous evolution under state and kinetic-term assumptions. Loop quantum cosmology also exhibits a bounce in a flat isotropic massless-scalar model. Neither homogeneous observable supplies a four-dimensional inhomogeneous pulse or a clock-projected state count. [Area operators](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9602046) · [Relational spectrum counterexamples](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.1721) · [Lorentzian simplex asymptotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2440) · [Isolated horizons](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0634) · [Condensates](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3576) · [Loop quantum cosmology](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0607039)

**Other null quantizations and algebraic gravity.** Reisenberger gives classical brackets for regular observables on two intersecting null hypersurfaces. Wieland quantizes a restricted impulsive shear sector with a relational clock and a Holst term. A direct comparison would embed a shared regularized profile, identify cap conditions, action and frame charge, then compare the dimensionless clock response and boost matrix elements in matched states. A difference already in the shared classical bracket would identify a boundary or reduction discrepancy. Jensen, Sorce and Speranza obtain a type II description of compact subregions in a small-$G_N$ expansion with an observer and assumptions on modular flow. A type II$_1$ factor has a finite normalized trace and supports arbitrarily many orthogonal nonzero projections. For example, projections of traces $2^{-n}$ can have a finite total trace. Its trace therefore measures something different from ordinary Hilbert rank. A bridge to v1 would specify the same observable algebra and families of states and compare normalized correlation functions as $\ell/\ell_P$ grows. Finite matrices can themselves have a type II limit. The types at two different scales alone cannot decide which construction fails. [Reisenberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.2541) · [Wieland](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17491) · [Jensen, Sorce and Speranza](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01837) · [Null subregion algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07915)

**Asymptotic safety.** The hypothesis is a continuum trajectory attracted at high energy to a fixed point with finitely many independent parameters. Functional renormalization-group calculations give fixed-point evidence in specified truncations. A Lorentzian calculation found a massless peak and continuum in a graviton spectral function. A subsequent scalar scattering calculation illustrates the sensitivity to momentum dependence even when the fixed point is specified. Match the infrared Newton constant, external helicities and matter content, then extract the dimensionless hard amplitude above from on-shell vertices with gauge identities and uncertainties under regulator and truncation changes. A persistent wrong infrared logarithm would challenge that trajectory's Einstein limit. Drift under an enlarged truncation measures the approximation. The fixed point and a gauge-fixed two-point function each leave the clock-restricted trace open. [Functional flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9605030) · [Fixed-point evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.4815) · [Lorentzian spectral function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13232) · [Momentum-dependent scattering example](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21285)

**Causal dynamical triangulations (CDT).** CDT sums causal triangulated geometries with specified topology and action. In an extended phase at controlled lattice volumes, simulations find a large-scale geometry described by a de Sitter volume profile and measure its fluctuations. One comparison accessible on that background is the connected covariance of slice volumes, normalized by $V_4^{3/2}$ at fixed total four-volume $V_4$. Evaluate it at matched proper times in units $V_4^{1/4}$, with the same slicing, time-origin alignment, topology and state preparation. This statistic is dimensionless in four spacetime dimensions. A de Sitter saddle of v1 would need the corresponding volume observables and ensemble before making this comparison. For a closer diamond test, introduce two physical tips and a marked cut, calibrate length and shape, and extrapolate the clock response at fixed physical widths. A failure to stabilize with lattice spacing or box size leaves that continuum estimate undetermined. Counting simplices in a sampled geometry measures its volume. [Ambjørn, Jurkiewicz and Loll](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0505154) · [Volume profile and fluctuations](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4481)

**Causal sets.** A locally finite partial order records causal precedence. In a Poisson sprinkling of density $\rho$ into a given smooth diamond of four-volume $V_4$, $\mathbb E[n]=\operatorname{Var}(n)=\rho V_4$ fixes the calibration in units $[\rho]=L^{-4}$. There is a sharper comparison than counting events. The mean Benincasa–Dowker–Glaser action on small sprinkled causal diamonds has a continuum bulk curvature term and a joint-area term in the controlled expansion. Compare the dimensionless $\langle S\rangle/\hbar$ as a function of the corner area at fixed metric and Newton normalization. Matching the boundaries, normal normalization and joint prescription is essential before equating a joint-area coefficient with v1's boost charge or entropy. A residual under those identifications would concern the proposed action or its boundary prescription. The sprinkling average conditions on a continuum geometry. Determining a physical state count requires a quantum measure over causal sets and a representation of the selected preparations. [Boundary terms for causal sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05388) · [Curved small diamonds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13192)

**Strings, M-theory and holography.** Worldsheet calculations give explicit four-graviton amplitudes in particular string vacua. A four-dimensional comparison requires a compactification, the resulting massless spectrum and a hierarchy placing external energies below both string and compactification thresholds. Extra massless fields still contribute in loops. For matched species, compare the two-loop target or the finite low-energy coefficients in identical conventions. A difference diagnoses that vacuum's low-energy physics. Strominger and Vafa counted BPS bound states for a class of charged extremal five-dimensional holes. Their area law is powerful evidence for a microscopic mechanism in that ensemble. Obtaining the neutral nonextremal four-dimensional density above calls for an independent calculation. The BFSS matrix proposal likewise reproduces a particular long-distance supergravity interaction in its eleven-dimensional limit. [Type II four-graviton calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9910056) · [Strominger and Vafa](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9601029) · [BFSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9610043)

A boundary conformal field theory (CFT) supplies another observable in a specified AdS dual. With reflecting global AdS boundary conditions and a CFT on a sphere of radius $L$, define $N_\partial(x)=\operatorname{Tr}\mathbf1_{[0,x]}((H_\partial-E_0)L/\hbar)$ in $c=1$ units, for fixed charges and boundary conditions. The energy variable $x$ and the multiplicities are dimensionless. For a discrete, finite-multiplicity spectrum with no finite accumulation point the count is finite at finite $x$. Trace-class $e^{-\beta(H_\partial-E_0)}$ at some $\beta>0$ also implies this bound. A precise embedding of v1 would specify the dual, field content, energy and charge map, and whether its comparison includes all states in that range. A mismatch in a matched sector would falsify that identification. The ABJM example realizes an AdS$_4$ setting with extra compact directions and fields. Matching all boundary energy states to one small bulk clock window would require a separate argument. CFT flat-space limits offer a route to a scattering comparison where the limit exists. [Maldacena](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9711200) · [ABJM](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1218) · [Flat-space amplitude extraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1485) · [v1's AdS argument](area-ads.md)

**Semiclassical, stochastic and postquantum classical gravity.** Deterministic semiclassical gravity sources a classical metric with a renormalized quantum stress expectation. Its small-fluctuation regime permits tests of mean backreaction. The Page–Geilker torsion-balance experiment tested a model in which macroscopically distinct source placements in an uncollapsed state source the averaged field. The observed field followed the realized placement. Its interpretation depends on the state and measurement assumptions. Stochastic semiclassical gravity goes further. For a specified scalar vacuum on Minkowski space, Martín and Verdaguer calculated metric and curvature two-point functions induced by stress fluctuations. Such a calculation can be compared through two identically smeared tidal measurements with the same matter state, subtraction and initial graviton fluctuations included or removed on both sides. A difference in the matched induced covariance would challenge the approximation in its proposed regime. Adding quantum matter to v1 is required for this particular comparison. [Page and Geilker](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.47.979) · [Stochastic semiclassical calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0001098)

Oppenheim instead takes the gravitational phase-space variables to be classical, and assigns a positive operator-valued probability density to them and the quantum matter they couple to. Linear, completely positive, trace-preserving hybrid dynamics can combine backreaction with decoherence and classical noise. The decoherence–diffusion trade-off restricts their strengths for the specified coupling and kernels. Test it by preparing masses with specified density profiles, branch separations and interrogation times. Measure matter interference contrast and the fluctuations of the calibrated gravitational field using the spatial and temporal filters in the same proposed model. Report, for example, the dimensionless visibility $|\rho_{12}(t)|/|\rho_{12}(0)|$ and the variance of a filtered acceleration in $\mathrm{m}^2\mathrm{s}^{-4}$. Converting the latter to the model's diffusion coefficient requires its response kernel, bandwidth and noise backgrounds. A joint confidence region incompatible with every allowed choice of the model's kernels would exclude that class under the experimental assumptions. A single bound normally restricts parameters. A spin-entanglement witness tests whether a locally coupled gravitational field carries only classical information. A verified violation excludes that hypothesis if the probes start separable, other interactions are bounded, and no quantum system passes between them by another route. A null witness can also result from ordinary decoherence or poor sensitivity. The vacuum null-cone proposal needs a matter coupling and detector dynamics to predict either experiment. [Oppenheim's hybrid theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03116) · [Decoherence–diffusion inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01982) · [Bose and collaborators' interferometers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06050) · [Local mediation assumptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19969)

**Other continuum dynamics and emergent gravity.** Higher-curvature actions introduce physical scales and alter propagation or scattering if used as fundamental dynamics. Hořava's anisotropic construction takes a preferred foliation and permits higher spatial derivatives. Compare a long-wavelength tensor packet's speed relative to the low-energy light cone, or its dimensionless dispersion $\omega/(c|\mathbf k|)$ as a function of the physical wave number in units of the model's crossover scale. Matter clocks or another operational reference are needed for an observed speed. A bound on dispersion restricts the coefficients and accessible scales of the specified model. For Lorentz-invariant higher-derivative gravity, the pole positions, residues and prescription for propagating states enter a scattering comparison. Power-counting and renormalizability answer ultraviolet divergence questions. Positivity and measured probabilities require their own checks. Jacobson's thermodynamic argument instead assumes a local horizon entropy density and a heat relation to recover the Einstein equation. Its local first-law and focusing response can agree with v1 while leaving the microscopic rank undetermined. A failure of the macroscopic relation would challenge an assumption of equilibrium or entropy density. No specific microscopic spectrum follows from an equation of state alone. [Hořava](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.3775) · [Stelle](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.16.953) · [Jacobson's horizon argument](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504004) · [Fixed-volume entanglement equilibrium](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04753)

## Which result would change the assessment?

A common weak-pulse coefficient would establish recovery of characteristic Einstein evolution in that preparation. Reproducing the hard four-graviton scale logarithm would also test interaction vertices and intermediate channels. Either agreement leaves competing microscopic descriptions viable. A derived finite matching coefficient or a specified distribution that differs between two otherwise matched models would distinguish their dynamics. [Scattering audit](scattering.md)

The direct test of v1's distinctive claim remains available even without a competing ultraviolet theory. Construct the physical clock and exhibit either a regulator-independent finite bound on its projector rank with a nonzero allowed state, or arbitrarily many mutually orthogonal states that pass the same fixed bound and survive the constraints. A finite partition function or finite rank at each cutoff leaves this limit open. This criterion applies equally to quantization on a null surface, on a spatial slice and by a sum over histories once each identifies the same physical preparation.
