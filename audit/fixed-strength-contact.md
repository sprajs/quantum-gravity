# Common timing windows at fixed nonzero wave strength

Use the same two distinct cap fractions, point calibration, matched weighted radius, weak Rosen family and translated smooth compact window as in the [synchronized weak-wave calculation](synchronized-contact.md). In particular, the positive target superenergy for the background of strength $\lambda$ is $w_\lambda=\lambda^2w_0$. The target stays fixed under the metric variations defining each bracket. Fix the fractions $0<a<b<1$, radius $R$, emitter frame and window shape $f$. Write $h$ for its shrinking proper-time scale. Assume the swept causal hull is in the convex, image-free patch, the Killing field generating the sweep is uniformly timelike there, and all the cuts and the return-time control are regular. These conditions hold for the indicated family near flat space on a sufficiently small common patch for the experiment.

**Fixed-strength conclusion.** For all sufficiently small fixed $\lambda>0$, the absolute value of the ambient classical Einstein bracket of the two synchronized readings tends to infinity as $h\downarrow0$. The weak-wave calculation determines a nonzero Taylor coefficient of the term of order $h^{-3}$. The proof allows more singular terms at fixed strength, so it does not give the sign or the leading power there. The measurement includes the pointwise superenergy calibration of the central emitter. [Original optical clock, Chandrasekaran, arXiv:2611.34924v1, Eqs. (3.26)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E27)

## Removing two normal derivatives from the optical source

Write the full clock differential on vacuum solutions as $c_a(\lambda)P_\lambda+R_a(\lambda)$, where $P_\lambda$ differentiates the superenergy at fixed trial rapidity, including frame orthonormalization. The coefficient $c_a(\lambda)$ is the exact boost multiplier. It extends smoothly across zero, since the analytic boost derivative of the clock vanishes to second order and the denominator is $4\lambda^2w_0$. The point functional itself is smooth and vanishes at zero. Its metric differential has order at most two. Common timing profiles make the $P_\lambda$–$P_\lambda$ pairing exactly zero by skewness.

For the remainder, start with the [curved optical adjoint, Eqs. (1)–(4)](weak-curved.md). Only the outgoing tidal integral can involve two metric derivatives. Use the trace-free coefficient of the varied all-screen tidal tensor, subtracting its trace times the linearized Ricci contraction along the ray. Indeed the full trace of the tidal matrix of an orthonormal screen is $-\mathrm{Ric}(k,k)$, and the background Ricci tensor vanishes. This trace identity concerns the full variation, including the moving ray and screen. Applying it to the metric-only tidal variation leaves frame and transport terms involving at most one metric derivative. The subtracted linear functional is conserved because linearized Ricci kills every pure gauge perturbation on this background. It vanishes on all vacuum solutions. Replacing first one and then the other complete conserved source in the finite-window pairing therefore leaves the bracket invariant. This justifies subsequent calculations with the gauge-fixed tensor kernel, including contractions between pieces which individually need not be conserved.

The trace-free tidal coefficient satisfies $K_{\rm TF}(t,n)=O(t^2)$ at the launch, with a smooth quotient in affine radius $t$, sky direction $n$ in each chart, and $\lambda$. In the notation of the adjoint, $K=LJ^T$, $J=t I+O(t^3)$, $B_J(r,0)=J(r)$ and $J^TN=DI/2$. Substitution in $L(0)$ gives a scalar matrix using $(J^TN)'=D'I/2$. All endpoint radii at which $N$ is evaluated have a uniform positive lower bound.

The principal terms of $\delta R_{AkBk}$ differentiate the metric twice in directions drawn from $k,e_A,e_B$, all tangent to the outgoing null face. In a smooth sky chart, $k=\partial_t$. Each parallel screen direction has angular coefficients $t^{-1}$ times smooth functions and an affine coefficient bounded with a smooth extension to $t=0$. This follows by expanding the angular Jacobi vectors as $t$ times their initial screen axes plus $O(t^3)$. Write each pair of tangent ambient derivatives as two derivatives of pullbacks minus a first ambient derivative from differentiating the moving directions. The latter has coefficient at worst $O(t^{-1})$ before multiplication by $K_{\rm TF}$. Integrate the two tangent derivatives by parts in the parameter domain, split at $C_a(n)$ and the waist $S(n)$. The worst interior order-zero density is $O(1)dt\,dn$, since two angular directions cost at most $t^{-2}$, and affine differentiation can lower the order in $t$ by at most one each time. The remaining first-order density is bounded. Terms at the positive endpoint surfaces have order at most one with smooth coefficients; both the motion of the boundary as a function of angle and the jump at the cap enter those terms. At $t=0$ affine boundary terms before and after one integration vanish because their coefficients are respectively $O(t^2)$ and $O(t)$ (a single angular derivative costs at most $t^{-1}$). There is no new derivative of a point mass.

The other terms in the adjoint already integrate at most one derivative on the outgoing face and the timelike observer, and order zero on the return face. The endpoint displacements of the observer have the timelike geodesic adjoint representation, with an algebraic frame variation at launch. Thus $R_a$ consists of smooth order-zero point insertions, face densities bounded with respect to affine radius and angle even at either cone vertex, smooth cap and waist densities, and bounded smooth interval densities on the central observer. The integrated jets have order at most one. The densities on each separate parameter piece are smooth to its boundary and in $\lambda$. At flat space the reduced weights vanish before differentiating their embeddings: the matched-radius adjoint has $T=0$, $H=-1$, $B=1$, the tidal coefficient is pure trace and $Q=0$. Accordingly $R'_a(0)$ has the same order-one representation on the flat strata.

## Two remainder sources

Let $E_h$ denote the Einstein causal kernel smoothed in relative Killing time by the autocorrelation $\eta_h(s)=h^{-1}\eta(s/h)$ of $f$. On the common compact set, for mixed endpoint derivatives of total order $m\le 2$ and an auxiliary smooth Riemannian distance $d$,
$$
 |\partial^m E_h(x,y)|\le C\big[1+h^{-m-1}/(h+d(x,y))\big].                 \tag{1}
$$
For $d\lesssim h$ use the [timelike smoothing bound (4)](local-curved-bracket.md). For $d\gg h$ near the null front the retarded or advanced root is simple. In stationary coordinates the spatial distance $r$ between the two orbits is comparable to $d$ there, the world-function slope in the time variable has magnitude comparable to $r$, and the direct coefficient is $r^{-1}$ times a smooth function in spatial polar coordinates. Endpoint differentiation of the root is bounded, and differentiation of the amplitude or each further differentiation of the root costs at most one additional inverse power of $r$. The term with the $j$th derivative of the window is bounded by $C r^{-1-m+j} h^{-1-j}$ for $0\le j\le m$. This implies (1) since $r\gtrsim h$. Tail boundary terms involve fewer derivatives and the tail density stays bounded. Away from the diagonal use a finite cover and transversality to the timelike sweep. Away from the null front and its $O(h)$ neighborhood all required derivatives remain bounded. The direct and tail representations used here follow from the [plane-wave Hadamard kernel](weak-rosen-green.md).

For two nonpoint strata carrying order-one jets, the bound for $h^3\partial^2 E_h$ is integrable against the product measures except for two overlapping observer segments. Smooth surface and volume parameters away from the tips give locally finite $1/d$ integrals, also when two surfaces coincide. At a vertex a face with weight $dt\,dn$ has spatial polar parametrization bi-Lipschitz equivalent to $tn$ on the stationarity quotient for weak waves. In three flat spatial dimensions the angular integral of $1/|tn-t'n'|$ is bounded by $C/\max(t,t')$ after angular integration; smooth bounded changes of radial and angular variables preserve this estimate in the common spatial coordinates. The double radial integral is finite. Near a segment through the vertex, $d\gtrsim t+|t'-t_{\rm tip}|$ up to a fixed constant, whose reciprocal is integrable in the two parameters. Other separated pieces need no vertex estimate.

Almost every such pair is non-null. One verification uses analyticity of the unswept face, cut and observer embeddings and the world function. On every pair of strata with nonempty relative interiors its zero set has measure zero unless it vanishes identically on an open product. For every pair of face or round-cut types at flat space choose distinct spatial directions and affine positions whose points are not null related; for observer–face pairs choose an observer time away from either null root. Non-nullity of those particular interior labels persists at small strength. Analytic continuation in the connected parameter domains excludes an open product of zeros. The pieces from splitting at a cap have connected domains, and a partition of unity in sky charts does not enlarge the exceptional sets. Consequently bounded convergence in (1) gives zero after multiplying by $h^3$. For terms of total order less than two the same conclusion holds directly, including order-zero point insertions: for $m\le1$, $h^3\partial^m E_h$ is bounded and tends to zero off the diagonal, and no order-one measure has an atom. A pair of point masses has $m=0$.

On the central observer use stationary tensor and derivative frames. Each kernel with at most two endpoint derivatives restricts to a distribution of relative proper time, since the line is timelike. Away from coincident times the restricted distribution is a piecewise smooth bounded tail, with one-sided smooth limits at zero. The difference from this function is point supported, hence a finite linear combination of delta derivatives of order at most $m+1$. The order follows by testing at width $h$ against arbitrary smooth compact profiles in the $O(h^{-m-2})$ timelike smoothing bound. The cross-correlation of the two compact interval densities is Lipschitz; each extended-by-zero interval density is bounded and of bounded variation. Its convolution with the timing autocorrelation has $j$th derivative bounded by $O(h^{1-j})$ for $j\ge1$. Even the third derivative therefore contributes $O(h^{-2})$. This proves
$$
 R_a(\lambda) E_h R_b(\lambda)=o(h^{-3})
                                                                    \tag{2}
$$
at fixed small strength, and the same estimate for the two first strength derivatives of the individual reduced sources paired with the flat kernel.

## One insertion at the common launch

It remains to justify an expansion through $h^{-3}$ of the mixed terms with $P_\lambda$ and to control its dependence on strength. They involve endpoint derivatives of total order $m\le3$ on the integrated strata, and $m\le2$ at isolated points. Only the launch vertex of the outgoing face has coalescing direct roots. The following local calculation gives an expansion in integer powers of $h$ and single powers of $\log h$, termwise twice differentiable in $\lambda$ with remainder $o(h^{-3})$ also after two strength derivatives.

Use stationary coordinates $(T,X)$, with $X=0$ the central orbit, near launch. For a point of the outgoing face the two small null delay roots relative to the launch orbit are exactly $0$ and a signed $\rho$, with $\rho/t$ smooth and strictly positive up to the tip. Indeed the face is the null cone of $p$, and rescaling the world function by $t^{-2}$ with delay $tz$ gives the two distinct simple flat tangent-cone roots in $z$ at $t=0$. Their separation remains positive uniformly in angle and small strength. Use $\rho$ as the local radial variable. At larger affine radius only the root at zero matters for sufficiently narrow compact windows.

Let $M$ exceed the radius of the support of $\eta$. On $0\le\rho\le Mh$ rescale all coordinates about the vertex and the lag by $h$, using the metric coefficients $g_\lambda(h\cdot)$. The coordinate causal kernel scales as $h^{-2}$ times that for the rescaled metric, with one further $h^{-1}$ per endpoint derivative. Smooth frame transformations add lower orders. At unit smearing width the latter kernels are smooth in the rescaled endpoints, $h$ and $\lambda$ on the compact rescaled domain, including $h=0$. This also follows directly by using the nondegenerate time maximum of the world function in the stationary lag. Smooth completion of the square gives $2\sigma=D^2-z^2$, with $D^2$ smooth and nonnegative near the spatial diagonal. The signed direct term integrates a difference $[A(D)-A(-D)]/D$ of smooth amplitudes, including the window and time Jacobian. This difference is smooth in $D^2$, even at $D=0$. The two smooth tail integrals remove an interval with signed contribution $\int_0^D[A(-z)-A(z)]dz$, also smooth in $D^2$. One can use a cutoff containing the roots, as away from them the integrands are smooth. The same construction includes spatial endpoint derivatives by differentiating the resulting smooth kernel. On this rescaled domain the endpoints, integration densities and their parameter derivatives are smooth in $(\rho/h,h,\lambda,n)$. Taylor expansion in $h$ on a fixed compact integration domain therefore gives ordinary powers starting no lower than $h^{-m-1}$ after the radial integration.

For $Mh\le\rho$ in the vertex chart, the opposite branch lies outside the window. A direct root contributes a finite sum of terms $h^{-1-j}\eta^{(j)}(0)$ times smooth amplitudes times integer powers of $\rho$ bounded below by $-1-m+j$, by the same polar differentiation used in (1). Each integral in $\rho$ from $Mh$ to a fixed small radius has a Taylor expansion of its smooth amplitude at zero; the exponent $-1$ yields a single logarithm. The Taylor remainder after a sufficiently high finite order has a twice strength-differentiable integral down to zero. The upper endpoint, including cutoff terms, supplies smooth strength-dependent coefficients. For the tail, ordinary interior integrals have bounded smooth amplitudes and bounded window mass. After pinning the outgoing root or straightening the waist root, up to two strength derivatives preserve these bounds because the integration boundary in lag is then fixed. Endpoint differentiation of the step contributes root jets of lower order with the same polar expansion. This proves the claim on the outgoing face without separately extending either singular direct branch across $\rho=0$.

Each positive-radius outgoing cut or waist lies on the pinned simple root and stays away from the launch; the same direct evaluation applies with smooth coefficients there. On the return face the launch front meets only the waist. Interior return generators from the future tip are timelike related to $p$, and the generators meet the outgoing face transversely at the waist. The signed small root is therefore a smooth transverse coordinate to that boundary, with fixed sign on its interior and uniform nonvanishing derivative there. Integrating the smooth root coefficient against $h^{-1-j}\eta^{(j)}(s/h)$ on the corresponding half interval gives ordinary powers with smooth coefficients by $s=hz$. The far return tip is timelike related to launch. For the observer segment and any point at launch, use the previous restriction to the stationary orbit. The coefficients of its point-supported derivatives are smooth in strength: equivalently, the same scaling of the kernel about the coincident point gives Taylor expansions of its smooth profile for any test window, distinguishing the finite set of delta jets by their moments against test functions with prescribed derivatives at zero. Pairing these jets with a single interval density smooth to each end, or with a point, produces only ordinary powers. Tails are bounded. This accounts for both tips, all cut boundaries and the observer's endpoint and frame variations.

Combining the mixed terms gives a finite sum $\sum_{d\le-3}h^d[A_d(\lambda)+B_d(\lambda)\log h]+o(h^{-3})$, with smooth coefficients at zero, through the displayed order. The same expansion for the mixed terms may be differentiated twice in strength by the fixed-domain estimates above. Equation (2) handles the remaining term at each fixed strength. To identify $A_{-3}$ at the first nonzero curvature order, use that all reduced sources vanish at zero. At order $\lambda^2$ the two first-variation sources pair with the flat propagator; differentiating moving supports therefore does not raise their orders. Their remainder–remainder product is $o(h^{-3})$ by (2). The [weak-wave calculation, Eq. (1)](synchronized-contact.md) now fixes
$$
 A_{-3}(\lambda)=\lambda^2\frac{16}{5}G_N w_0(c_a u_b-c_b u_a)\eta''(0)
                    +o(\lambda^2),
$$
where $c_a=-R^5(1-5a^4+4a^5)/225$ and $u_a=R^2(1-a)^2/6$. The constant and linear terms vanish because both mixed-source factors vanish at zero. Uniqueness of finite power-log expansions identifies this coefficient even if terms with other exponents or a logarithm occur at higher orders in strength. The displayed multiplier is negative for every $a<b$. Thus $A_{-3}$ is nonzero throughout a sufficiently small punctured positive strength interval. For each such fixed strength the first nonzero term in the power-log ordering diverges in magnitude. This establishes the conclusion without interchanging the two raw limits.
