# 5.2. Finite dimension, area spectrum and the AdS thermal trace

**The physical question.** *Does imposing an upper bound on a causal diamond's geometric clock force its quantum state space to be finite-dimensional—and does that yield a discrete horizon area and a finite Anti-de Sitter thermal trace?*

**Verdict.** Three ingredients of the argument hold up: the geometric upper bound on corner area, the boost-sensitive two-point function in the linearized vacuum, and the concentration estimate on finite basis cutoffs. What the proof does *not* establish is the bridge to a finite dimension. Its contradiction requires every exhaustive sequence of cut projections to reproduce the same unnormalized **closed** amplitude, including a subtracted identity insertion, whereas §4.4 only proves convergence for **open** matrix elements between fixed finite-norm vectors. Under the paper's lower bound on each state's weight, an independently established finite norm for the reflected cap would itself imply finite dimension. The area-spectrum and AdS thermal-trace conclusions hold only under their respective additional hypotheses. [§4.4, eq. (4.29)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=32) · [§5.2, pp. 38–45](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=38)

## Area, boost and multiplicity

In the nonzero allowed space write $s=\widehat{\mathscr A}/(4G_N\hbar)$. Assume the clock exists as a positive self-adjoint operator and strongly commutes with total area. In (4.27), dropping $e^{\widehat\Xi}\ge I$ gives $\bar u_*\ge (1-\epsilon)\mathscr A/\widehat{\mathsf a}$ on each fixed-area sector. Cauchy–Schwarz applied to $\int_S\widehat\varrho_S(y)\psi\,d\Omega$ gives $\widehat{\mathsf a}^{\,2}\le4\pi\int_S\widehat\varrho_S(y)^2d\Omega=4\pi\mathscr A I$. Thus (5.21) gives $0\le s\le s_*=\pi\ell^2/[G_N\hbar(1-\epsilon)^2]$. This bounds area values while leaving their multiplicities open. Continuing $\eta$ to $i\pi$ in the boost group gives the half-weight $e^{-s/2}$. Identifying the corresponding geometry with $Ue^{-s/2}P_{\mathscr D}$ is the physical cap prescription (5.23). [Clock form (4.27)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=31) · [Eqs. (5.21)–(5.25)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=39)

With that prescription, $W=C_b^\dagger C_b=f_b(s)e^{-s}$ on $\mathcal H_{\mathscr D}$. The Gaussian $f_b$ has a positive minimum on $[0,s_*]$. In particular, for $c=e^{-s_*}\min f_b>0$ and every finite-rank projection $P$,

$$
cI\le W\le I,\qquad c\,\mathrm{rank}\,P\le\mathrm{Tr}(PWP)\le\mathrm{rank}\,P .
$$

Consequently, finite dimension, compactness of $W$, finiteness of $\mathrm{Tr}\,W$, and the Hilbert–Schmidt property of $C_b$ are all equivalent here. For compactness, use $I=W^{-1}W$ and boundedness of $W^{-1}$. A tiny positive $c$ suffices. [Eq. (5.30)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=41)

## The two boost calculations

For $u\ne u'$ the regulated flat-space integral is $\int_0^\infty\omega e^{-\tau\omega}\cos(\omega x)\,d\omega=(\tau^2-x^2)/(\tau^2+x^2)^2$, where $x=u-u'$. Its limit yields $-1/x^2$. At $(-d,d)$ a right-handed boost multiplies this correlator by $4e^\eta/(1+e^\eta)^2=\mathrm{sech}^2(\eta/2)$. A product of sines of commuting Gaussian variables has expectation $e^{-(j_L^2G_{LL}+j_R^2G_{RR})/2}\sinh(j_Lj_RG_{LR})$. The resulting nonzero change is a valid linearized-vacuum signal. The transfer to the exact $C_b$ requires control of its local state. In (D.36) the bound on $\langle:\!\sigma_\perp^2\!:\rangle_{C_b}$ comes from the smooth-background calculation. Area weighting on its own constrains neither excitations within a given area nor the total weight of other saddles. The determinant cancels in the one-loop **ratio** (5.28a), leaving the absolute normalization of $Z(0,0)$ undetermined. [Eqs. (5.28)–(5.29)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=40) · [Eqs. (D.15)–(D.21)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=71) · [Eq. (D.36)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=73)

On a finite-rank cut the vector $\Psi_P$ is the normalized sum of $C_b e_i\otimes\bar e_i$ over a basis of $P\mathcal H$. Removing a scalar phase, using $|e^{ix}-1|\le|x|$, and bounding the difference of expectations of a bounded observable $O$ gives

$$
|\Delta\langle O\rangle_P|\le\frac{|\eta|}{\pi}\|O\|
\left(\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_P(s-s_0)^2\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
\rho_P=PWP/\mathrm{Tr}(PWP).
$$

If the dimension is infinite, a finite partition of $[0,s_*]$ has a band $I$ of width $\Delta s$ with the full Hilbert cardinality. Pair one basis vector from outside $I$ with $r-1$ from inside, grouping unused inside vectors into cycles of size $r$. The union of finite cycles exhausts the basis. For its projection $P$, the weight outside $I$ is at most $1/(cr)$, so the squared spread is bounded by $(\Delta s)^2+s_*^2/(cr)$. This reproduces (D.25) and works for continuous area spectra as well. The cycle construction correctly makes the normalized response arbitrarily small. [Eqs. (D.22)–(D.25)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=72)

## Where the contradiction needs an extra hypothesis

For fixed Hilbert vectors, (4.29) is the identity $\langle v,Pu\rangle\to\langle v,u\rangle$ as $P\to I$ strongly. Its absolute sum is bounded by $\|u\|\|v\|$. The squared norm of the **unnormalized** vector on the cut in (D.22) is $\mathrm{Tr}(PWP)$. In infinite dimension this is at least $c\,\mathrm{rank}\,P\to\infty$. The purported full vector $\sum_i C_b e_i\otimes\bar e_i$ then has no Hilbert norm, so it cannot supply the fixed vectors needed to apply (4.29). The term $-\delta_\eta I/2$ in (5.37) explicitly retains this divergent normalization. Diffeomorphisms can identify the classical descriptions of a cut and uncut region, while a separate analytic condition decides whether the resulting closed functional is finite and admits this basis exhaustion. [Fig. 19 and eqs. (4.29)–(4.30)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=32) · [Sign contradiction (5.37)–(5.38)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=43)

There are two precise conditional conclusions. A finite unnormalized reflected cap with the stated Schmidt weights immediately gives $c\dim\mathcal H_{\mathscr D}\le\|C_b\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$. Alternatively, an independent, positive unnormalized $Z[X]$ for this particular insertion, together with a proof of agreement for every exhaustive cycle ordering, would give the paper's sign contradiction. A convergence theorem for fixed external wave packets and a finite normalized correlator do not establish either input. Appendix E.1 explicitly imposes finiteness of compatible smoothed closures as an axiom. On bounded boost spectrum one can choose the smearing equal to one throughout that spectrum. The separate issue in §5.2 is verifying that axiom in the proposed theory. A related Euclidean construction also states finiteness among its hypotheses. [Appendix E.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=77) · [Colafranceschi, Dong, Marolf and Wang](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP10%282024%29063)

### Explicit test of the cutoff dependence

Take $\mathcal H=\mathbb C^2\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ with area values $1,1+2\pi$. Choosing $s_b=2+\pi$ and $\sigma_s=1$ in the paper's Gaussian makes $W=wI$ with the same positive weight on both levels. Choose $C_b=\sqrt w I$. Let $O$ flip the two area labels on each side of the doubled space, keeping the copy label fixed. Cut off the lower band at $kN$ copies and the upper band at $N$. For each fixed positive integer $k$, these projections tend strongly to $I$. The simultaneous flip has $N$ matched pairs, and the boost at $\eta=1$ gives their two terms opposite phases. The normalized boost contrast is exactly

$$
\langle O_1-O_0\rangle_{P_N}=\frac{2(\cos 1-1)}{k+1},\qquad
\mathrm{Tr}(P_NWP_N)=w(k+1)N .
$$

A direct evaluation of the finite doubled vectors at $N=7$ gave $-0.4596976941$ for $k=1$, $-0.1838790777$ for $k=4$, and $-0.0224242778$ for $k=40$. The exact expression holds for every $N$. Thus a finite boost contrast in balanced truncations and suppression in the cycle truncations coexist with ordinary open sewing. This model calculation tests the logical inference from strong convergence; it leaves open the physical question of which closed amplitudes exist in gravity. ## Further tests and consequences

- **A phase test within the corner variables.** At fixed angular embedding, shifting only the frame variable $\Gamma(y)$ by $\lambda(y)$ contracts (3.42b) to $-\delta\int_S\lambda(y)\mathscr K(y)\,d\Omega$. The normalized classical clock (3.27) depends on $\varrho_S$ and the radiative profile and is invariant under that shift. Thus the smeared local charge classically Poisson-commutes with this clock in the displayed parametrization, even for nonconstant $\lambda$. If its quantum counterpart $K_\lambda$ strongly commutes with $P_{\mathscr D}$ and has a diffuse distribution $F$ in a nonzero normalized allowed state $\psi$, the vectors $e^{2\pi i n F(K_\lambda)}\psi$ are orthonormal and remain allowed. This follows by integrating $e^{2\pi i(n-m)t}$ over $0<t<1$, exactly as in the JT example. Diffuseness occurs in the simple density-coordinate realization $f_S=q\lambda+f_0$ at fixed $f_0$ with Lebesgue measure in $q$. In (4.23), $\partial_q\int_S\lambda\,dA=\mathscr A\,\mathrm{Var}_{dA/\mathscr A}(\lambda)>0$. Deciding whether both quantum premises hold requires the actual representation of the coupled corner variables in the projected space. The example singles out a possible degeneracy for a constructive calculation. [Corner symplectic form (3.42)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=19) · [Density (4.23) and clock (4.27)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=31)
- **Area eigenvalues.** If finite dimension holds, a self-adjoint area has at most $\dim\mathcal H_{\mathscr D}$ distinct eigenvalues, and (5.39) is the ordinary Gibbs normalization. A nonzero exact boost contrast then forces some right insertion to have a nonzero matrix element between distinct area eigenspaces. Neither the eigenvalues nor a universal gap are computed. [Eqs. (5.39)–(5.41)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=43)
- **Replica check.** For $W_P=PWP$, $R_P(2)=\mathrm{Tr}(W_P^2)/(\mathrm{Tr}\,W_P)^2\le1/\mathrm{Tr}\,W_P$. A uniform positive return ratio for the actual Hilbert traces would suffice. (D.48)–(D.49) instead integrate weights over a regular one-loop family with measure $ds$ and then use Cauchy–Schwarz. To see the distinction, on $L^2[0,1]$ multiplication by a bounded continuous function bounded below by $c>0$ has infinite Hilbert trace. The corresponding integrals of the function and its square against $ds$ are both finite, and their ratio is positive. The missing step identifies the saddle integrals with the limit of finite-rank Hilbert traces including all sectors. [Appendix D.3, eqs. (D.47)–(D.49)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=75)
- **AdS thermal trace.** For the disjoint diamond and cylinder, each fully summed diamond row in (5.44) vanishes because the centered band insertion has mean zero. If some positive and negative coefficients occur and one requires convergence of the net over all finite sets of pairs, the series is absolutely convergent. Equivalently, the sum of absolute terms is $2p(1-p)\sum_iw_i$, with $0<p<1$. Thus that requirement already forces $\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-\beta(H-E_0)}<\infty$. For example $H=E_0I$ on $\ell^2$ admits every complete-row cancellation while its thermal trace diverges. Once trace class holds, the finite-rank estimate (5.46), a finite-multiplicity ground state, the minimal-projection argument on its cyclic sector, and the usual normal Gibbs state follow. Absolute convergence of the joint closed trace is the outstanding physical input. [Eqs. (5.42)–(5.46)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=44) · [Eqs. (5.47)–(5.49)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=45)
