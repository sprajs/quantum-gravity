# Appendix D.3 — Focusing and the number of graviton states

**Physical question.** Does null focusing along a causal diamond's incoming cone impose an effective energy budget tight enough to limit the number of graviton states to $\log d_Q\lesssim 1+(sQ_*)^{3/4}$, and do the replica-ratio and angular-patch bounds independently establish finite Hilbert-space dimension?

**Outcome.** For a round-background null collar with fixed boundary data and an assumed uniform quantum budget $Q_*$ on the quadratic radiative cost (D.40), both the symplectic compactness estimate and the occupation-count exponent $3/4$ in (D.46c) reproduce cleanly. The missing physical link is showing that the incoming clock projector of (5.1)–(5.2) actually enforces such a budget. Because the incoming clock contains no angular derivatives, explicit smooth incoming profiles of arbitrarily high azimuthal frequency $m$ yield the *exact same* averaged clock while their transverse focusing cost grows as $m^2$; similarly, endpoint-localized radial waves change the clock by an arbitrarily small amount while their radial cost diverges logarithmically. Nor do the replica ratio (D.48) (which integrates over a continuous saddle label rather than taking a Hilbert-space trace) or the small-patch symplectic bound (D.55) close this gap. Appendix D.3 therefore provides a valid conditional one-loop check on a budget-restricted radiative sector rather than an unconditional count of clock-selected diamond states. [Manuscript, pp. 73–76](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=73) · [TeX source archive](https://arxiv.org/src/2611.34924v1)

## Cross focusing and the quadratic cost, (D.37)–(D.41)

On any smooth closed spherical cut $C_u$, Gauss–Bonnet gives $\int R[q]\,dA=4\pi\chi(S^2)=8\pi$, the total divergence in (3.57d) integrates to zero, and $\mathcal L_\ell dA=\Theta_{(\ell)}dA$. In an affine null foliation, this yields

$$
\partial_u\int\Theta_{(n)}dA=-4\pi+\int |\varpi|^2dA+\Lambda A(C_u).
$$

On a round cut, the cosmological term has relative size $|\Lambda|r^2$, and the vacuum Einstein equations with cosmological constant still give $R_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b=0$ identically. Equation (D.38) holds on every smooth genus-zero affine cut, round or nonround.

To recover the radial derivative term in (D.40), track the rescaling to luminosity normals: put $r=Rv$ ($R=\varrho_S$), area form $r^2d\Omega$, null tangent $k=\partial_r$, and transverse normal $k\cdot n=-1$ orthogonal to the cuts. Here $\theta_k=2/r$, so the nonaffine Raychaudhuri equation $\partial_r\theta_k=\kappa\theta_k-\theta_k^2/2-|\sigma_k|^2$ fixes $\kappa=r|\sigma_k|^2/2$. In these normals the integrated transverse expansion $M(r)=\int\theta_n\,dA$ acquires the extra term $-\int\kappa\theta_n\,dA$. Expanding around a flat round cone ($\theta_n^{(0)}=-1/r$, $\varpi^{(0)}=0$, $|\sigma_k|^2=|\partial_r h|^2/4+O(h^3)$) and integrating for smooth perturbations at $\Lambda=0$ gives

$$
\frac{M(R)-M(\epsilon R)+4\pi R(1-\epsilon)}{R}
=\int_\epsilon^1dv\int d\Omega\left(
 |\varpi^{(1)}|^2_{S^2}+\frac{v^2}{8}|\partial_v h|^2_{S^2}\right)+O(h^3).
$$

Because $M(R)$ and $M(\epsilon R)$ are evaluated in luminosity normals, varying the cuts or affine normalization changes those boundary terms. This derivation identifies exactly which boundary data control the quadratic radiative cost: fixing both luminosity endpoint expansions to their flat values forces the quadratic budget to zero, whereas fixing only the cut areas leaves the transverse expansions unconstrained. Related classical monotonicity theorems similarly specify both the null foliation and the signs of the expansions. [Hayward, *Quasi-Localisation of Bondi-Sachs Energy Loss*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9405071)

Expanding in normalized spin-two tensor harmonics of angular momentum $j\ge 2$, the divergence norm is $t_j=\sqrt{(j-1)(j+2)/2}$. (For the even-parity harmonic constructed from the trace-free Hessian of a unit scalar harmonic with $L=j(j+1)$, the squared norm is $L(L-2)/2$ and the divergence is $-(L-2)D_A Y/2$, giving $t_j^2=(L-2)/2$; the odd harmonic follows by contracting with the sphere volume form.) Since the round background expansion has no angular gradient at leading order, (3.57c) reproduces (D.39) up to the sign convention for the normalized vector harmonic. When the cap connection variation vanishes and $h_I(\epsilon)=0$, integrating yields

$$
\varpi_I(v)=-\frac{t_j}{2v^2}\int_\epsilon^v w^2 h'_I(w)\,dw
=-\frac{t_j}{2}\left[h_I(v)-\frac{2}{v^2}\int_\epsilon^v w h_I(w)\,dw\right].
$$

For an oscillatory radial wave packet of frequency $k$, the final integral is suppressed by $1/k$. Since the symplectic area of two quadratures scales as $(R^2/G_N)|k||h|^2$, one quantum of symplectic area $\hbar$ carries cost scales $|k|/s$ and $j(j+1)/(s|k|)$ in the two terms of (D.41), where $s=\pi R^2/(G_N\hbar)$.

## What the incoming clock controls

Does bounding the incoming clock $\bar u_*$ actually bound the quadratic cost $Q$ above? Two explicit classical families show that it does not.

**High-frequency azimuthal waves at fixed clock.** Choose a smooth axisymmetric envelope $\chi(\theta)$ supported away from the poles and a smooth radial profile $f(v)$ vanishing at $v=\epsilon$ and $v=1$. In the unit-sphere orthonormal frame, take the unit-determinant diagonal metric with entries $e^{z_m},e^{-z_m}$, where $z_m=a f(v)\chi(\theta)\cos(m\phi)$ for any integer $m\ge 1$ (agreeing with the round metric outside the envelope). Evaluating (3.26c) directly gives the exact focusing functional

$$
\Xi_m(v,\theta,\phi)=\frac{a^2}{4}\chi(\theta)^2\cos^2(m\phi)
   \int_\epsilon^v w|f'(w)|^2\,dw .
$$

Because integrating $\exp\Xi_m$ over $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$ covers an integer number of periods, the azimuthal average is strictly independent of $m$. At fixed round corner area, every member of this infinite sequence produces **exactly the same clock reading**, including all nonlinear powers of shear inside the exponential. For any strict clock margin above $(1-\epsilon)R$, a single nonzero amplitude $a$ fits inside the margin for all $m$. Moreover, radiative perturbations with different $m$ are orthogonal in angle, and for two distinct radial quadratures in a fixed-$m$ channel, the linearized symplectic pairing is independent of $m$ because $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2(m\phi)\,d\phi=\pi$.

In sharp contrast, the angular divergence of $h$ contains a term proportional to $m\sin(m\phi)$, so the transverse cost $\int|\varpi^{(1)}|^2$ grows as $a^2 m^2$ at fixed radial profile (and by choosing $\int_\epsilon^1 v f(v)\,dv=0$, one can even make the linearized connection vanish at both endpoints). As a numerical cross-check at a latitude with $\chi=1$, taking $\epsilon=1/4$, $a=0.12$, and $f=\sin^2(\pi(v-\epsilon)/(1-\epsilon))$ on a $240$-interval radial and $512$-point azimuthal quadrature gives an identical azimuthally averaged dimensionless clock of $0.7523135366$ for $m=1,3,11,31$, while the azimuthal average of $|\partial_\phi\cos(m\phi)|^2$ climbs through $0.5$, $4.5$, $60.5$, and $480.5$.

**Endpoint-localized radial waves.** A second test breaks control of the radial cost. Using the same diagonal metric with a fixed bounded angular profile and $z=a_\delta F((1-v)/\delta)$ for $F\in C_c^\infty((0,1))$, choose $a_\delta^2\propto\delta\log(1/\delta)$ so that $\sup\Xi(1)\le c\log(1/\delta)$ for a fixed $0<c<1$. Because the perturbation acts only within width $\delta$ of $v=1$, its change to the exact clock divided by $R$ is at most $\delta^{1-c}\to 0$, yet the radial cost $\int v^2|z'|^2\,dv$ grows without bound as $\log(1/\delta)$ while the final affine slope stays strictly positive. Bounding the unweighted radial cost thus also requires more than the incoming clock margin.

**Scope of these tests.** These families test the incoming clock functional against the quadratic radiative cost; the large-$m$ sequence has no uniform curvature bound, and these classical data do not by themselves construct infinitely many quantum states of a complete causal diamond. Excluding them via a second regular null sheet, prescribed transverse expansion, or cap compatibility requires an estimate that brings in that extra condition—whereas (5.1)–(5.2) project solely with $\mathbf 1_{[0,\ell]}(\widehat{\bar u}_*)$. The text after (D.46) gives no derivation of the claimed uniform bound $Q_*\lesssim_\epsilon 1+[\ell/R-(1-\epsilon)]^{1/3}$ when the extra boundary data are free to vary. Even if established, that bound would give $sQ_*=O(R^{5/3})$ as $R\to 0$ at fixed $\ell$, and extending a one-loop count down to $R\sim\ell_P$ would still require independent error control.

## Conditional quantum counting, (D.42)–(D.46)

Given a quadratic budget $Q$, the symplectic compactness and Bose counting argument is rigorous. Integrating (D.39) by parts against $g$ with vanishing endpoints gives $\int v^2 h'g=(2/t_j)\int v^2\varpi[h]g'$; applying Cauchy–Schwarz in the $Q$-norm and antisymmetrizing yields (D.42c). Substituting $x=vh$ and $y=vg$ cancels the undifferentiated terms in the antisymmetric integrand and satisfies $\int |x'|^2=\int v^2|h'|^2+[v|h|^2]_\epsilon^1=\int v^2|h'|^2$, so the Dirichlet sine spectrum on length $1-\epsilon$ gives (D.44).

This lifts cleanly to a spectral compactness proof. Represent $\Omega(h,g)$ by a bounded skew-adjoint operator $K$ on the real Hilbert completion with norm $\sqrt Q$. Removing the first $J$ sine modes of $vh$ for all $j\le J$ removes at most $r_J=2J[(J+1)^2-4]$ real directions; on the orthogonal complement (projection $P$), different angular sectors decouple and the radial ($j\le J$) and angular ($j>J$) bounds give $\|PKP\|=O(R^2/(G_N J))$. Since $K-PKP$ has rank at most $2r_J$, $K$ is compact with at most $O(J^3)$ singular values exceeding a constant times $R^2/(G_N J)$.

In a positive normal-mode quantization of this quadratic cost, each oscillator block of symplectic singular value $\lambda_i$ carries excitation cost proportional to $\hbar/\lambda_i$, recovering $N(\nu)\le C_\epsilon(s\nu)^3$ with a spectral gap of order $1/s$. Bounding the number of Fock states below a hard cutoff $Q_*$ on the normal-ordered sum $H_Q=\sum_i\nu_i n_i$ via the Bose partition function gives

$$
\log d_Q(Q_*)\le \beta Q_*+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1m\int_0^\infty e^{-m\beta t}\,dN(t)
\le \beta Q_*+6C_\epsilon\zeta(4)(s/\beta)^3 .
$$

Optimizing over $\beta>0$ recovers $\log d_Q\lesssim_\epsilon 1+(sQ_*)^{3/4}$. Turning this conditional count into a theorem for the physical subspace requires two extra inputs: identifying this diagonal normal-ordered oscillator space with the paper's continuum Gaussian representation (including vacuum subtraction), and proving an operator budget across *every* physical state. At fixed area, a uniform form inequality $P_{\mathscr D}H_QP_{\mathscr D}\le Q_*P_{\mathscr D}$ would suffice by the min–max principle even if $H_Q$ does not commute with $P_{\mathscr D}$; a classical bound for fixed endpoint data or an expectation value in one semiclassical state does not supply that operator inequality.

The quantum energy inequality (D.28) provides a useful consistency check on the vacuum subtraction. For a single free, transversely smeared channel with spectral density $c_f\xi$, the vacuum term is

$$
\frac{c_f}{\pi}\int_0^\infty dk\int_0^\infty d\xi\,\xi|\widetilde g(k+\xi)|^2
=\frac{c_f}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty dp\,p^2|\widetilde g(p)|^2
=\frac{c_f}{2}\|g'\|_2^2 .
$$

Positivity of the smeared square bounds the normal-ordered integral below by $-c_f\|g'\|_2^2/2$, and Cauchy–Schwarz for the windowed positive-frequency mode gives $4\langle a_h^\dagger a_h\rangle\le \|h\|_2^2 E_g$, confirming the coefficient in (D.34c). However, that is a *lower* bound for a single smeared polarization in the free-commutator regime—it yields no *upper* budget on a sum over all angular harmonics. Moving from the positive bare squares in the clock to normal-ordered excitation costs requires a continuum prescription controlling the vacuum terms; rigorous chiral quantum energy inequalities explicitly specify both smearing and representation assumptions. [Fewster and Hollands, *Quantum Energy Inequalities in two-dimensional conformal field theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0412028)

## Replica comparison and angular patches, (D.47)–(D.55)

**Replica ratio.** For a finite-rank compression $A=P C^\dagger C P$ of a positive contraction, $\operatorname{Tr}A^n\le\operatorname{Tr}A$ for every $n>1$, so $\operatorname{Tr}A^n/(\operatorname{Tr}A)^n\le (\operatorname{Tr}A)^{1-n}$. A strictly positive limit of these actual finite-rank ratios over an exhausting net therefore forces $\operatorname{Tr}(C^\dagger C)<\infty$, confirming (D.47). In the saddle-point formula (D.48), Cauchy–Schwarz gives $(\int\sum k_a)^2\le (\int e^s d(s)\,ds)(\int e^{-s}\sum k_a^2\,ds)$, proving (D.49) whenever the first factor is finite (e.g., if $d(s)$ is uniformly bounded on a compact interval) and the denominator is nonzero.

What (D.48) misses is the distinction between a continuous integral over a saddle label and a Hilbert-space trace. Set $0\le s\le 1$ with $d(s)=k_1(s)=1$: the saddle-integral ratio (D.48) equals $1-e^{-1}>0$, whereas for the identity operator on the direct integral $L^2([0,1],ds)$, every rank-$N$ projection compression has ratio $\operatorname{Tr}A^2/(\operatorname{Tr}A)^2=1/N\to 0$. Applying (D.47) to (D.48) therefore requires an explicit physical measure and trace identification with uniform control as the cut is exhausted; the disk action factor alone supplies neither.

**Localized angular patches.** Classically, writing $a_0=\int\varrho_S\epsilon_0$, the clock bound enforces $\int dA\int e^{\Xi}\,dv\le a_0\ell\le (1-\epsilon)A_*$, matching (D.50). In (D.52), applying Cauchy–Schwarz to $\sqrt{\Xi'e^{-t\Xi}}\cdot e^{t\Xi/2}/\sqrt v$ uses $\int\Xi'e^{-t\Xi}\,dv=(1-e^{-t\Xi(1)})/t$, and Hölder's inequality bounds $\int_{P\times[\epsilon,1]} e^{t\Xi}\le [(1-\epsilon)A_P]^{1-t}[(1-\epsilon)A_*]^t$; choosing $t=[1+\log(A_*/A_P)]^{-1}$ reproduces (D.52b). Because the conformal-metric target space carries the hyperbolic metric $dz_1^2+e^{2z_1}dz_2^2$, geodesic distance convexity bounds the interpolated radial speed, and convexity of the square and exponential yields (D.54). Integrating the radiative symplectic potential around four such edges reproduces the $A_P[1+\log(A_*/A_P)]/G_N$ bound in (D.55). This vanishes as the patch area $A_P\to 0$ and genuinely controls symplectic area for loops localized on shrinking patches, but fixed-annulus high-$m$ waves (like the azimuthal sequence constructed above) do not shrink their support as their wavelength decreases—so the patch bound imposes no angular-mode cutoff on them.
