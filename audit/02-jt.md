# Section 2 — A prologue in JT gravity

**Physical question.** Does putting a strict upper bound on a geometric clock automatically leave a spacetime region with only finitely many quantum states? Section 2 tests that idea in two-dimensional Jackiw–Teitelboim (JT) gravity with a free scalar field—a clean control case with no gravitational shear.

**Outcome.** No: the fixed-corner-charge JT model genuinely admits infinitely many mutually orthogonal states sharing the exact same clock distribution, even with the matter state held fixed and for an arbitrarily small positive clock ceiling. Because the clock stays a continuous coordinate with an unconstrained conjugate momentum, phase oscillations in the clock wavefunction alone generate an infinite orthogonal family. The type $\mathrm{I}_\infty$ factor and divergent-trace conclusions follow for the specified observable algebra, Gaussian representation, and gluing prescription. Two qualifications apply: the unconstrained clock momentum relies on freedom to vary the cap dilaton (and (2.2) leaves the boundary action $I_{\partial\mathscr D}$ symbolic), while the dilation operator in (2.15) is self-adjoint on the full ambient clock half-line rather than intrinsically on the bounded interval $(0,\ell)$.

## Geometry and flux, equations (2.1)–(2.5)

The first check is purely classical: how the null clock $u_*$ relates to the diamond width $w$, and how positive matter flux alters the dilaton without changing the constant-curvature geometry.

Let $L=L_{\rm AdS}$. The embedding prescription in (2.1) gives $(f^{-1}\circ X)^*f^*g=X^*g$ by composition of pullbacks. Varying $Y_\mu$ and $\Phi$ in (2.2) imposes vanishing torsion and $d\omega^{01}=-L^{-2}\epsilon_g$. For $e^0=L\sec\sigma\,d\tau$ and $e^1=L\sec\sigma\,d\sigma$, the torsion-free connection is $\omega^{01}=\tan\sigma\,d\tau$. Thus $d\omega^{01}=-\sec^2\sigma\,d\tau\wedge d\sigma=(R/2)\epsilon_g$, giving $R=-2/L^2$ independently of the matter flux.

On the right null ray $d\tau=d\sigma$, the affine geodesic equation yields $d\sigma/du=C\cos^2\sigma$. Setting $d\lambda/du=1$ at the tip fixes $C=1/L$. Direct integration gives $\lambda=L\,{\rm arsinh}(\tan\sigma)$ and $u=L\tan\sigma$, so at the cap $d\lambda/du=1/\cosh(\lambda_c/L)$. Writing $x=w/(2L)$ and $a=\epsilon$, subtracting the cap reading and renormalizing the tangent yields

$$
f(x)=\frac{u_*}{L}=\frac{\sinh x-\sinh(ax)}{\cosh(ax)},\qquad
\cosh^2(ax)f'(x)=\cosh[(1-a)x]+(1-a)\sinh x\,\sinh(ax)-a .
$$

Because the right-hand side is at least $1-a>0$, the clock reading starts at zero, grows monotonically without bound, and reduces in flat space to $u_*=(1-a)w/2+O(w^3/L^2)$. For this cap prescription, clock reading and width therefore label the same positive coordinate.

Eliminating torsion gives the bulk gravitational action $\tfrac12\int\sqrt{-g}\,\Phi(R+2/L^2)$. Metric variation yields $g_{ab}\Box\Phi-\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi-g_{ab}\Phi/L^2=T_{ab}$. Along an affine null tangent $k^a$, this reduces to $\Phi''(u)=-(\varphi'(u))^2$. Two solutions with the same initial dilaton and slope therefore obey

$$
\Delta\Phi(u_*)=-\int_0^{u_*}(u_*-s)(\varphi'(s))^2\,ds .
$$

A localized positive pulse $T_{uu}=E\,\delta(u-u_0)$ lowers the final dilaton by $E(u_*-u_0)$ for $0<u_0<u_*$, while the constant-curvature equation leaves the metric and clock reading unchanged. This check uses the classical stress tensor.

## Clock momentum, equations (2.6)–(2.9)

Next we check whether fixing both corner dilaton charges and the entire matter profile still leaves a free classical momentum conjugate to the clock reading.

Integrating by parts in (2.2) gives the bulk symplectic potential $Y_\mu\delta e^\mu+\Phi\delta\omega^{01}-(\star d\varphi)\delta\varphi$. Pulling back $\star d\varphi$ produces opposite signs on the two outward rays; traversing the left ray inward and the right ray outward gives the same sign in both scalar integrals in (2.7). A relative corner boost supplies the conjugate pair $(\Gamma_i,\Phi(S_i))$ with the paper's oriented rapidities.

Subtracting two metric equations at the same matter configuration and taking the trace gives $\Box\Delta\Phi=2\Delta\Phi/L^2$, and hence $\nabla_a\nabla_b\Delta\Phi=g_{ab}\Delta\Phi/L^2$. Direct substitution confirms the three solutions $\cos\tau\sec\sigma$, $\sin\tau\sec\sigma$, and $\tan\sigma$. Their values and first derivatives at the origin are linearly independent; because the Hessian equation transports a solution and its gradient uniquely along any curve, these three exhaust the solutions on this patch. Requiring $\Delta\Phi$ to vanish at both corners $(0,\pm\sigma_S)$ for $w>0$ eliminates the first and third modes.

For the surviving mode $\Delta\Phi=c\sin\tau\sec\sigma$, the connection equation fixes

$$
\Delta Y_0=-\frac{c}{L}\sin\tau\tan\sigma,\qquad
\Delta Y_1=-\frac{c}{L}\cos\tau .
$$

Varying the pulled-back $e^0,e^1,\omega^{01}$ under an embedding displacement $(\delta\tau,\delta\sigma)$, the terms proportional to $d\delta\tau$ cancel between $\Delta Y_0\delta e^0$ and $\Delta\Phi\delta\omega^{01}$. The remainder is $-c\,d(\cos\tau\sec\sigma\,\delta\sigma)$. Integrating from the left corner to the right corner gives $-2c\sec\sigma_S\,\delta\sigma_S=-(c/L)\delta w$, matching the sign in (2.9d). Consequently,

$$
\Delta p_{u_*}=-\frac{c}{L}\frac{dw}{du_*}.
$$

At every $u_*>0$, $dw/du_*$ is finite and positive while $c$ is an unrestricted real parameter in the theory as formulated here. This directly exhibits an unconstrained classical momentum conjugate to $u_*$ even at fixed matter and fixed corner charges. Note the boundary condition behind this freedom: the variation changes the cap dilaton. Holding the full initial data on the cap fixed would specify a different boundary problem. In addition, while this difference calculation confirms the momentum direction, the full reduction in (2.7) also relies on the stated frame and field matching and the adopted boundary polarization, and equation (2.2) leaves the boundary action $I_{\partial\mathscr D}$ symbolic.

## Quantization and the interval, equations (2.10)–(2.15)

Once the clock is a continuous multiplication operator in the quantum theory, restricting its reading to $[0,\ell]$ leaves an infinite-dimensional subspace of clock wavefunctions.

For an interior test function $f$, $\delta j(f)=-\int f'\delta\varphi$. In the scalar part of $\Omega=\delta\Theta$, the Hamiltonian vector field $X_{j(f)}\varphi=f/2$ satisfies $\Omega(\delta,X_{j(f)})=\delta j(f)$. Acting on $j(f)$ with $X_{j(g)}$ yields $\{j(f),j(g)\}=\tfrac12\int f g'$, with vanishing cross-brackets between distinct rays. A constant shift of the matched scalar across both rays and the cap has momentum $\int_{\Sigma_0}\pi_\varphi\,dx+\sum_i[\varphi_i(1)-\varphi_i(\epsilon)]$, matching (2.11). In (2.12a), the product of the coefficients of $q_j$ and $i p_j$ is $1/(2\hbar)$, giving $[a_j,a_k^\dagger]=\delta_{jk}$. Finite-occupation vectors are dense in the chosen pure Gaussian Fock representation, which fixes a concrete representation of the infinitely many canonical pairs.

On the resulting tensor product, the spectral projector of the multiplication operator $u_*$ multiplies by the indicator function of $[0,\ell]$ for any $\ell>0$. Choose a normalized profile $h\in C_c^\infty(0,\ell)$, a fixed unit matter vector $\xi$, and the cumulative probability $F(u)=\int_0^u |h(t)|^2dt$. The states $h(u)e^{2\pi i nF(u)}\xi$ all have the same clock probability density $|h|^2$. Because $dF=|h|^2du$, their inner products reduce to $\int_0^1 e^{2\pi i(n-m)s}ds=\delta_{nm}$, even if $h$ has zeros or disconnected support. No energy bound is imposed on this family.

For the dilation operator $D=-i\hbar(u\partial_u+1/2)$, differentiating the phase reproduces $2\pi n\hbar\int u|h|^4du$ in (2.15) exactly. On the ambient half-line $L^2(\mathbb R_+,du)$, the unitary change of variables $y=\log u$, $\psi\mapsto e^{y/2}\psi(e^y)$ maps $D$ to $-i\hbar\partial_y$ on $\mathbb R$, which is self-adjoint. On the bounded clock space $(0,\ell)$, however, it becomes a derivative on $(-\infty,\log\ell)$, whose minimal symmetric realization has unequal deficiency indices, and the ambient dilation group carries states across the upper cutoff $\ell$. Thus (2.15) is a valid ambient measurement on these compactly supported packets, rather than a self-adjoint operator intrinsic to the bounded subspace. An intrinsic interval observable that produces the same expectation differences is $-i\hbar(b\partial_u+b'/2)$, where $b$ is a smooth real function of compact support in $(0,\ell)$ equal to $u$ on a neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp}h$; its flow is complete and acts unitarily inside $(0,\ell)$.

## Algebra, gluing and trace, equations (2.16)–(2.17)

Finally, we check the von Neumann algebra type of the bounded-clock region and why its reflected trace diverges under the paper's gluing rule.

In log-clock coordinates, any bounded operator commuting with all clock multiplication operators is itself a multiplication operator; commuting also with every translation forces that multiplier to be constant almost everywhere. By the double-commutant theorem, these generate all of $B(L^2(\mathbb R_+))$. Combining this with the irreducible matter Weyl algebra in the chosen pure Fock representation gives $B(\mathcal H_\Sigma)$ under weak closure. Compressing by $P_{\mathscr D}$ gives $B(P_{\mathscr D}\mathcal H_\Sigma)$, because every bounded operator on the range extends by zero on its orthogonal complement. Since the Fourier family shows that $P_{\mathscr D}\mathcal H_\Sigma$ is infinite dimensional, the full regional observable algebra at fixed charges is a type $\mathrm{I}_\infty$ factor.

When two regions are glued at a corner with opposite orientations, the corner term becomes $\Phi(S)\delta(\Gamma_R-\Gamma_L)$, so a common boost cancels. Equation (2.16) holds in the strong operator topology for any countable measurable partition of clock readings with $\sum_\alpha P_\alpha=1$. At finite fixed charges $K_L,K_R$, the specified analytically continued half-turn multiplies by $e^{-\pi(K_L+K_R)/\hbar}$. If the remaining evolution $U$ is an isometry on the prepared subspace ($U^\dagger U=1$), the reflected weight is $W=e^{-2\pi(K_L+K_R)/\hbar}1$. Summing diagonal matrix elements over any $N$ of the Fourier packets therefore grows linearly with $N$, confirming the claimed divergence. This calculation tests the consequences of the stated half-turn and isometric-evolution prescription; the divergence is specific to this reflected weight, and the rank of the four-dimensional clock projector (where gravitational shear enters) requires a separate argument.
