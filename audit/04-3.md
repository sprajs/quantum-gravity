# Section 4.3 and Appendix C.3 — Boundary observables and the gravitational clock

**Physical question.** Can the classical null-cone shear, normalized corner area density, and gravitational clock reading be quantized into positive self-adjoint operators with a dense domain—and do finite-mode boundary sources generate all bounded operators on the clock-filtered subspace?

**Outcome.** The conformal metric parametrization, the $1/4$ focusing coefficient, the bounded-source insertion formula, and the fixed-area rank-one operator construction all check out. Wherever the regulated shear factors share a common domain, the paper's square ordering gives a positive focusing form, and at positive fixed area the capped clock obeys an exact lower bound $\mathscr A\le 4\pi[\ell/(1-\epsilon)]^2$ on any nonzero fixed-area sector passing the clock cutoff. Two foundational continuum problems remain open: constructing the continuum shear-square operator in (4.22) (whose bare linearized vacuum expectation diverges quadratically, whereas Appendix D's Wick subtraction can lose positivity), and proving that the exponential clock's form domain is dense in the incoming Fock space so that removing the focusing cap does not collapse the physical subspace. Both gaps propagate directly into (4.27), the spectral projector (5.2), and the state count in §5; Appendix C.2 explicitly retains the projection onto the form-domain closure $M$ in (C.13).

**Scope.** Equations use the published PDF numbers. The derivations of (4.20)–(4.28) and (C.18)–(C.22) appear in [§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS3) and [C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS3), and the clock-domain argument appears in [C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p3.1). The corresponding printed pages are 30–31 and 65–68 of the [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1).

## Weyl operators and the focusing square, (4.20)–(4.22)

1. **Weyl phase.** In a Darboux chart with $[\hat\phi^I,\hat\phi^J]=i\hbar J^{IJ}I$, the exponent commutator is $[i\lambda_I\hat\phi^I,i\lambda'_J\hat\phi^J]=-i\hbar\lambda_IJ^{IJ}\lambda'_JI$. Because this commutator is central, Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff reproduces the minus sign and factor of $1/2$ in (4.20), and the mixed coefficient of a finite-dimensional displacement gives the symmetrized product on smooth vectors. In the infinite Gaussian tensor product, a centered linear smearing with mode coefficients $(c_j,d_j)$ has vacuum variance $\sum_j[c_j^2(\Delta q_j)^2+d_j^2\hbar^2/(4(\Delta q_j)^2)]$, which must be finite for the field to act on the vacuum. Countability of modes and the unrestricted positive widths in (4.7) supply no such estimate for a pointwise field.

2. **Conformal metric and focusing factor.** Set $x=z^1$, $t=z^2$, and $E=e^x$. The unit-determinant matrix $\bar q$ in (4.21a) satisfies $\det\bar q=E(E^{-1}+Et^2)-(Et)^2=1$, with inverse diagonal entries $E^{-1}+Et^2, E$ and off-diagonal entry $-Et$. Defining $M_i=\bar q^{-1}\partial_i\bar q$, direct matrix multiplication gives

   $$
   M_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&2t\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
   M_t=\begin{pmatrix}-E^2t&1-E^2t^2\\E^2&E^2t\end{pmatrix},\qquad
   \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(M_iM_j)=\operatorname{diag}(1,E^2)_{ij}.
   \tag{A1}
   $$

   Substituting into (3.26c) yields $\Xi_y(v)=\tfrac14\int_\epsilon^v s[(\partial_s x)^2+e^{2x}(\partial_s t)^2]\,ds$, confirming the $1/4$ coefficient in (4.22) for both polarizations. Classically, $du/dv=(\varrho_S/p_{\epsilon,y})e^{\Xi_y(v)}$, reproducing the affine-invariant clock reading (3.27).

3. **Operator ordering and domain.** Whenever $\hat z^1$ is self-adjoint and the displayed products are defined on $\psi$, the square ordering of (4.22) produces a manifestly nonnegative quadratic form:

   $$
   \tfrac14\int_\epsilon^v s
   \big(\|\partial_s\hat z^1\psi\|^2+
          \|e^{\hat z^1}\partial_s\hat z^2\psi\|^2\big)\,ds\ \ge 0 .
   \tag{A2}
   $$

   This is a nontrivial quantization choice: in one canonical pair, for instance, the Weyl symbol of $\hat p\,a(\hat q)\,\hat p$ is $p^2a(q)+\hbar^2a''(q)/4$ by (C.9), so square ordering fixes a particular quantum correction. Promoting (A2) to a positive self-adjoint operator requires a densely defined closed (or closable) form, including control over the noncommuting product $e^{\hat z^1}\partial_s\hat z^2$. Taking the spectral exponential $e^{\Xi_y(v)}$ then presupposes that $\Xi_y(v)$ itself has been constructed.

4. **Continuum ultraviolet test.** In the linearized, transversely smeared freely falling vacuum of (D.19), keeping the paper's frequency damping $\tau>0$ at coincident affine coordinates gives

   $$
   \langle\sigma_\perp(u)^2\rangle_\tau
       =c_\perp\int_0^\infty\omega e^{-\tau\omega}\,d\omega
       =c_\perp/\tau^2 .
   \tag{A3}
   $$

   Smearing this diagonal in $u$ against any fixed nonnegative test function of positive integral retains the $1/\tau^2$ divergence. While divergence of a vacuum expectation alone does not prove an empty form domain or settle the nonperturbative theory, it shows why the bare finite-mode square argument does not construct the continuum operator. Appendix D instead subtracts the vacuum singularity before taking coincidence and obtains a lower bound for a smooth smearing of the Wick square containing a negative derivative term in (D.35). Subtraction, however, need not preserve positivity: for a single oscillator, a squeezed state with variance below the vacuum gives $\langle\hat q^2-\langle0|\hat q^2|0\rangle I\rangle<0$. A continuum prescription bridging Appendix D's subtracted observable to the positive operator required in (4.22) is not provided. [Appendix D.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p2.7)

## Corner normalization and clock, (4.23)–(4.27)

Write $\mu=\boldsymbol\epsilon_0$ with $\mu(S)=4\pi$, and fix $\mathscr A>0$. On discrete angular cells of weights $\mu_i>0$, suppose $B=\sum_i\mu_i e^{\hat f_i}$ exists as a positive densely defined form sum with trivial kernel. Then for every $\psi\in D(B^{-1/2})$,

$$
\sum_i\mu_i\|e^{\hat f_i/2}B^{-1/2}\psi\|^2
=\|B^{1/2}B^{-1/2}\psi\|^2=\|\psi\|^2 .
\tag{A4}
$$

Each normalized summand therefore defines a bounded positive weight $w_i\le I/\mu_i$ summing to $\sum_i\mu_i w_i=I$. This proves (4.24)–(4.25) at the angular-cell regulator without assuming that the $\hat f_i$ commute. In the continuum, the same argument constructs *integrated* weights given a measurable field and densely defined form sum, whereas pointwise operator densities and their square roots in (4.24) still require construction. In the classical (scalar) limit, (4.23) is invariant under constant shifts $f_S\mapsto f_S+\mathrm{constant}$.

Assuming the continuum normalization (4.25) with positive $\rho_y=\widehat{\varrho}_S(y)$, set $a=\int\rho_y\,d\mu$. Cauchy–Schwarz yields $\|a\psi\|^2\le 4\pi\int\|\rho_y\psi\|^2d\mu=4\pi\mathscr A\|\psi\|^2$. Moreover, $a\psi=0$ forces $\rho_y^{1/2}\psi=0$ almost everywhere by positivity, hence $\mathscr A\|\psi\|^2=0$ by normalization, so $\ker a=\{0\}$ and $a^{-1/2}$ is densely defined (though potentially unbounded). Given positive self-adjoint $\Xi_y(v)$ depending measurably on its labels, sandwiching $I\le e^{\min(\Xi_y(v),R)}\le e^R I$ between $\rho_y a^{-1/2}\psi$ gives, on $D(a^{-1/2})$,

$$
(1-\epsilon)\mathscr A\|a^{-1/2}\psi\|^2
\le Q_R[\psi]\le e^R(1-\epsilon)\mathscr A\|a^{-1/2}\psi\|^2 .
\tag{A5}
$$

Because the two form norms are equivalent, the capped clock form $Q_R$ is closed. At $R=0$ the clock operator is $(1-\epsilon)\mathscr A a^{-1}$, which reduces in the shear-free spherical case $\rho_y=\sqrt{\mathscr A/(4\pi)}I$ to $(1-\epsilon)\sqrt{\mathscr A/(4\pi)}I$. For general data, combining (A5) with $a\le\sqrt{4\pi\mathscr A}I$ yields that same scalar lower bound on the clock's eventual domain. Conditional on the operators, this proves the area ceiling $\mathscr A\le 4\pi[\ell/(1-\epsilon)]^2$ on any nonzero fixed-area sector passing the clock cutoff $\ell$—bounding the allowed area *values*, though not their multiplicities. At $\mathscr A=0$, $a^{-1/2}$ has empty positive spectral support and the fixed-positive-area derivation yields no clock.

Within a single fixed-area space, commutation with $\widehat{\mathscr A}=\mathscr A I$ is automatic. Using the joint clock–area spectral probabilities of (5.3) on the larger space $\mathcal H_\omega$—which contains area-changing insertions—additionally requires a compatible family of clock operators across the area spectral decomposition, or an independent proof of a globally defined clock strongly commuting with $\widehat{\mathscr A}$.

**Removing the focusing cutoff.** At fixed mode resolution, define $Q=\sup_R Q_R$ on $D(a^{-1/2})$ and let $M=\overline{\{\psi:\sup_R Q_R[\psi]<\infty\}}$. Monotone convergence of closed forms defines a positive self-adjoint operator $T$ on $M$, and the capped resolvent filter converges strongly to $L(L+T)^{-1}P_M$ at fixed $L$, matching (C.13). Sending $L\to\infty$ then yields $P_M$. Recovering every incoming state requires $M=\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$. The coherent-vector differentiation in (C.17) establishes $M=\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$ *if* the uncapped filter recovers every nearby finite-mode displacement, but the paper's quantum-tail estimate after (5.16) is explicitly heuristic: equations (C.16c,d) bound a classical indicator against a positive Wigner density at fixed resolution, supplying neither a noncommuting operator tail estimate nor uniformity as resolution increases. [Behrndt et al., Theorem 4.2 and observation immediately following it](https://www.numerik.math.tu-graz.ac.at/~behrndt/BehrndtHassiSnooWietsma09.pdf)

**Counterexample to the general classical-to-quantum inference.** In a dimensionless one-pair model with $Q=x$ and $P=-i\hbar\,d/dx$, take two angular cells with constant corner weights and focusings $cQ^2$ and $cP^2$ ($c>0$). The classical clock reading, proportional to $e^{cx^2}+e^{cp^2}$, is finite everywhere and assigns vanishing Gaussian probability above any growing cutoff, while every capped quantum form is bounded. Yet a vector lies in the uncapped form domain only if both

$$
e^{cx^2/2}\psi(x)\in L^2(dx),\qquad
e^{c\hbar^2\xi^2/2}\widehat\psi(\xi)\in L^2(d\xi).
\tag{A6}
$$

With Fourier kernel $e^{-ix\xi}$, the $L^2$ Hardy uncertainty principle forces $\psi=0$ whenever the product of the two Gaussian exponents is at least $1/4$, i.e., $c\hbar\ge 1$. In that regime $M=\{0\}$ despite vanishing classical tails. This example tests the logical step used for (5.16); whether the specific gravitational focusing operators avoid this obstruction depends on their own joint domains. [Escauriaza et al., p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.05670)

## Source insertions and the boundary algebra, (4.28) and (C.18)–(C.22)

* **Second-order source insertions, (4.28).** Writing $\Delta H_I=-j\mathcal O_{1,I}-j^2\mathcal O_{2,I}/2+O(j^3)$ in the interaction picture, differentiating the linear Dyson term twice gives $(i/\hbar)\int\mathcal O_{2,I}$, while the quadratic Dyson term gives $-2/\hbar^2\int_{t>s}\mathcal O_{1,I}(t)\mathcal O_{1,I}(s)\,dt\,ds$. This verifies the sign, factor of two, and same-time contact term for twice-differentiable bounded source families with integrable norm bounds; the manuscript treats polynomial interactions order by order.
* **Relative sources in the reference dynamics, (C.18).** Under the relative-source isomorphism, $S_I(-V)\mapsto S_{I_0}(V)^{-1}$ and $S_I(F-V)\mapsto S_{I_0}(V)^{-1}S_{I_0}(F)$, so taking the left inverse in (C.18b) cancels the two factors. Because the reference Hamiltonian $H_0=0$ leaves $\mathcal Q(f)$ time-independent, $\int j\,dt=1$ gives $\widehat Y_f(\lambda)=\exp(i\lambda\mathcal Q(f)/\hbar)$. This uses the admitted finite-mode Schwartz sources, including the spatially nonlocal functions permitted in §4.1.
* **Rank-one Weyl symbols, (C.19).** Inserting (C.19a) into the Weyl kernel $(2\pi\hbar)^{-n}\int e^{ip\cdot(x-x')/\hbar} f((x+x')/2,p)\,d^np$ fixes $y=x-x'$ upon momentum integration and returns $u(x)\overline{v(x')}$. For normalized $u=v$, $P=|u\rangle\langle u|$ satisfies $e^{i\pi P}=I-2P$. General $u,v$ decompose into real and imaginary symbols whose bounded self-adjoint operators are recovered by norm differentiation of their source unitaries at zero.
* **Strong convergence to the full algebra, (C.20).** On any elementary tensor differing from $\Omega$ only in its first $m$ modes, $E_N$ coincides with $|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ for all $N\ge m$. Density and $\|E_N\|=1$ give strong convergence (while operator-norm convergence fails because a unit excitation purely in mode $N+1$ gives norm difference $1$). Acting with local matrix units on $\Omega$ spans a dense subset of the incomplete tensor product, so the von Neumann closure contains all rank-one operators and equals $\mathcal B(\mathcal K_{\mathscr A})$ in this representation.
* **Clock compression, (C.21)–(C.22).** For any existing orthogonal projection $P$, inserting $I=P+(I-P)$ gives the compressed-product identity (C.21). Bounded multiplication on both sides preserves strong convergence, producing compressed matrix units $|Pa\Omega\rangle\langle Pb\Omega|$ whose generating vectors are dense in $P\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$ and hence generate $\mathcal B(P\mathcal K_{\mathscr A})$. At positive fixed area, the reflected norm squared in (5.23) is $e^{-\mathscr A/(4G_N\hbar)}\|P\psi\|^2$ with kernel $\ker P$ under that gluing prescription. All of these operator statements hold for projections of any rank, including zero; interpreting the compression as a measurement of the full gravitational clock requires the continuum domain and spectral construction analyzed above.
