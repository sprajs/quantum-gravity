# Section 3.1 · First-order gravity

**Physical question.** How do Einstein's vacuum equations emerge from the paper's first-order (tetrad-and-connection) action on a causal diamond bounded by null cones and spacelike caps, and do the boundary and joint terms actually cancel all independent variations of the spin connection?

**Outcome.** On the invertible, oriented gravitational branch selected in (3.4), the bulk action and field equations reproduce vacuum general relativity with cosmological constant $\Lambda$. The null-face, spacelike-cap, and three joint terms cancel every independent connection variation with the three joint signs as printed, matching Lehner et al. Four qualifications matter for what follows: (1) equation (3.5a) requires the full spin-covariant derivative $D_\omega$ acting on internal indices, whereas (3.1) uses the same symbol for an antisymmetrized spacetime derivative leaving $\omega\wedge\omega$ explicit; (2) the simplicity constraint admits other branches (such as $B=0$), and fixing $B$ determines $e$ only up to an overall sign until time orientation is chosen; (3) the affine action depends on the choice of null-generator normalizations; and (4) the small-cap limit requires regular shrinking caps with controlled extrinsic curvature and normal logarithms. This section establishes the classical action on the selected sector; constructing a quantum measure or the later reduced operator algebra requires separate arguments.

## Bulk action and classical equivalence · (3.1)–(3.9)

Use internal indices $I,J$, signature $(-+++)$, $\epsilon_{0123}=1$, $\Sigma^{IJ}=e^I\wedge e^J$, and $V=e^0\wedge e^1\wedge e^2\wedge e^3$. Repeated antisymmetric index pairs in the action sum over both orderings.

1. **Simplicity constraint.** With $B=\star\Sigma$, the oriented coframe gives $B^{01}=-e^2\wedge e^3$ and $B^{23}=e^0\wedge e^1$, so $B^{IJ}\wedge B^{KL}=\epsilon^{IJKL}V$ and $\epsilon_{IJKL} B^{IJ}\wedge B^{KL}=-24V$. Substituting into (3.3) makes it vanish. Conversely, varying a pair-symmetric multiplier with fixed $\epsilon$-trace forces the other 20 components of $B\wedge B$ to vanish. The projector sign in (3.3) follows from $\epsilon^{IJKL}\epsilon_{IJKL}=-24$.
2. **Cosmological constant and Palatini integrand.** The multiplier term evaluates to $-\tfrac12\Phi_{IJKL}\epsilon^{IJKL}V=-2\Lambda V$, while the curvature term is $\tfrac12\epsilon_{IJKL}\Sigma^{IJ}\wedge F^{KL}$. In the torsion-free coframe where $F^{IJ}=\tfrac12R^{IJ}{}_{KL}\Sigma^{KL}$, contracting indices yields $RV$. This verifies every numerical factor in (3.6)–(3.8), including the $\Lambda/6$ inside the Palatini integrand.
3. **Zero torsion from varying $\omega$.** Varying the spin connection gives $D_\omega B=0$. Because $D_\omega$ preserves the internal Hodge dual and $\star^2=-1$, this becomes $T^I\wedge e^J=e^I\wedge T^J$ for the torsion $T^I=D_\omega e^I$. Contracting with the dual frame and summing on $J$ (with $U=\sum_J\iota_J T^J$) gives $T^I=-e^I\wedge U$; a second contraction gives $U=-3U$, so $U=0$ and hence $T^I=0$. Invertibility of the tetrad enters this step explicitly.
4. **Einstein equations and the multiplier.** Varying the tetrad on this branch yields $\epsilon_{IJKL}e^J\wedge(F^{KL}-\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\Sigma^{KL})=0$. For the torsion-free connection these are $G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=0$, equivalently $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$. Conversely, given any Einstein metric, setting $\Phi_{IJMN}=-\tfrac14R_{IJKL}\epsilon^{KL}{}_{MN}$ satisfies (3.5b) with internal $\epsilon$-trace $R=4\Lambda$; pair symmetry holds because the Riemann operator of an Einstein metric has no traceless Ricci block and therefore commutes with the internal dual. As an independent sign check, constant curvature $F^{IJ}=k\Sigma^{IJ}$ gives $\Phi=-(k/2)\epsilon$ and $k=\Lambda/3$.
5. **Integration by parts and the derivative notation clash.** Integrating by parts gives $B_{IJ}\wedge D_\omega\delta\omega^{IJ}=d(B_{IJ}\wedge\delta\omega^{IJ})+\delta\omega^{IJ}\wedge D_\omega B_{IJ}$. In signature $(-+++)$, the second term is $-\tfrac12\epsilon_g^{dabc}\delta\omega_{dIJ}D_{[a}B^{IJ}{}_{bc]}V$, matching (3.9). Note a notation ambiguity: in (3.1) the antisymmetrized spacetime derivative computes $d\omega$ while leaving $\omega\wedge\omega$ explicit, whereas (3.5a), (3.9), and (3.10) require the full spin-covariant derivative $D_\omega$ acting on internal indices. Reading (3.5a) literally with only the spacetime derivative of (3.1) would break local Lorentz covariance.

The simplicity equation admits other sectors, including the degenerate branch $B=0$, so classical equivalence relies explicitly on the branch choice made after (3.3). In a fixed internal frame, nondegenerate $B$ determines $e$ only up to the simultaneous sign flip $e\mapsto -e$ (a Lorentz transformation rotates both $e$ and $B$). Choosing time-oriented tetrads fixes this sign, while the metric is unchanged in either case.

## Null, cap, and joint terms · (3.10)–(3.16)

Because $\omega$ is varied independently in first-order form, the boundary action on the null faces, spacelike caps, and codimension-2 joints must cancel the boundary term $B_{IJ}\wedge\delta\omega^{IJ}$ from the bulk variation.

At the corner $S$, (3.11) gives $n_p\cdot\ell_p=-1$. At a spacelike cap, writing $c=-n_i\cdot\ell_s>0$, equation (3.12) gives $n_i=\ell_s/(2c)+c\,n_s$ and $n_i^2=-1$. The three contractions in (3.13) then produce the inaffinity $\kappa$, the screen expansion $\Theta$, and the cap extrinsic-curvature trace $K$ with future-directed unit normal.

To verify cancellation for all independent connection variations at a face point, pick an orthonormal frame with outward radial leg $1$, screen legs $A=2,3$, and null vectors $\ell=(1,s,0,0)$ and $n=(1,-s,0,0)/2$, where $s=+1$ on the expanding side and $s=-1$ on the contracting side of the reference diamond. Locally, the pullbacks are $e^0=du$, $e^1=s\,du$, $e^2=dy$, $e^3=dz$, and the outgoing Stokes orientation is negative relative to $du\wedge dy\wedge dz$ on both faces. Writing $a^{IJ}=\delta\omega^{IJ}$,

$$
\delta_\omega\kappa=s\,a_u^{01},\qquad
\delta_\omega\Theta=\sum_{A=2,3}(a_A^{0A}-s\,a_A^{1A}).
$$

Before dividing by $16\pi G_N$, the oriented bulk boundary integrand from (3.9) is $-2s(\delta_\omega\kappa+\delta_\omega\Theta)\,du\wedge dy\wedge dz$, which cancels (3.15b) including its factor of two. On a future spacelike cap with $n=(1,0,0,0)$, the outgoing bulk integrand is $+2\sum_{i=1}^3 a_i^{0i}=+2\delta_\omega K$ times the cap volume element, and the past cap carries the opposite sign. Because these checks are pointwise in an adapted frame, they hold across any curved face. An exact alternating-symbol enumeration also verified all 256 index tuples in the $B\wedge B$ identity and all 36 independent tangential connection components across the two null faces.

For a torsion-free connection, $\partial_u\sqrt{\det q}=\tfrac12\sqrt{\det q}\,q^{AB}\partial_u q_{AB}=\sqrt{\det q}\,\Theta$, so integrating along each regular generator proves (3.16) with both area signs. Off shell, however, independent connection variations add the torsion term in (A.3); substituting (3.16) *before* varying would wrongly drop the transverse connection variation verified above. In the joint integrand, a variation of the corner rapidity enters with coefficient magnitude $\mu/(8\pi G_N)$.

The spacelike signs $(+,-)$, null signs $(+,-)$, and three joint signs $(+,+,-)$ in (3.15a) agree with the explicit two-cone calculation of Lehner et al. once $1/(16\pi G_N)$ is restored, with the factor $1/2$ inside the null–null logarithm fixing the reference normalization. Notice that the value of the action depends on the paper's generator-parameter conventions: a constant rescaling $\ell_p\mapsto c\ell_p$ ($c>0$) at fixed geometry shifts the affine action by $(\mathscr A_{\partial\Sigma_0}-\mathscr A_S)\log c/(8\pi G_N)$ through the two adjacent joints. [Lehner et al., Sec. II.6.3, Eq. (118)](https://arxiv.org/html/1609.00207v1#S2.SS6.SSS3)

Finally, the small-cap limit behaves cleanly only for regular shrinking cap families: if the linear cap size is $r$, with $K=O(1/r)$ or better and the relative normal logarithm bounded or $O(|\log r|)$, then the cap and joint integrals scale as $O(r^2)$ and $O(r^2(1+|\log r|))$, respectively. Ensuring those bounds requires controlling both the cap family and the null-generator normalizations.
