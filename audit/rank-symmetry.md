# Can angular symmetries decide the rank of a clock window?

On a marked sphere, one quarter-turn and one smooth twist that preserves solid angle suffice for a test of finite rank. Suppose they act unitarily on one fixed-area sector, transform its area density geometrically, and preserve the same clock window. If that window has finite rank, every state it accepts has the exactly uniform area density as an operator eigenvalue. Measuring any departure from this property rules out finite rank for that window. If a single additional change of angular frame redistributes solid angle and acts covariantly on the area measure, *every* nonzero such window has infinite rank in the separable sector. This second conclusion uses covariance of the area measure under all rotations. The additional transformation can change the clock. These are conditional results about the external frame of Venkatesa Chandrasekaran's [arXiv:2611.34924v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1).

## Which transformations preserve the classical reading?

Take the reference sphere to have its round solid angle and use the probability measure $d\mu=\boldsymbol\epsilon_0/(4\pi)$. Write $r=\sqrt{\mathscr A/(4\pi)}>0$. In longitude $\phi$ and height $z=\cos\theta$, consider
$$
 s_\tau(z,\phi)=(z,\phi+\tau z),\qquad \tau\ne0.
$$
This map rotates the horizontal Cartesian components by an angle $\tau z$. It extends smoothly over both poles and has Jacobian one. Under its angular pullback on the ray data with the reference measure fixed, $\rho=\varrho_S$ transforms as a scalar. The cap ratio $v$ is unchanged on corresponding rays. At fixed $v$ the conformal metric and its $v$ derivative pull back together, so the contraction in the focusing integral $\Xi$ is a scalar. Both angular integrals in the clock transform by change of variables. Their ratio is invariant for any smooth profile in this chart. This proves an identity of the classical functional; extending the transformation through the cap as an admissible physical surface action is a separate question. [Focusing, (3.26)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E26) · [Clock and average, (3.27)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E28)

The conformal displacement generated by $\nabla z$ has a different effect. It takes $z$ to $(z+\tanh t)/(1+z\tanh t)$ and its pullback Jacobian is $J_t=(\cosh t+z\sinh t)^{-2}$. Start with constant $\rho=r$ and zero shear. Pull back the physical area at fixed reference solid angle so that $\rho'=r\sqrt{J_t}$. The conformal data remain independent of $v$ and $\int J_t\,d\mu=1$. Since $\int\sqrt{J_t}\,d\mu=t/\sinh t$, the new reading is
$$
 \bar u_*'=(1-\epsilon)r\,\frac{\sinh t}{t}.
$$
For any $t\ne0$ this exceeds the original $(1-\epsilon)r$. Thus the full group of angular changes already fails the invariance test on shear-free data. This calculation holds the reference solid angle fixed throughout.

## Finite rank forces sharp local area

Let $P$ be the projection onto one specified clock window in the physical fixed-area Hilbert space $\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$. Use the following precise reading of v1's density normalization. The positive square-root densities $\rho(y)$ give strongly measurable vectors $\rho(y)\psi$ for almost every $y$, and the quadratic form identity
$$
 \int\|\rho(y)\psi\|^2d\mu=r^2\|\psi\|^2
 \tag{1}
$$
holds on the ambient fixed-area space, with the usual extension by the isometry from a common form core when required. The operators at different $y$ may fail to commute. The mean $H=\int\rho(y)d\mu$ is bounded and $0\le H\le rI$ by (1). Suppose a quarter-turn carrying the polar axis to an equatorial axis and the single twist $s_\tau$ have unitary implementers on this same space satisfying $U_gPU_g^*=P$ and $U_g\rho(y)U_g^*=\rho(gy)$ in the integrated sense. V1 specifies the area normalization and discusses changes of boundary variables. [Area and clock operators, (4.24)–(4.27)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E24) · [Clock selection, (5.20)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E20)

**Theorem.** Under these hypotheses, finite rank implies
$$
  \rho(y)P=rP\quad\text{for almost every }y.
  \tag{2}
$$

**Proof.** For finite $d=\operatorname{rank}P$, the field $B(y)=\rho(y)P$ has values in the Hilbert space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators. Summing (1) over an orthonormal basis of $P\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$ gives $\int\|B(y)\|_{\rm HS}^2d\mu=dr^2$. Consequently $F:f\mapsto\int f(y)B(y)d\mu$ from $L^2(S^2,\mu)$ into that Hilbert space is Hilbert–Schmidt, by Parseval. Conjugation by $U_g$ intertwines $F$ with pullback by $g^{-1}$ on its input. The compact positive operator $F^*F$ therefore commutes with all these pullbacks. Each of its positive-eigenvalue eigenspaces is finite dimensional and invariant.

To identify these eigenspaces, diagonalize the unitary twist on any one of them. Write an eigenfunction in azimuthal Fourier modes $f_m(z)e^{im\phi}$. Its eigenvalue $\lambda$ satisfies $(e^{im\tau z}-\lambda)f_m(z)=0$ almost everywhere (either pullback convention gives the same result). For $m\ne0$ the zero set of this factor has measure zero. Every vector in the eigenspace is therefore invariant under **all** rotations about the polar axis. The quarter-turn preserves the eigenspace and conjugates those axial rotations to rotations about a perpendicular axis. Rotations about these two axes generate $SO(3)$, so every vector in the eigenspace is constant. Thus $F^*F$ annihilates mean-zero functions by its compact spectral decomposition. It follows that $F$ annihilates them as well, and $B(y)=HP$ almost everywhere.

For $\psi\in P\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$, (1) now gives $\|H\psi\|=r\|\psi\|$. The positive contraction $H/r$ can attain this norm only on its eigenvectors of eigenvalue one. Hence $H\psi=r\psi$, which proves (2). Global normalization in (1) gives the contraction bound even when individual $\rho(y)$ can move an accepted input out of the window.

## How to detect the contradiction

* **One bounded measurement and one clock acceptance.** In an arbitrary unit input $\psi$ measure $d_\psi=1-\langle H\rangle_\psi/r$. Equation (1) gives $2r^2d_\psi=\int\|(\rho(y)-rI)\psi\|^2d\mu$. If $P$ has finite rank, (2) puts its range in $\ker(rI-H)$, hence $P\le H/r$ and $\|P\psi\|^2\le 1-d_\psi$. Acceptance strictly above this bound proves infinite rank. The same comparison can use two functions of v1's clocks. Writing $T_0$ for the clock with the focusing cutoff set to zero, (5.15) gives $T_0=(1-\epsilon)r^2H^{-1}$. Hence $1-d_\psi=(1-\epsilon)r\langle T_0^{-1}\rangle_\psi$, a bounded expectation for the same preparation. A two-copy measurement would require a readout of the zero-focus observable used in the regulator calculation. In particular, a positive $d_\Omega$ for the incoming Gaussian and v1's claimed $\|P_\ell\Omega\|^2\to1$ as $\ell\to\infty$ imply infinite rank for all sufficiently large finite thresholds for which the angular hypotheses hold. If the clock form is dense, the increasing finite-ceiling windows exhaust the sector. Finite rank at every ceiling would then force $H=rI$ and a uniform operator-valued area density throughout the sector. A rotationally uniform *mean area measure* need not give $d_\Omega=0$. The Gaussian's actual shape and acceptance require operator estimates in its chosen canonical chart. [Incoming Gaussian, (4.7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E7) · [Zero-focus clock, (5.15)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E15) · [Vacuum acceptance, (5.19)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E19)
* **One more angular change, for any nonzero window.** Define the normalized area effect $M(E)$ by the weak form $r^{-2}\int_E\rho(y)^2d\mu$. If (2) holds, every accepted vector $\psi$ obeys $M(E)\psi=\mu(E)\psi$ for each measurable $E$. To see this, pair with any ambient vector $\eta$ and use $\langle\rho(y)\eta,\rho(y)\psi\rangle=r^2\langle\eta,\psi\rangle$ almost everywhere. Assume a unitary on the same sector geometrically transports the marked $M$ by one smooth map $h$ with $h_*\mu\ne\mu$. It sends $\psi$ (or its inverse does, depending on convention) to a simultaneous eigenvector of these effects with eigenmeasure $h_*\mu$. If all rotations act covariantly on the same marked area measure, its rotated copies have uncountably many distinct eigenmeasures. Indeed the orbit is a connected non-singleton subset of probability measures in the weak topology, since the unique rotation-invariant probability on the sphere is $\mu$. Distinct eigenmeasures imply orthogonal vectors, by choosing a self-adjoint effect on which the eigenvalues differ. This is impossible for a nonzero vector in the separable incoming sector of §4.2. This argument needs neither clock invariance under $h$ nor a local-boost commutator with $P$. [Fixed-area representation, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2) · [Earlier obstruction using a commuting moment](corner-commutant.md)

Equation (1) uses square-root densities as operators. A normalized positive operator-valued measure alone permits a counterexample. On $L^2(S^2,\mu)$ let $M(E)$ multiply by the indicator of $E$ and let $P$ project onto the constants. Pullbacks with the square-root Jacobian implement angular changes. Rotations and the twist preserve $P$, and all vector area measures are absolutely continuous. Yet multiplying the constant by an indicator takes it out of this rank-one window. Evaluation at a single point would supply a delta distribution in this example, outside the square-root density hypothesis (1).

## What rotations alone allow

An infinite Hilbert space with exact rotation symmetry, a positive angular area density, and the ordered clock (4.26) can have a nonzero finite window at every sufficiently large finite threshold. Take a single spin-$j$ representation for each integer $j\ge1$. Let $J_i$ be its dimensionless rotation generators, $C=j(j+1)$ its Casimir, $0<\alpha<1$, and $D=1+\alpha^2/3$. On this block set
$$
 \rho(n)=\frac r{\sqrt D}\left(I+\frac{\alpha\, n\cdot J}{\sqrt C}\right),
 \qquad \Xi_n(v)=\frac{v^2-\epsilon^2}{8}\,C I.
$$
The density is strictly positive. Rotation covariance and $\int n_i n_k d\mu=\delta_{ik}/3$ give $\int\rho^2 d\mu=r^2 I$ and $H=rI/\sqrt D$. The prescribed scalar focusing has the positive square structure (4.22), for example with $z^1(v)=(v-\epsilon)\sqrt C\,I$, $z^2=0$ in its one-coordinate expression. Equation (4.26) yields a clock eigenvalue on the entire block,
$$
 t_j=r\sqrt D\int_\epsilon^1\exp[(v^2-\epsilon^2)j(j+1)/8]dv .
$$
These eigenvalues increase strictly to infinity for $0\le\epsilon<1$. Each has finite multiplicity $2j+1$. The first window that contains only the $j=1$ block has rank three and $d_\psi=1-1/\sqrt D>0$ on every accepted input. Rotations therefore permit finite rank and fluctuating area even with the prescribed ordering. This model supplies local operator profiles and a rotation-covariant scalar focusing. It imposes no vacuum Einstein constraints or global gravitational tensor polarization. [Positive focusing, (4.22)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E22) · [Clock, (4.26)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E26)

## Does the twist act on the gravitational space?

A physical external reference sphere needs its own marking. At a smooth vertex, changing the orientation of a single local tetrad with fixed time direction rotates the sphere of null rays. No such tetrad change performs the latitude-dependent twist. A cap with independently marked ray labels may admit this larger action. It must preserve the matching conditions on the full metric and normal connection as well as the definition of the clock. A simultaneous diffeomorphism of the bulk fields and embedding preserves their pullbacks. Gluing may remove an internal frame altogether. [Smooth-vertex normalization following (3.25)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E25) · [Angular matching, (3.62)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E62) · [Independent rim displacements](match-reduction.md)

Equation (B.24) gives a classical marked angular charge. Equations (C.1) and (E.16) compare boundary descriptions, with $P'=VPV^*$ for the correspondingly transformed clock. To apply the theorem, construct the angular action on one fixed-area representation and show that the quarter-turn and the twist return the same projector for the same instrument. For example, deriving geometric transformation of $\rho(y)$ and of $\Xi_y(v)$ in (4.26), including the regulated closed-form domains, would prove the window identity by change of integration variables and the spectral theorem. For a commuting log-area field with Gaussian Sobolev covariance the twist takes the area law to a mutually singular law. In this model it has no implementing unitary on the fixed representation. If only the tetrad rotations act, the spin example exhibits the missing implication. The four-dimensional rank remains a **proof gap** until the physical surface action or a direct spectral estimate settles it. ## Reproduce the checks

Run `python3 checks/rank_symmetry.py` from the repository root. The script integrates the transformed round clock at $t=1$, checks a nonzero degree-two harmonic in the twisted degree-one field, and verifies the normalized spin-one density and the rank-three window. At $r=1$, $\alpha=1/2$ and $\epsilon=1/4$ the expected round clock ratio is **1.17520119**, the imaginary quadrupole overlap is **0.12407010**, and the first two spin clocks are **0.85937801** and **1.05673106**. The ceiling $1$ accepts three states, each with shape deficit **0.03923108**. The full suite runs with `make check`. [Executable check](../checks/rank_symmetry.py)
