# What the null-cone construction predicts

**The physical question.** *If this null-cone quantization of gravity worked as proposed, what concrete physical predictions would it make for black holes, gravitational waves, locality, and experiments—and what remains outside its reach?*

**The short answer.** Venkatesa Chandrasekaran's proposal addresses four-dimensional **vacuum** Einstein gravity with a cosmological constant. Its most distinctive physical claim is that capping a geometric clock leaves only finitely many distinguishable states inside a causal diamond; its most explicit finite-coupling calculation is a pair correlation between two chosen operations on a specified incoming Gaussian state. The state-count claim depends on proving that cut and uncut amplitudes agree for the closed trace used in §5.2, while the pair correlator can be verified independently inside a Gaussian oscillator model. Neither supplies a testable correction to a binary inspiral waveform, a black-hole evaporation curve, or a cosmological power spectrum. Equation numbers below refer to `arXiv:2611.34924v1`. [Manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1) · [TeX source archive](https://arxiv.org/src/2611.34924v1)

## Ordinary gravity and the meaning of a measurement

The boundary frame supplies a reference point, direction and affine parameter for measuring the shear of a family of light rays. A source specifies a polarization and a smearing in that frame. Under the same diffeomorphism of the geometry and the reference frame, the measured shear is invariant. The covariant field equations and the initial data determine the classical geometry wherever smooth characteristic evolution applies. Equations (6.1)–(6.5) give the vacuum Einstein equation for a coherent preparation, in the limit of small fluctuations and over a time during which the preparation stays localized. [Sections 3.5–3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=21) · [Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=52)

For example, at $\Lambda=0$ the spherical vacuum exterior admits Schwarzschild mass $M$. With $c=1$, $g_{tt}=-(1-2G_NM/r)$ gives the weak-field potential $\Phi=-G_NM/r$ from $g_{tt}=-(1+2\Phi)$. Relating this to acceleration requires a freely falling test body. Linearization about Minkowski space gives $\Box h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}=0$ in transverse-traceless gauge, so the two radiative polarizations propagate on the classical null cone. These are implications of recovering the vacuum equations. A prediction for an actual source and detector additionally requires their stress, dynamics and coupling to the metric. The scalar in the two-dimensional JT prologue supplies none of these in four dimensions. The four-dimensional action also takes $G_N$ and $\Lambda$ as inputs. [Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=5) · [Equations (6.4) and (6.7)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=52)

At one loop (6.9)–(6.16) propagate the incoming covariance $\Sigma^{IJ}$ through the first and second variations of the classical geometry. Differentiating $E[g(\phi)]=0$ twice gives $E^{(1)}[\delta_I\delta_J g]=-2E^{(2)}[\delta_I g,\delta_J g]$. Its contraction with $\Sigma^{IJ}/2$ yields the quadratic graviton source in (6.13). This is a check on the semiclassical equations and on the dependence of the answer on the chosen state. For a local unsmeared backreaction measurement, derivatives of the two-point distribution at coincidence require a specified subtraction and matching of finite terms. Appendix D.1 compares the quadratic functional and its measure under common boundary conditions. A numerical prediction for a specified detector would additionally need a state, switching and smearing functions, and a detector response. [Section 6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=53) · [Appendix D.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=68)

## Locality, caustics and singularities

Temporal factorization (4.2e) composes ordered source operations. Equation (6.16) propagates the perturbative metric response with a retarded Green function. A different statement would be that any two physical observables with spacelike separated **endpoints** commute. The geodesic used to locate an endpoint samples the connection along its path, and the frame depends on corner data. The paper expressly allows spatially nonlocal sources in Section 4.1. An informative calculation would fix both paths and anchors and evaluate the smeared bracket of the two dressed operators, including the variations of both frames. At leading order (6.10) gives an antisymmetric part $i\hbar\,\delta_I g(p)J^{IJ}\delta_J g(p')$ for the commutator. Endpoint separation alone does not evaluate this expression. Perturbative examples of other dressings exhibit nonzero spacelike commutators. A nonzero bracket of extended observables would still require an operational signalling analysis. [Sections 3.5 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=21) · [Equation (6.10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=53) · [Donnelly and Giddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07921)

The photon-sphere example exhibits a **caustic**. Rays launched in different planes intersect at the antipode of a regular sphere in Schwarzschild spacetime. Another diamond can cover the meeting point. At $r=0$, the invariant $R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=48G_N^2 M^2/r^6$ diverges in the classical Schwarzschild solution. Matching regular tetrads on overlaps gives no continuation through that boundary. The paper's smooth covers and its controlled expansions, which require curvature radii and smearing lengths well above the Planck length, do not establish geodesic completeness or a bounce. A test of quantum singularity resolution would specify incoming states approaching a classically incomplete endpoint, a relational tidal measurement at fixed resolution and a law of evolution that predicts outcomes there or beyond it. Finite results obtained by stopping the calculation before the endpoint do not test such a law. [Sections 3.2 and 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=14) · [Section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=38)

## Unitarity, entropy and black-hole information

For an admissible deformation, $U_{\mathscr M}$ is a unitary generator of the proposed algebra. Equation (4.18) then proves $\|U_{\mathscr M}b\|_\omega=\|b\|_\omega$ in any of its GNS representations. The Born probability (4.19b) for an effect $E$ is $\omega(b^\dagger U_{\mathscr M}^\dagger E U_{\mathscr M}b)$. These results are exact for those inputs. Identifying a particular unitary with the cutoff-independent dynamics of the interacting continuum is a further requirement. A direct test would construct the regulated evolution and compare the matrix elements obtained by subdividing a fixed region along two different cuts. Open sewing of two normalizable vectors follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. Closing a cut sums diagonal weights over a complete basis and requires separate trace control. For example, the identity has norm one and infinite trace on an infinite-dimensional space. [Sections 4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=25) · [Appendix E.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=76)

For the clock-selected subspace the focusing inequality bounds the area. If $0\le\hat s\le s_*$, with $\hat s=\widehat{\mathscr A}/(4G_N\hbar)$, then $e^{-s_*}P_{\mathscr D}\le P_{\mathscr D}e^{-\hat s}P_{\mathscr D}\le P_{\mathscr D}$. Thus finiteness of this reflected trace is **equivalent** to finite dimension on that subspace. The lower bound makes the step from an open amplitude to a closed preparation decisive. Conditional on finite dimension, the area has a finite spectrum for this specified clock bound. The manuscript supplies neither the individual eigenvalues and their spacings nor an emission rate between pairs of levels. Predicting emission lines would require their coupling to outgoing modes, the transition energies, strengths and widths. [Equations (5.21)–(5.23)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=38) · [Equations (5.38)–(5.41)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=43)

If the area-weighted ensemble exists, $S=\langle\hat s\rangle+\log Z$ follows directly from its density matrix. For the large equilibrium Schwarzschild saddle, the manuscript gets $q=257/302$. This gives $q/[2(1-q)]=257/90$ for the clock-selected logarithm and $257/90-1/2=106/45$ for the fixed-mass coefficient of $\log s$ with spins summed. Sen's mixed ensemble has $C_{\rm local}=212/45$ multiplying $\log r_h$, which yields $106/45$ multiplying $\log s$ since $s\propto r_h^2$. The numerical agreement is genuine for the same ensemble and field content. Both calculations use the one-loop massless graviton and ghost data. The match leaves the finite-rank claim and the proposed microscopic count to be tested independently. [Section 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=45) · [Sen, equations (1.3)–(1.5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0971#page=6)

The two-sided calculation concerns an equilibrium Hartle–Hawking state with a reflecting wall on each side. For the retained oscillator of wall frequency $\omega$, tracing (5.75) over one side gives $p_n=(1-e^{-2b\omega})e^{-2nb\omega}$, with $b=\hbar\beta_R/2$. This is a stationary thermal distribution. One can also check (5.76) by differentiating $\int_0^\infty\sin(x\nu)/\sinh(\pi\nu)\,d\nu=\tfrac12\tanh(x/2)$. At $x=\log(u_L/u_R)$, the derivative is $u_Lu_R/(u_L+u_R)^2$, giving the stated shear correlator and, after Wick contraction, the squared area-acceleration correlator in (5.77). These correlations are already present in the paired Gaussian modes on fixed backgrounds. The Hilbert–Schmidt identity (5.73) gives two-boundary factorization under the adopted gluing inner product. None of these calculations evolves a collapsing state into outgoing radiation at future null infinity. [Section 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=49)

An information-recovery calculation would specify the radiation observables at infinity, include the relevant massless species or explicitly restrict to graviton emission, evolve distinguishable collapsing states, and track the information available in the outgoing radiation. For complete evaporation of an initially pure system with no remaining sector, the late radiation should be pure and should preserve distinguishability of the initial orthogonal states. Unitary evolution of the combined system alone is compatible with a mixed radiation subsystem. The time dependence of the radiation entropy, including a possible Page turnover, is absent from the equilibrium calculation. [Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9306083)

## Which tests would discriminate?

| Candidate | What could be calculated or falsified |
| --- | --- |
| Radial pair correlation (4.32)–(4.34) | The source fixes the mode rotation $e^{-ik/s}$ and the chosen Gaussian fixes the variance $1/(2sk)$. Wick contraction then gives $\mathrm{Li}_2(e^{-2i/s})/(2s^2)$. Even replacing the phase by one leaves an absolutely convergent $\sum k^{-2}$. A comparison sensitive to gravitational interactions would fix external graviton wave packets and derive their evolution from $H_c$. The prescribed rotation alone does not determine a wave's frequency-dependent speed in vacuum. [Section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=33) |
| Interacting graviton scattering | Derive an infrared-controlled asymptotically flat four-graviton amplitude, or a suitable large-diamond limit, for specified helicities. Test its low-energy unitarity cuts and its known physical two-loop scale dependence with common subtraction conventions. Then extract a fixed finite matching coefficient or a nonperturbative correction with an error estimate. A mismatch in the shared low-energy regime would challenge the proposed dynamics. [Bern, Chi, Dixon and Edison](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02422) |
| Finite diamond state count | Compute or bound the rank of the same clock projector at fixed physical $\ell,\epsilon,G_N$ as both radial and angular cutoffs are removed. An infinite orthonormal family supported below that clock bound, surviving the physical quotient, would disprove finite dimension. A persistent continuum component in the clock-restricted area spectrum would also disprove it. Finite matrices at any finite cutoff do not settle either test. [Sections 5.1–5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=35) |
| Clock and area as measurements | Equation (5.3) gives a conditional probability once the state and commuting spectral measures are known. A comparison with an experiment needs a repeatable preparation, dynamics for the reference systems, a measurement resolution, and a predicted distribution that differs from the competing theory for those very settings.
