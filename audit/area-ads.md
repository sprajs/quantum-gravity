# Area-changing insertions and the AdS energy spectrum

**The physical question.** *Does a gravitational-wave probe actually connect states of different horizon area, and does pairing a finite diamond with an Anti-de Sitter universe force the AdS energy spectrum to be discrete?*

**Verdict.** Testing for a nonzero response to a **uniform relative boost** is an exact way to check whether an insertion connects distinct total areas—provided the response is computed in the physical representation on the clock-selected cut. In practice, the paper computes that response in linearized gravity; identifying it with the exact matrix element, and assuming the cut is already finite-dimensional, are additional inputs to (5.41). The AdS argument (5.44)–(5.46) has a different missing input: demanding that its disconnected geometry converge under arbitrary finite-subset orderings is mathematically equivalent to the finite thermal trace the paper wants to prove. Once that trace is available, the later level-counting and ground-state-sector arguments go through. [§5.2, pp. 38–45](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1) · [Area and AdS argument](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2.p17.1)

## Which corner charge is measured?

After matching the two induced metrics, the oriented corner potential contains

$$
\frac{1}{8\pi G_N}\int_S dA(y)\,\delta[\Gamma_R(y)-\Gamma_L(y)].
$$

A simultaneous boost of both frames has zero charge on matched data. A relative shift by a prescribed angular profile $h(y)$ has charge $K[h]=(8\pi G_N)^{-1}\int_S h(y)\,dA(y)$. Choosing $h=1$ gives $K[1]=A/(8\pi G_N)$. A probe can have small transverse support while the right frame is boosted uniformly over the whole cut. The paper explicitly makes that choice. A boost applied just to a patch would instead measure that patch's weighted area. For example, in the two states $|10\rangle,|01\rangle$ of a two-patch model the total occupation equals one. An operator exchanging them commutes with the total, while a boost generated by the first patch's occupation changes its off-diagonal phase. [Corner form](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS3.p4.2) · [Matching and charge cancellation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS6.p4.1) · [Uniform right-frame transformation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p5.1)

Let $P=P_{\mathscr D}$ strongly commute with $A$, write $B=P\sin(j_R\widehat\sigma_{f_R})P$ on $P\mathcal H$, and suppose $U(\eta)=e^{i\eta A/(8\pi G_N\hbar)}$ acts on the right factor of the doubled cut while the left insertion stays fixed. If $A$ has a finite spectral resolution $A=\sum_j A_jP_j$ there, then

$$
U(\eta)BU(\eta)^*-B
=\sum_{k,j}\left(e^{i\eta(A_k-A_j)/(8\pi G_N\hbar)}-1\right)P_kBP_j .
$$

Any nonzero two-sided expectation of this difference times the bounded left insertion proves $P_kBP_j\ne0$ for at least one $k\ne j$. Cyclicity in (5.20) then supplies an $X$ with the nonzero matrix element stated in (5.41). The initial eigenvalue of that specific shear transition may already have been reached by $X$. No spacing, absolute eigenvalue, or connectivity of every pair follows. Before discreteness, the spectral theorem gives a weaker implication. Failure to commute with the spectral measure of $A$ yields a nonzero block between disjoint spectral sets, which can be refined to disjoint intervals. Their spectral subspaces can be continuous. [Spectral selection (4.12)–(4.15)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E12) · [Cyclic preparations](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS1.p17.1) · [Compression with intervening excursions](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS3.p2.2)

For the linearized freely falling vacuum, $G(-d,d)=-c_\perp/(4d^2)$ and $e^\eta G(-d,e^\eta d)/G(-d,d)=4e^\eta/(1+e^\eta)^2$. This yields a nonzero response at order-one rapidity and nonzero mixed covariance for suitable sources in the Gaussian sine correlator. A sharp incoming state is not itself the doubled preparation used for this calculation. To infer (5.41), one needs a well-defined normalized doubled state, covariance of the same dressed insertion under the represented charge, and a bound keeping its exact response away from zero in that state with the external clock and area weights. The reference-vacuum computation, smooth-background estimates, and formal suppression of additional loops give evidence at separated scales. They do not give a nonperturbative remainder bound in the chosen pure extension. Testing the area-changing insertion by the same correlator used to establish the finite cut also makes it dependent on the earlier sewing argument. ## Sharp total area and regularity

The fixed-area Gaussian in §4.2 represents the uniform boost by the scalar $e^{i\eta A/(8\pi G_N\hbar)}$. Its regular canonical pairs describe the remaining data, including angular variations of the area density. This construction is compatible with an exactly sharp total area on the incoming subalgebra. In a representation of multiple areas, the area-preserving algebra commutes with each total-area spectral projection. Superselection is relative to this choice of observables. For the full source algebra, one nonzero block in (5.41), if established, makes the corresponding sectors communicate. Equivalently, once the compressed algebra is known to equal all bounded operators and the area has more than one spectral value, some bounded operator connects area bands. That abstract argument does not identify the particular shear insertion. A pure state extension yields an irreducible GNS representation. Its existence alone fixes neither strong continuity of the represented boost on all source-excited vectors nor their area-transition amplitudes. [Fixed-area state and observables](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p1.1) · [Pure extension](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p8.1) · [Clock-selected algebra](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS1.p17.2)

There would be an obstruction to additionally postulating a **regular canonical translation of total area**. Set $K=A/(8\pi G_N)$, and suppose $V(t)^*KV(t)=K+tI$ globally. For a normalized $K$ eigenvector $\psi_k$, $V(t)\psi_k$ has eigenvalue $k+t$. Thus $\|V(t)\psi_k-\psi_k\|=\sqrt 2$ for every $t\ne0$, contrary to strong continuity at zero. Even without regularity of $V$, the shift identity for all real $t$ makes $\operatorname{spec}K$ translation invariant, incompatible with a nonempty semibounded spectrum. If one instead chooses regular dilations of positive area, a nonzero exact eigenvalue still moves to orthogonal eigenstates for arbitrarily small nonzero dilation. These are tests of proposed zero-mode transformations. The paper only exhibits regular Weyl variables **at fixed total area**. Neither a nonzero area matrix element of shear nor its definite-frequency component is a global unitary translating the entire spectrum. The compression of a full source can also fail to be unitary on the allowed subspace. Thus a blanket Weyl-algebra contradiction would exceed the evidence. [Fixed-area Weyl relations](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS3.p2.1) · [Halvorson, theorem on sharp eigenstates and regular Weyl translations](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0110102)

In (D.13), the unnormalized rapidity integral yields a Dirac delta in the **continuous** spectral label $s_R-s_L$. Substituting equal discrete eigenvalues into that delta would give an undefined gauge volume. For atomic spectral labels the normalized mean $(2T)^{-1}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{i\alpha(s_L-s_R)/(2\pi)}d\alpha$ instead tends to $\mathbf 1_{s_L=s_R}$. This recovers the matching selection rule on atoms. Inferring a physical gluing measure or a unit multiplicity for each state across continuous and discrete spectral types needs an inner product with the corresponding spectral normalization. ## What a disconnected thermal cylinder tests

Grant a finite-dimensional diamond in a faithful state with weights $v_a$ and a projection of probability $p\in(0,1)$. For the independently evolving AdS component let $T=e^{-\beta(H-E_0)}$, $w_i=\langle i|T|i\rangle$, and $d_a=v_a(\mathbf1_I(A_a)-p)$. Then $\sum_a d_a=0$ and $\sum_a|d_a|=2p(1-p)$. Monotone summation of the positive terms gives the exact identity, allowing infinite values,

$$
\sum_{i,a}|w_i d_a|=2p(1-p)\operatorname{Tr}T .
$$

The sum over completed rows vanishes for every cutoff on $i$. A scalar family converges over the directed net of **all** finite subsets precisely when it is absolutely summable. Demanding this stronger form of sewing for (5.44) is therefore equivalent to thermal trace class at the specified $\beta$. Any two-outcome finite ancilla with $0<p<1$ gives the same test. Its exchange operator, the presence of area transitions, and the form of the area eigenvalues do not enter the sum. The state count of the disjoint component enters only through the assumed joint sewing rule. [Equations (5.42)–(5.46)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E42)

A diagnostic example takes $H=E_0I$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ and $p=v_1=v_2=1/2$. Every row is $(+1/4,-1/4)$. Completed rows sum to zero, while partial rows make arbitrary finite-subset sums unbounded. Evolution is unitary and every open matrix element obeys the paper's Cauchy–Schwarz sewing bound. The thermal trace is infinite. This distinguishes the open-state sewing identities (4.29)–(4.30) from the closed-cylinder assumption. Appendix E separately requires finite closures with **compact energy support**. Even finite-rank energy windows alone cannot imply an unrestricted thermal trace at fixed $\beta$. For $H|n\rangle=(\log n)|n\rangle$ in fixed energy units the windows are finite and $Z_\beta=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\beta}$ diverges for $0<\beta\le1$. An independent estimate on thermal preparation or growth of the boundary density of states would supply the missing input. [Open-state sewing](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS4.p3.1) · [Energy smearing in Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A5.I1.i5)

Given $\operatorname{Tr}T<\infty$ for even one $\beta>0$, $\mathbf1_{[E_0,E_0+\Lambda]}(H)\le e^{\beta\Lambda}T$, proving (5.46). Since $T$ is positive, injective and compact on a nonzero space, its largest eigenvalue gives a finite-multiplicity ground energy. Its positive eigenvalues yield a pure point spectrum of $H$, with finite multiplicities and possible accumulation only at $+\infty$. A minimal nonzero projection in the finite-dimensional ground-state corner of the boundary algebra acts as a rank-one projection on the cyclic sector of a chosen vector in its range. Multiplication by boundary operators on both sides then generates matrix units there as in (5.47)–(5.48). This conclusion concerns that cyclic sector. Trace class also gives a normal Gibbs KMS state for the Hamiltonian dynamics there at the admitted $\beta$. Identifying it with a geometrically prepared Hartle–Hawking state requires the Euclidean cylinder to implement this trace. Identifying a particular interior area-changing operator on that AdS sector requires its representation on the sector.
