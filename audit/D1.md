# Appendix D.1 — The one-loop determinant

**Physical question.** Does the paper's one-loop Gaussian expansion around a regular classical saddle reproduce the reduced Hessian identities, isolate source contact terms, and deliver a well-defined continuum determinant for the causal diamond's null region?

**Outcome.** At a regular saddle with finitely many modes, the Gaussian signs and reduced-Hessian identities in (D.1)–(D.12) reproduce cleanly, and the smooth four-dimensional Ricci-flat graviton-plus-ghost heat-kernel coefficient ($53/45$, yielding $(106/45)\log s_b+O(1)$ on a spherical cut) checks out analytically and numerically. Two substantive gaps remain. First, identifying the trace $\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}D_uD_vK)$ in (D.9) with the explicit source contact term $-\partial_j^2 H[j]$ fails even in a two-variable polynomial test because $D_u,D_v$ include motion of the saddle. Second, Appendix D.1 writes down a formal Hessian and measure without evaluating the Lorentzian null-region determinant—leaving boundary states, corners, residual symmetries, and ultraviolet matching between descriptions open. [Appendix D.1, pp. 68–70](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=68)

## Two different source derivatives

Equation (4.28) defines the explicit contact insertion as $-\partial_j^2 H[j]$ evaluated **at fixed canonical variables**. Immediately after (D.9), however, the manuscript identifies $\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}D_uD_v K)$ with that contact term while explicitly letting the derivatives $D_u,D_v$ act through the source-dependent saddle $x_*(u,v)$. Those two operations disagree even for two separate, purely linear sources. [Equation (4.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E28) · [Equation (D.9)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.E9)

Consider the two-variable quartic action

$$
I(x;u,v)=\tfrac12 x^TAx+x_1^4/24+ux_1+vx_2,\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

At zero sources, $x_*=0$ and $G=A^{-1}$, while differentiating the saddle equation gives $\partial_u x_{*,1}=-2/3$ and $\partial_v x_{*,1}=1/3$. Since the Hessian is $K=A+\operatorname{diag}(x_{*,1}^2/2,0)$,

$$
\left.\partial_u\partial_v I\right|_x=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(G\,D_uD_vK)=-4/27 .
$$

Because both first derivatives $D_u K$ and $D_v K$ vanish at $u=v=0$, this nonzero term is the entire trace contribution to the mixed two-point response at one loop; diagrammatically, it links the two linear sources through propagators and a quartic vertex. The mathematical identity (D.9) remains valid, but isolating the explicit contact insertion requires differentiating the source at fixed field and accounting separately for the saddle shift.

## Local graviton and ghost counts

To check the local one-loop coefficient later invoked in (5.67c), take the standard gauge-fixed second-order operators $P=-(\nabla^2+E)$ on symmetric tensors and vectors over a smooth four-dimensional Ricci-flat Euclidean manifold. Writing $X=R_{abcd}R^{abcd}$ and $\Omega_{ab}=[\nabla_a,\nabla_b]$, the interior $t^2$ heat-kernel coefficient after factoring out $(4\pi t)^{-2}$ is

$$
b_4=\frac{2\,\mathrm{rank}(P)\,X+180\operatorname{tr} E^2+
30\operatorname{tr}\Omega_{ab}\Omega^{ab}}{360}.
$$

For vectors $E=0$; for symmetric rank-two tensors $E h_{ab}=2R_{acbd}h^{cd}$, whose squared trace equals $3X$ by Riemann symmetries. Because a rotation acts on both indices of a symmetric tensor, its curvature trace is six times the vector trace in four dimensions. Complex vector ghosts contribute with weight $-2$ relative to a single real bosonic determinant. [Bastianelli, Bonezzi and Melis, Eqs. (31)–(47)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-022-11119-w)

| Bundle | Rank | $\operatorname{tr}E^2/X$ | $\operatorname{tr}\Omega^2/X$ | $b_4/X$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Symmetric tensor | 10 | 3 | −6 | $19/18$ |
| Vector | 4 | 0 | −1 | $-11/180$ |
| Tensor minus two vectors | 2 | 3 | −4 | $53/45$ |

These numbers are local fiber ranks and curvature traces: the net rank $10-2(4)=2$ counts local physical polarizations, not boundary-eigenfunction multiplicities. On a spherical cut with the conical-defect factor used in (5.67c), $8\pi\int_S R[q]\,dA=64\pi^2$, yielding the local logarithmic contribution $(212/45)\log r_S=(106/45)\log s_b+O(1)$. Both the arithmetic and sign check out. In Sen's fixed-mass Schwarzschild calculation, however, one also sums over angular momenta and treats collective-coordinate zero modes explicitly; carrying the local bulk coefficient over to the paper's trace requires establishing which boundary and zero-mode contributions survive the gravitational gluing. [Manuscript, Eq. (5.67)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.E67) · [Sen, §§2.3–2.4 and 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0971)

## What a spectral evaluation would require

For a positive elliptic operator $P$ with specified domain and discrete spectrum, zeta-function regularization defines $\zeta_P(z)=\sum_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{-z}$ by analytic continuation and $\log\det{}'(P/\mu^2)=-\zeta'_P(0)-2\zeta_P(0)\log\mu$. Rescaling $P\mapsto L^{-2}P$ shifts the log determinant by $-2\zeta_P(0)\log L$. In a standard smooth four-dimensional elliptic problem, $\zeta_P(0)$ equals the integrated constant heat-kernel coefficient minus the excluded zero-mode count; boundaries, corners, conical singularities, and nonelliptic Lorentzian operators all require additional justification. [Hawking, *Zeta function regularization of path integrals in curved spacetime*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01626516)

Boundary conditions alone can change the logarithmic scaling completely. Take $-d^2/dx^2$ on $[0,L]$: two Dirichlet endpoints give $\zeta(z)=(L/\pi)^{2z}\zeta_R(2z)$ and $\det P=2L$, whereas one Dirichlet and one Neumann endpoint give $\zeta(z)=(L/\pi)^{2z}(2^{2z}-1)\zeta_R(2z)$ and $\det P=2$. Both realizations share the exact same interior operator and have trivial kernel, yet one carries a $\log L$ term and the other does not.

Appendix D.1 supplies no corresponding spectrum or renormalized relative determinant for its Lorentzian first-order contour operator. A constructive verification would need a shared regulator between the first-order and metric descriptions, explicit initial-state and gluing domains, control of residual gauge and edge modes and measure Jacobians, and a controlled continuum limit. Subtracting the zero-source functional cancels only source-independent divergences: even a simple mass-squared shift in a flat four-dimensional elliptic model leaves ultraviolet summands $\log(1+\delta m^2/(k^2+m^2))=O(k^{-2})$, whose momentum integral still diverges. Consequently, §6.2's equality of one-loop functionals holds formally under matched regulator, measure, and boundary conditions, while whether the null-cone reduction realizes those hypotheses—or generates additional local counterterms—remains unverified. [End of §6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S6.SS2.p6.1)
