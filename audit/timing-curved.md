# Equal-time measurements by two clocks on a vacuum plane wave

Two spherical clocks can have a sharp equal-time bracket even when their diamonds overlap. On the symmetric vacuum plane wave, sufficiently short congruent clocks centered one radius apart have bracket **zero** for the preparation below. Two clocks on the **same emitter** with different durations and cap fractions also have a well-defined sharp pairing in a specified range. For arbitrary cap fractions and timing, a smooth finite-duration measurement always defines a mixed bracket when a timelike isometry sweeps its entire preparation. These are first derivatives of the nonlinear two-cone reading on a nonflat background. [Chandrasekaran, arXiv:2611.34924v1, Eqs. (3.26)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E27) · [The full metric differential](weak-curved.md)

## What a finite clock resolution measures

Work on the globally hyperbolic Rosen strip with sufficiently large transverse periods. With $a_1(u)=\cosh(\kappa u)$ and $a_2(u)=\cos(\kappa u)$, the metric is $-2du\,dv+\sum_i a_i^2 dx_i^2$. Use regular pre-caustic two-tip diamonds and whole-sky averages. At each marked launch event orthonormalize the timelike and longitudinal trial vectors, then select the regular boost branch giving the specified Weyl superenergy. Keep the proper duration or another regular geometric return control fixed. The resulting compact current $j=d\bar u_*$ includes the moving cap, waist, tips, observer and local calibration. It is conserved at this background because all calibrated launch marks with the same $V^u$ are related by local isometries. All brackets here use the causal inverse $E=G_{\rm ret}-G_{\rm adv}$ of the gauge-fixed Einstein–Hilbert Hessian on the surrounding strip. [Calibration and Ward identity, Eqs. (4)–(6)](weak-curved.md) · [Normalized tensor kernel, Eq. (3a)](weak-rosen-green.md)

Transport the **whole** marked experiment by a local background isometry $\Phi_s$, including the trial vectors and any return constraint. Then $j_s=(\Phi_s)_* j$. Suppose its generator $K$ is timelike on a neighborhood of the swept compact preparation, with all translates defined there. For $w\in C_c^\infty$ supported in this time range, define $j_w=\int w(s)j_s\,ds$. The averaged current remains conserved and satisfies
$$
 \mathrm{WF}(j_w)\subset\{(x,\xi):\xi(K_x)=0\},\qquad
 E j_w\in C^\infty.
 \tag{1}
$$
Indeed apply distributional pushforward to $w(s)\,ds\otimes j$ under $(s,y)\mapsto\Phi_s y$. A target wavefront covector must pull back to zero in the smooth parameter component, which gives the first inclusion. No nonzero null covector annihilates a timelike vector. The causal propagator transports only null covectors, giving the second conclusion. The remaining wavefront of the *source* may be spacelike. Smoothing the *response* already defines $j_B(Ej_w)$ for another compact sharp clock. These arguments use local isometries on the preparation and the global propagator on the strip. [Pushforward wavefront theorem, §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.7662v2) · [Characteristic relation of the causal propagator, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15062v2)

Such a proper-time motion exists for the previously measured $\kappa=L=1$ diamond. In Brinkmann coordinates $X_i=a_i x_i$ and $v_B=v+\frac12\sum_i(a_i'/a_i)X_i^2$, take $K=(\partial_u+\partial_{v_B})/\sqrt2$. It is Killing and has unit timelike norm on the central observer. For tips $(u,v)=(0,0),(L,L)$ at $x=0$, the two cone inequalities imply
$$
 X_1^2\le 2L\frac{\sinh(\kappa u)\sinh(\kappa(L-u))}
                         {\kappa\sinh(\kappa L)}
 \le \frac{L}{\kappa}\tanh(\kappa L/2).
$$
The first bound follows by eliminating $v$ in the inequalities $\sum x_i^2/I_i\le2v$ and $\sum x_i^2/(I_i(L)-I_i)\le2(L-v)$ with $I_i=\int_0^u a_i^{-2}$, taking the tip limits by continuity. Thus $g(K,K)=-1+\kappa^2(X_1^2-X_2^2)/2\le-1+\tfrac12\tanh(1/2)<0$ for the quoted unit parameters. Small proper-time displacements stay within an injective patch. A transverse translation conjugates this construction to a second emitter. If a global flow is desired, take a smooth convex combination with a global future timelike field, equal to the local Killing field on the swept support. Convexity of the future timelike cone preserves timelikeness. The local flow already suffices for (1). For any fixed smooth probe on which the original sharp reading is nonzero, convergence $j_{w_h}(Ef)\to j(Ef)$ preserves its nonzero response for probability windows supported sufficiently close to zero. [Two-cone inequalities](joint-focal.md) · [Nonzero gravitational probe, Eq. (7)](weak-curved.md)

More generally let $P_{AB}(s,t)$ denote the two-time bracket of two independently swept currents, interpreted as a distribution. Pull the kernel $E(x,y)$ back to $(\Phi_s^A x,\Phi_t^B y)$, contract it with $j_A(x)\otimes j_B(y)$ and integrate in $x,y$. The maps are submersions. Each kernel wavefront has two nonzero null legs. Its parameter components evaluate these legs on the timelike generators. They cannot be canceled by the currents, which are independent of $s,t$ in this representation. The distribution product and the proper pushforward therefore exist, including for a joint smooth timing density. For factorized windows its value is exactly the bracket of the averaged currents. Skewness gives $P_{AB}(s,t)=-P_{BA}(t,s)$. A stationary delay law additionally requires common-translation covariance of the experiment **and its causal kernel**. Local isometries need not descend through the transverse periods. In an injective causal hull with no contributing periodic images, covariance follows from the isometries of the covering wave and uniqueness of its local retarded and advanced kernels on a causally compatible neighborhood. [Distribution products and pushforwards](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.7662v2) · [Rosen images and causal support](weak-rosen-green.md)

## Locating pairs with a sharp value

A sufficient condition is that no null geodesic, with its parallel covectors, simultaneously meets characteristic conormals of strata of the two currents. Include zero separation in this condition. To identify the strata, use the metric differential on the outgoing and returning faces, split the outgoing integral at its cap and include the observer integral and the point preparation. Away from the vertices these are pushforwards of finite jets with smooth coefficients on each smooth face or curve with boundary. Their wavefront lies in the conormals of the face interiors, the cap, the waist and the observer curve or in the full cotangent fibers of the two tips. The observer interior has only spacelike conormals. This bound includes derivatives of the metric at a vertex, the shift of the returning cone and the edge terms of differentiation. [Explicit differential, Eqs. (1)–(4)](weak-curved.md) · [Two-source domain, Eq. (2)](weak-domain.md)

With this separation the product $j_A(Ej_B)$ exists at the stated timing. Separation throughout a compact neighborhood of $(s,t)=(0,0)$ gives a smooth $P_{AB}$ there. In the preceding pushforward, a nonzero output covector in the parameters would require cancellation of *both* spacetime components by conormals of the currents. That is precisely a shared null ray. Thus any pair of smooth probability windows supported at shrinking offsets converges to the same $P_{AB}(0,0)$, irrespective of relative widths or shapes. The same conclusion holds for joint probability windows supported in a shrinking neighborhood.

There is a simple geometric test for strictly separated directions on a weakly curved diamond. Start with a flat round clock of half-duration $R$, midpoint $(T,\mathbf x)$ and cap fraction $0<a<1$. Every possible characteristic conormal of its strata lies on a null line through one of three events on its emitter, at times
$$
 \mathcal T(T,R,a)=\{T-R,\; T+(2a-1)R,\;T+R\}.                 \tag{2}
$$
The two faces supply generators through their respective tips. The waist sphere has two radial null normals, one in each of those cones. The cap sphere at time $T-R+aR$ and radius $aR$ has an outgoing normal through the past tip and an inward normal through the middle event in (2). A point marking supplies every null direction through its own tip. These facts give an upper bound even if particular tensor contractions remove some characteristic directions.

For two emitters at flat spatial separation $d$ a sufficient strict inequality is
$$
 \min_{t_i\in\mathcal T_A,\;t_j\in\mathcal T_B}
              \big|d-|t_i-t_j|\big|>0.                         \tag{3}
$$
Any shared null line would connect a pair of the listed events, including coincident events. The test only uses the limiting *geometry* of the sources. The Weyl-superenergy calibration is degenerate at zero curvature. For every nonzero $\kappa$ choose its regular value for the indicated emitter, $w_*=\kappa^4$ for $V^u=1/\sqrt2$. The launch, cap and waist support strata vary smoothly to the flat geometry, even though the derivative of the boost calibration need not stay bounded as $\kappa\to0$.

Here is the stability argument transferring (3) to a curved vacuum. Fix the durations and positions in a common bounded Rosen coordinate patch and take $\kappa R_{\max}$ sufficiently small, or rescale all dimensions down together on a fixed nonflat wave. Use future timelike central geodesics at fixed Rosen transverse coordinates and synchronize their midpoints in $u,v$. The cone intersections remain transverse and the fractional cap slope stays positive by continuity on the physical launch sky. The smooth compact sky parametrizations of the caps and waists and their derivatives converge. On the face interiors the only null conormals are along the generators. Their limits at a tip lie in its full cotangent fiber and at an edge in its conormals. The same containment holds for limits of normalized edge and tip conormals. To see that no propagation can reintroduce a shared line, suppose there were such a line along a sequence tending to the flat parameters. Normalize null directions on a slice of the cotangent cone, for instance by giving their future tangents unit coordinate time speed. Between two compact source points the connecting causal segment stays in a common bounded patch: $u,v$ are monotone and the integrated causal inequality gives $|\Delta x_i|\le\sqrt{2\Delta u\Delta v}/\min a_i$ throughout that $u,v$ range. Choose periods large enough to exclude winding there. In the time parameter $(u+v)/\sqrt2$ the null directions form a compact set and the geodesic equations depend smoothly on the metric. A subsequence converges to a segment, possibly of zero length, whose directions lie in the limiting conormals of both preparations. Its extension is a flat common null line, contrary to (3). The same proof allows small independent timing offsets. Smooth curvature tails inside the light cone enter the value of the bracket and obey the same wavefront bound. ## Two overlapping experiments

*Two locations, equal clocks.* Choose equal $R$, $a=1/3$ and common launch coordinates $u=v=0$. The return coordinates are $u=v=L=\sqrt2 R$. Put the two parallel emitter geodesics at $x_1=\pm R/[2a_1(L/2)],\ x_2=0$, so their transverse separation at the simultaneous midpoints measures $R$ along that coordinate line. Use the same proper duration $2R$ and reflected trial frames. In the flat limit the possible differences in (3) are $0,2R/3,4R/3,2R$ and $d=R$, giving margin $R/3$. The interiors overlap with room to spare, a property that persists at small curvature. At sufficiently small **nonzero** $\kappa R$ the sharp mixed bracket exists by (3) and stability. The time-orientation-preserving Rosen isometry $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(-x_1,-x_2)$ induces a rotation by $\pi$ at the center, descends to the torus and exchanges the preparations. It preserves the Einstein kernel. Hence $j_A(Ej_B)=j_B(Ej_A)$. Skew adjointness then gives
$$
                  j_A(Ej_B)=0 .                                       \tag{4}
$$
This exact value is attained in the limit with independent probability windows. At finite width reflection-compatible sweeps and a swap-invariant joint timing law also give zero. Each clock responds to vacuum perturbations on this background: in the compensated cap-pulse calculation $F(u)=\sqrt{\sinh(\kappa u)\sin(\kappa u)}/\kappa$ and $Z'(c)=\kappa[\coth(\kappa c)-\cot(\kappa c)]>0$ for the short diamonds. All remaining factors in the magnitude of the focal coefficient are strictly positive. The pulse can be launched after the local calibration and both emitter geodesics have the stated fixed duration. [Focal response, Eqs. (3)–(5)](joint-focal.md)

*One location, different durations and cap fractions.* Choose launches on one emitter geodesic whose unperturbed proper-time midpoints coincide. Each experiment uses its own local launch calibration. No extra midpoint mark is imposed on the perturbed metrics. Let the large clock have half-duration $R$ and $a=1/3$. Give the smaller clock half-duration $R/5$ and, for example, cap fraction $2/3$. The large clock's three events lie at midpoint offsets $-R,-R/3,R$. Every point of the small closed flat diamond satisfies $|t-T|+|\mathbf y-\mathbf x|\le R/5$. Thus the small support lies strictly inside the timelike region of each of the three events, with a margin of at least $2R/15$ in this inequality. No null-normal ray of the large source reaches any point in a neighborhood of the small diamond. The same compactness argument preserves this stronger separation at sufficiently small nonzero curvature. Consequently $Ej_{\rm large}$ is a **smooth metric variation on the entire small preparation**. Its sharp bracket $j_{\rm small}(E j_{\rm large})$ is given directly by evaluating the two-cone differential on that local smooth field. Changing the smaller cap fraction within $(0,1)$ leaves the support argument valid. This analysis gives existence and timing-window independence. The numerical value for these unequal clocks has not been evaluated. [Metric differential, Eq. (4)](weak-curved.md) · [Tensor kernel, Eq. (3a)](weak-rosen-green.md)

For coincident tips, or for durations and displacements on the resonances excluded by (3), this proof provides no transverse separation. The documented axial wavefront on the unit Rosen example actually obstructs the sufficient product criterion for that clock paired with its own field at the cap pole. An equal-time rule for a general pair of distinct preparations on the same tips still requires the contracted singular coefficients or a specified timing prescription. The jump computed for a flat clock Hessian and the nonzero response to a smooth probe on a curved background do not supply that calculation. These are proof gaps for extensions of the sharp clock algebra. [Curved axial wavefront, Eq. (9)](weak-curved.md) · [Flat translated Hessian](weak-clock-contact.md)
