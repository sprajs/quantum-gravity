# Which cap degrees of freedom does the incoming clock see?

A round, truncated incoming cone can have the same area and affine clock reading for a continuous family of inequivalent vacuum interiors. On a marked cap with freely varying Cauchy data, two variations in this family have a nonzero Einstein symplectic product. In linearized gravity a smaller patch inside the cap even supports a canonical pair commuting with the algebra measured on the truncated sheet. The full nonlinear theory has an obstruction to keeping the surrounding geometry exactly flat. Whether the physical quantum clock has an independent cap pair in its commutant remains a **proof gap**. [Venkatesa Chandrasekaran, arXiv:2611.34924v1, phase space (3.19)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E19) · [Capped affine clock (3.27) and average (3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E27)

## A canonical pair in the interior of the flat cap

In Minkowski space take the initial ball $t=a$, $|\mathbf x|\le a$, with rim $C$, and take the sheet $|\mathbf y|=t$ from $t=a$ to the waist $t=R$. For $|\mathbf x|\le a-d$ on the cap, the separation from every sheet point satisfies
$$
 |\mathbf y-\mathbf x|^2-(t-a)^2\ \ge\ 2d(t-a)+d^2>0 .
$$
A sufficiently small open spacetime neighborhood on the future side of this compact cap patch is still spacelike to the whole closed sheet, including $C$ and the waist. This uses a finite positive cap radius. A point tip has no such interior.

On the flat slice, put $P_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\Delta-\partial_i\partial_j$ and take
$$
 T_{ij}=(P_{ik}P_{jl}-\tfrac12 P_{ij}P_{kl})S_{kl}\phi,
 \qquad S=\operatorname{diag}(-1/2,-1/2,1),\qquad N=\int T:T\,d^3x.
$$
Choose a nonzero smooth radial bump $\phi$ in the patch, centered on any interior point. The tensor is divergence-free and trace-free. In Fourier variables the angular average of its squared multiplier is $3|k|^8/5$, so $N=(3/5)\int |\Delta^2\phi|^2d^3x>0$. For the ADM metric perturbation $h$ and its momentum density $\pi$, including the factor $1/(16\pi G_N)$ in $\pi$, set
$$
 Q=N^{-1/2}\int T^{ij}h_{ij}\,d^3x,\qquad
 P=N^{-1/2}\int T_{ij}\pi^{ij}\,d^3x . \tag{1}
$$
The linearized spatial gauge variation of $h$ is a symmetrized gradient. A lapse changes $\pi$ by a multiple of $(\partial_i\partial_j-\delta_{ij}\Delta)$ applied to that lapse. The flat slice has zero extrinsic curvature. Integration by parts thus gives zero variation for both observables, including changes of the coordinates used to mark the patch at this order. Their Hamiltonian data have the same compact support and obey the constraints. The ADM bracket gives $\{Q,P\}=1$ on the gauge quotient. Against arbitrary smooth linearized tests the joined cap and sheet give the same pair of Hamilton equations, since these packets vanish on a collar of the sheet and all its cuts. [Cap TT packets and the metric current](rim-potential.md) · [Angular normalization of the TT symbol](weak-master.md)

Both preparations have smooth compact spacetime sources. In TT gauge let $u_{ij}$ be the wave with either of the specified Hamiltonian data on the cap, with $u_{0\mu}=0$. Choose a time step $\chi$ rising smoothly from zero to one in a short interval just after $a$. For $D=\partial_t^2-\Delta$ the tensor $j=D(\chi u)=\chi''u+2\chi'\partial_tu$ is compact by finite propagation and conserved by spatial transversality. Its retarded and advanced solutions are $\chi u$ and $(\chi-1)u$. Thus its causal response is $u$, up to the conventional conversion from metric sources to Einstein units. The transition interval can be chosen so the support of $j$ lies wholly inside the capped region, spacelike to the closed incoming sheet. This construction uses the free vacuum equation on a fixed background and tests the physical polarizations. [Linearized gravitational source algebra](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0261)

In the Weyl algebra of the free metric, exponentiating (1) gives $U(s)V(t)=e^{-i\hbar st}V(t)U(s)$ for $U(s)=e^{isQ}$, $V(t)=e^{itP}$. Sheet observables whose linear smearings have physical support on the retained sheet and cuts have zero symplectic pairing with both packets. Their bounded Weyl operators therefore commute exactly. In any representation, a self-adjoint clock affiliated with the von Neumann algebra generated by such sheet observables has every spectral projection in that algebra. One sufficient construction is a limit in the strong resolvent sense of self-adjoint clocks affiliated with this same algebra. This includes nonlinear functions of the free sheet fields when their definition has the stated affiliation.

For a projection $P_I$ commuting with both families on a common physical area sector, any nonzero range has infinite dimension. Indeed, on a finite range of dimension $n>0$ the two restricted unitaries would satisfy the same relation and taking determinants would give $e^{-in\hbar st}=1$ for all $s,t$. This argument needs neither a tensor factorization nor a special state. It does require commutation of the bounded unitaries with the projection and a nonzero window. The free calculation does not establish either hypothesis for the physical clock (4.27). ## A second-order obstruction to a strictly localized vacuum displacement

Consider an exact vacuum curve with vanishing cosmological constant through the flat cap, initially tangent to the momentum packet. Write $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\lambda^2 b_{ij}+O(\lambda^3)$ and $K_{ij}=\lambda T_{ij}+O(\lambda^2)$, allowing a constant rescaling of (1). The Hamiltonian constraint $R[g]+(\operatorname{tr}_g K)^2-K:K=0$ at order $\lambda^2$ gives
$$
 \oint_C n_i(\partial_j b_{ij}-\partial_i b_{jj})\,dA
 =\int_{\Sigma_0} T:T\,d^3x=N>0. \tag{2}
$$
Keeping the complete Cauchy data flat in a collar of $C$ contradicts (2). This also follows nonperturbatively for a smooth vacuum ball with exactly Euclidean metric and zero extrinsic curvature on that collar. Extending by Euclidean data gives a complete asymptotically flat vacuum slice with zero ADM four-momentum. Positive-mass rigidity puts its development in Minkowski space. The conclusion concerns physical curvature, allowing slices of Minkowski space with nonzero extrinsic curvature elsewhere. The restriction on the *full* collar is stronger than fixing its area and the normalized clock. [Huang and Lee, rigidity for complete asymptotically flat initial data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06040)

## An exact symplectic family with constant area and clock

Corvino's time-symmetric vacuum gluing gives smooth nonflat scalar-flat Cauchy data on $\mathbb R^3$ agreeing with a Schwarzschild slice outside a compact set. Choose such a filling, with positive geometric mass $m_0$ and $K=0$. Spatial scaling gives $g_\lambda(x)=g(x/\lambda)$ in components in a scaled Cartesian chart, with mass $m=\lambda m_0$. For an interval of scales choose one fixed areal radius $a$ in their common exterior, beyond the transition and $2m$. Start the retained outgoing radial null sheet there, with fixed marked angles, and end it at a common areal radius $R>a$. The exterior domain of dependence contains the sheet since the inner edge of the known exterior lies strictly inside the initial rim. For a full capped cobordism, choose $R-a$ sufficiently small. In a common short-time development the inward future null sheet from the waist reaches $r=a$ while still in the exterior collar and a spacelike final ball fills its rim. The same construction works for nearby scales and small time translations below. This realizes the fixed positive ratio $\epsilon=a/R$ and the two regular intersecting **truncated** sheets of (3.18) with freely specified, independently marked cap data. The gluing theorem controls the retained Schwarzschild collar. It supplies no optical matching inside the nonspherical filling that would prolong the outgoing generators back to a single regular emitter, or prolong the other sheet forward to a regular return vertex. A preparation demanding both point tips or fixing their worldline requires separate existence and matching proofs. [Corvino, scalar curvature deformation and gluing](https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00005533) · [v1 cap conditions (3.18)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E18)

The induced screen is $r^2 d\Omega^2$ and its radial shear vanishes. Thus $\mathscr A=4\pi R^2$ and $\bar u_*=R-a$ for every mass. An outgoing affine tangent normalized by a local static observer at $a$ has $dr/du=\sqrt{1-2m/a}$ throughout the ray. This factor cancels in the normalized reading $p_{\epsilon,y}\Delta u$. A curvature measurement at the initial rim gives $C_{abcd}C^{abcd}=48m^2/a^6$ and distinguishes the solutions with their marked caps. At the waist the invariant product of null expansions is $\theta_\ell\theta_n=-2(1-2m/R)/R^2$ for $\ell\cdot n=-1$. Fixing this product as well as the area fixes the mass. The family therefore varies transverse boundary data while preserving the clock and area. [Exterior curvature convention](weak-schwarzschild.md) · [v1 clock normalization (3.26)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E27)

There is an independent time variation. In a common chart pull back the bulk metric by a small time translation $\tau$ generated by $\xi$, equal to the Schwarzschild Killing vector throughout the exterior collar and extended smoothly through the ball. Keep the abstract cap and angular marks fixed when pulling the fields back. This moves the cap relative to the interior solution. The exterior metric and pulled-back exterior frames can be chosen stationary, so every displayed clock and curvature there retains its value along this variation. In outgoing Schwarzschild coordinates $u,r$, the vector is $\xi=\partial_u$ for all masses. The Einstein current identity on the cap, oriented by $\Omega(\delta,\mathcal L_\xi)=\delta H_\xi$, gives
$$
 \Omega_{\Sigma_0}(\partial_m,\partial_\tau)
 =\int_C(\partial_m Q_\xi-i_\xi\theta[\partial_m g])
 =\frac{1}{2G_N}+\frac{1}{2G_N}=\frac1{G_N}. \tag{3}
$$
Here $Q_\xi$ is the gravitational Noether charge. In the collar $ds^2=-(1-2m/r)du^2-2\,du\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$. The Noether integral equals $m/(2G_N)$ and the metric potential supplies the other half. The cap has no inner boundary. The sheet and joint terms pair to zero since the time variation and its frame variations vanish on their collars. Equivalently, evaluate the conserved mixed-surface current on a spacelike ball ending at the waist, where the same stationary exterior charge is obtained. Thus the marked Einstein family has local two-form $dm\wedge d\tau/G_N$. With boundary proper time the lapse is rescaled. At $r=a$ the proper-time energy $a(1-\sqrt{1-2m/a})/G_N$ has differential $dm/(G_N\sqrt{1-2m/a})$. Equation (3) uses the exterior Schwarzschild normalization. [Explicit cut-current contraction](03-4.md) · [Iyer and Wald, on-shell current identity (75)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9403028#page=14)

A simultaneous diffeomorphism of fields and embedding is identified in (3.19). The relative time shift above keeps the external embedding fixed and admits a varying mass charge. If a boundary prescription instead fixes that charge, fixes the cap state, or quotients a common displacement on an internal interface, the calculation on the free marked family does not prescribe its reduced bracket. The clock is constant on this symplectic family. Restricting a function to this family before quantization gives no commutation identity for its spectral projections on the full phase space. The allowable masses here satisfy $0<2m<a$ and the time translations were local. In particular a unitary translating this bounded mass by every real amount could not preserve this family.

Section 4.2 of v1 lists the two incoming *null* polarizations and corner variables in its fixed-area Gaussian. If these canonical Weyl generators exhaust the incoming product representation, their joint commutant there is scalar. Indeed, the one-mode Weyl algebras generate the individual bounded-operator factors. The decreasing products of the first $n$ one-mode Gaussian projectors converge strongly to the vacuum line on the incomplete tensor product (4.8). Acting with finite-mode matrix units generates every matrix unit between finite excitations. An independent pair commuting with *all* those generators would therefore require a larger representation or a change in their physical identification. Commutation with the clock alone is a weaker condition. [Product inner product (4.8)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E8)

The map from independent cap Cauchy data in (3.19) to the observable algebra or representation is still needed. A source dressed by rays from the waist, or a clock requiring an emitter worldline, a return event, or a field-dependent identification away from the incoming sheet, can have support outside the spacelike-separated sets used above. Equation (3.27) uses the normalized intrinsic incoming data once the marks and the waist are given. A second instrument specifying those marks through dynamical propagation must include the corresponding responses. A rank theorem would require an actual pair of area-preserving unitaries in the physical representation and proof that they preserve the same clock form domain and values, or its bounded spectral projections directly. Equation (2) explains why free local gauge invariance alone cannot supply this step. [Gaussian states (4.7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E7) · [Gravitational dressing and locality](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.94.104038) · [Two-tip clock source](weak-boundary.md)

## Reproduce the checks

Run `python3 -B checks/cap_interior_algebra.py` from the repository root, and `make check` for the suite. The focused calculation integrates the TT components on the compact radial test $(1-r^2)^8_+$ and independently computes its norm using the Fourier angular average. This piecewise polynomial has sufficient derivatives for both integrals. The test solves the radial monopole Poisson equation for the polynomial source, matches its derivative to the harmonic exterior at the packet radius and verifies its flux. It recovers the mass from the two waist expansions and differentiates the finite-radius energy numerically at two example exterior masses, in units $G_N=1$. The exterior test uses $a=2$, $R=5/2$ and checks local formulas without constructing the glued interiors numerically. [Executable calculation](../checks/cap_interior_algebra.py)

TT norm divided by 4 pi = 105226698752/5311735
Canonical normalization from the TT norm; solved boundary flux / TT norm = 1
m=1/8: clock=1/2, rim Weyl square=3/256, G dH/dm=1
m=1/4: clock=1/2, rim Weyl square=3/64, G dH/dm=1
~~~
