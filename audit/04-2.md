# Section 4.2 and Appendix C.1 — Hilbert space and physical inner product

**Physical question.** How does the paper build a fixed-area Hilbert space for incoming null-cone data, extend that state to the full gravitational observable algebra, and extract boost-filtered area sectors and transition probabilities?

**Outcome.** The fixed-area Gaussian Fock space of (4.7)–(4.10) is rigorously defined, and in the stated Schrödinger realization the finite-mode sources of Appendix C.3 generate all bounded operators $\mathcal B(\mathcal K_{\mathscr A})$ in the strong closure. Once the incoming state is established on a genuine $C^*$-subalgebra of a larger unital $C^*$-algebra, a pure state extension exists and yields $\pi_\omega(\mathcal A)''=\mathcal B(\mathcal H_\omega)$ in (4.17) from purity alone. What remains open is realizing the full continuum gravitational source algebra (including nonlinear and continuum relations), establishing continuity of the boost action after area-changing insertions, and determining cross-area transition amplitudes. The author explicitly selects one pure extension; the argument establishes algebraic existence under the stated hypotheses rather than uniqueness or a concrete continuum construction. [§4.2 source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2) · [Appendix C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS1) · [Appendix C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS3)

## 1. Gaussian normalization, ladder operators, and completeness (4.7)–(4.10)

For a single canonical mode centered at $(q^0,p^0)$ with position width $d=\Delta q>0$ and displacement $x=q-q^0$, the squared modulus of the Gaussian wavefunction is the normalized normal density of mean $0$ and variance $d^2$. Direct differentiation yields

$$
(p-p^0)\Omega=i\hbar\frac{x}{2d^2}\Omega,\qquad
a\Omega=0,\qquad
[a,a^\dagger]=1,\qquad
(\Delta p)^2=\frac{\hbar^2}{4d^2}.
$$

These relations hold on the displaced Schwartz core, and commuting $a$ past $a^\dagger$ gives $\|(a^\dagger)^n\Omega\|^2=n!$. The prose before (4.10) omits the normalization factor, while (4.10a) correctly divides by $\sqrt{n!}$. Phase multiplication, translation, and rescaling map the standard Hermite orthonormal basis directly onto the basis used in the paper.

Across infinitely many modes, overlaps factorize on product vectors that differ from the reference Gaussian in only finitely many slots. By definition (4.8), finite linear combinations of finitely supported occupation vectors are dense. Grouping them by total occupation number identifies the fixed-area space with the symmetric Fock space $\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\operatorname{Sym}^m\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ (with $m=0$ the vacuum)—the incomplete infinite tensor product in the chosen reference sector. Constructing it uses only the finite-dimensional integrals in (4.8), and the translation-invariant measure argument preceding (4.7) holds for the standard locally finite Borel measure on each real coordinate. [Derivation in the manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p2.1)

## 2. Irreducibility at fixed area (4.11), (C.18)–(C.22)

To show that the incoming algebra acts irreducibly on $\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$, Appendix C.3 builds rank-one operators from finite-mode Weyl symbols. For Schwartz functions $u,v$ on $\mathbb R^n$, substituting (C.19a) into the midpoint Weyl kernel and Fourier-inverting recovers the rank-one kernel exactly:

$$
K(x,x')=(2\pi\hbar)^{-n}\int d^np\,e^{ip\cdot(x-x')/\hbar}
 f_{uv}((x+x')/2,p)=u(x)\overline{v(x')}.
$$

A real normalized diagonal symbol produces the projection $P_u=|u\rangle\langle u|$, and since $e^{i\pi P_u}=1-2P_u$, equation (C.19c) recovers $P_u$ from a bounded unitary source with the printed sign and factor. Polarization yields the off-diagonal matrix units from real diagonal sources (equivalently, differentiating the norm-continuous exponentials of the real and imaginary parts recovers their bounded self-adjoint generators). Equation (C.18c) follows by substituting (C.4) in both factors and canceling $S_{I_0}(V)$.

On any finite-excitation product vector, the partial vacuum projection $E_N$ agrees with the full vacuum projector $P_\Omega$ as soon as $N$ covers its excited modes. Density and the uniform bound $\|E_N\|=1$ extend this to strong operator convergence on all of $\mathcal K_{\mathscr A}$. Convergence fails in operator norm—$\|E_N-P_\Omega\|=1$ for every finite $N$, as witnessed by a single excitation in mode $N+1$—but strong convergence suffices: sandwiching the strong limit yields all matrix units on a dense set of vectors, so the von Neumann closure is all of $\mathcal B(\mathcal K_{\mathscr A})$. Hence $\Omega$ is cyclic and its state on this represented incoming algebra is pure. Compressing on both sides by an orthogonal clock projection in (C.22) preserves this statement on the range whenever that projection exists, and (C.21) accurately retains the cross term through the orthogonal complement when compressing a product. [Source operator construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS3.p1.1)

What this proves is irreducibility for the concrete family of finite-mode operators. Passing to the universal algebra of all admissible gravitational source words in §4.1 requires showing that the Gaussian state respects every algebraic relation and the inherited $C^*$-norm. Constructing concrete finite-mode symbols does not verify relations involving continuum or nonlinear gravitational generators, nor does it prove that the finite-mode algebra embeds with its universal norm. The cited Buchholz–Fredenhagen paper constructs its dynamical algebra for scalar fields and explicitly distinguishes algebraic existence from the existence of physical states. [Universal norm in §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS1.p5.1) · [Buchholz and Fredenhagen, abstract and introduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06062)

## 3. The two continuum choices

Moving from finite-mode Fock spaces to a continuum gravitational state involves two distinct choices, each carrying physical content.

**Choice of covariance.** Separable smearing spaces admit countable bases, yet global canonical coordinates on the nonlinear reduced phase space and a continuous covariance on physical test functions are extra inputs. Section 3 pulls back fields via $\iota$ and writes the symplectic potential in canonical form. Picking widths $d_j>0$ mode by mode defines cylindrical finite-mode states, but for a centered smeared field $Q(f)=\sum_j f_j(q_j-q_j^0)$ to act on the vacuum, one needs $\sum_j |f_j|^2 d_j^2<\infty$ (and $\sum_j |f_j|^2\hbar^2/(4d_j^2)<\infty$ for its conjugate momentum). Unrestricted positive widths do not guarantee this: in a Fourier basis, smooth test coefficients $f_j=e^{-j}$ paired with widths $d_j=e^j$ (in fixed units) give a truncated norm squared equal to $N$ on the vacuum, diverging as $N\to\infty$. This does not rule out a well-chosen physical covariance—§4.4 specifies one particular short-distance scaling for its radial example—but it shows that an explicit ultraviolet choice is needed. [Reduced coordinates](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS3.p6.1) · [Moving embedding](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS4.p3.1) · [Radial specialization](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS4.p8.1)

Even among regular oscillator representations, changing widths across infinitely many modes generally yields a unitarily inequivalent representation. Scaling all widths at fixed centers by $d'_j=c d_j$ gives an $N$-mode vacuum overlap $[2c/(1+c^2)]^{N/2}$, and each mode has Bogoliubov creation coefficient of magnitude $|\sinh(\log c)|$. For $c=2$, the truncated squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the creation map is $9N/16$, which diverges. By Shale's theorem, no unitary operator intertwines the canonical commutation relations of these two Fock representations (a stronger obstruction than mere orthogonality of the vacua). [Shale, theorem 4.1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1962-0137504-6)

**Choice of extension.** For any unital $C^*$-inclusion $B\subseteq\mathcal A$, the extensions of a pure state on $B$ form a nonempty weak-$*$ compact face of the state space of $\mathcal A$, whose extreme points are pure extensions. Because inner products agree on $B$, the fixed-area Gaussian Fock space embeds isometrically into the resulting GNS space $\mathcal H_\omega$. Purity gives a scalar commutant, so $\pi_\omega(\mathcal A)''=\mathcal B(\mathcal H_\omega)$, and the embedded Fock space ensures that $\mathcal H_\omega$ is infinite-dimensional prior to imposing the clock cutoff. Purity determines the von Neumann type of the full algebra in the chosen representation, but it fixes neither the type of local subalgebras nor any finite trace of the identity. [Manuscript (4.16)–(4.17)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.E16) · [Kadison and Singer, pp. 383–384](https://www.isibang.ac.in/~soumyashant/misc/collected-works-of-kadison/1950s/1959_Extensions%20of%20Pure%20States.pdf)

Crucially, the Hahn–Banach / Krein–Milman extension theorem does not fix expectations outside $B$. In the elementary inclusion $B=\mathcal B(K)\otimes 1\subset\mathcal B(K\otimes\mathbb C^2)$, the vectors $\Omega\otimes e_1$ and $\Omega\otimes e_2$ induce the exact same pure state on $B$ while assigning probabilities $1$ and $0$ to $1\otimes|e_1\rangle\langle e_1|$. For the gravitational inclusion, the paper provides neither an explicit formula for the extension on general words nor a uniqueness or independence theorem; this toy model shows what the general theorem leaves undetermined without claiming to count extensions in gravity itself.

## 4. Boosts, dynamics, and transition probabilities (4.12)–(4.19)

If corner boosts are implemented by a strongly continuous unitary group $U(\eta)=\exp[i\eta\widehat{\mathscr A}/C]$ with $C=8\pi G_N\hbar$, then on an area eigenvector $i$ with eigenvalue $\mathscr A_i$,

$$
\alpha_\eta(a)i=\exp[i\eta(\widehat{\mathscr A}-\mathscr A_i)/C]ai .
$$

This reproduces the positive sign in (4.12), the area shift $Ck$ for an eigenfrequency $k$, and the spectral filter in (4.14). The squared error of the cutoff approximation equals the integral of $|\widetilde g_m((A-\mathscr A_i)/C)-\mathbf 1_J(A)|^2$ against the spectral measure of $ai$; bounded convergence proves convergence in vector norm, including the stated endpoint conventions at spectral atoms. For any countable disjoint spectral partition, completeness and $a^*a=1$ yield (4.15).

Here regularity is an extra physical requirement. Continuity of $U(\eta)$ on incoming fixed-area vectors follows automatically from their scalar boost phase, but (4.14) also requires strong continuity on $ai$ after area-changing insertions—something purity and the group law do not guarantee. As a minimal counterexample, take $U(\eta)=1\oplus\lambda(\eta)$ on $\mathbb C\oplus\ell^2(\mathbb R_{\rm discrete})$, where $\lambda(\eta)\delta_x=\delta_{x+\eta}$. The vector $1\oplus 0$ defines a pure state on the boost algebra and extends as a pure vector state to all bounded operators, yet a unitary can map it to $\delta_0$, whose boost orbit stays at distance $\sqrt 2$ from $\delta_0$ for every $\eta\ne 0$. To invoke the area spectral theorem in the gravitational model, one must establish strong continuity of the represented boosts on a dense set of source-prepared vectors (or construct the self-adjoint generator directly), and separately supply physical input for the sign of its spectrum. [Boost and filtering formulas](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p5.1)

For any unitary element $U_{\mathscr M}$, equation (4.18) is exact because $\omega(b^*U_{\mathscr M}^*U_{\mathscr M}b)=\omega(b^*b)$, and (4.19) produces standard Born amplitudes and probabilities for a normalized input state and bounded effect. These formulas do not determine $\omega(a^*U_{\mathscr M}b)$ when the word lies outside the incoming algebra, nor do they prove that the algebraic unitary for a continuum cap displacement is generated by a specified self-adjoint gravitational Hamiltonian; Appendix C.2 proves the time-ordered expansion only under explicit boundedness, continuity, and integrability assumptions. [Time evolution and Born rule](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p10.1) · [Appendix C.2 hypotheses](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2.p2.1)

## 5. Physical inner product and changes of reference action (C.1)–(C.6)

For implementable endpoint transformations, the later unitary multiplies the propagator on the left and the inverse of the earlier unitary multiplies on the right, matching (C.1). Expanding $(I^s-I)^{s'}+(I^{s'}-I)=I^{s+s'}-I$ verifies (C.2) and gives the operator ordering of the endpoint products in (C.3), so opposite identifications across an intermediate cut cancel cleanly (including consistently tracked endpoint phases).

For a change of reference action $I\mapsto I+V$ in (C.4)–(C.5), the identity $F^s+\Delta_s(I+V)+V=(F+V)^s+\Delta_s I$ carries the shift Ward relation over to the relative $S$-matrix. Composing with the $-V$ map returns the identity on generators whenever the admissible deformation classes correspond as assumed. For the resulting $*$-isomorphism $\beta$, the pulled-back GNS inner product satisfies

$$
(\omega\circ\beta)(a^*b)=\omega(\beta(a)^*\beta(b)),
$$

which maps null classes to null classes and induces a surjective isometry (hence a unitary equivalence) between the two GNS Hilbert spaces. This shows that the state chosen for one reference action is unitarily equivalent to its own pullback under another; it does not construct a gravitational state or prove that independently chosen extensions agree. Finally, the statement about $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{\mathscr D}Q^*Q)$ holds via $\|Q\rho_{\mathscr D}^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2$ for any trace-class density operator with full support on $\mathcal H_{\mathscr D}$, while the existence of that density operator and the finiteness of its trace are later physical claims. [Reference-action and trace statements](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS1.p2.1)
