# Does the algebra construct gravitational cap evolution?

**Physical question.** By packaging gravitational action deformations into an abstract algebra of unitary symbols, the paper obtains norm preservation and finite-cut sewing almost immediately. How much genuine continuum gravitational *dynamics* does that algebra actually construct, and which physical steps remain open?

**Outcome.** Equation (4.18) proves exact norm preservation in any representation where the gravitational deformation acts as an admitted unitary generator, and equations (4.29)–(4.31) prove sewing of *open* amplitudes across finitely many successively ordered caps in that same representation. Three core dynamical steps remain open: (1) realizing the interacting gravitational Hamiltonian $H_c$ as a self-adjoint generator or continuum propagator limit in the chosen representation (Appendix C.2 proves a time-ordered exponential only for bounded, norm-integrable generators); (2) implementing arbitrary changes of intermediate cut or slicing consistently (where nonlinear canonical changes alter Weyl ordering by noncentral terms, and even free fields meet the Torre–Varadarajan obstruction between generic curved cuts); and (3) controlling *closed* contractions and traces (which do not follow from strong convergence of open matrix elements).

Source scope: §§3.4–4.4, 5.2, and Appendices C and E. [Paper, §§3.4–4.4, 5.2, C and E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1)

Matched outward charges cancel across an interior cut in §3.6 and Appendix B, giving a classical generator and a consistent classical gluing check on a regular patch. |

Applying this to physically distinct slicings requires the cut-dependent algebras, states, observable maps, and evolution to obey that covariance. This separation between algebra and physical realization already appears in the paper's main algebraic reference: Buchholz and Fredenhagen construct dynamical algebras for scalar Lagrangians and explicitly identify the existence of suitable physical states as a separate problem. Purity of the state extension in §4.2 guarantees irreducibility, but imposes no bounds on the continuity or differentiability of gravitational transition amplitudes with respect to time, cap geometry, or source strength. [Buchholz and Fredenhagen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06062)

## Direct checks of the logical implications

Three short calculations pinpoint exactly where abstract algebraic properties stop short of continuum dynamics.

**Unitary composition allows discontinuous time dependence.** On $\ell^2(\mathbb R_{\mathrm{discrete}})$, define $T(t)e_x=e_{x+t}$. Every $T(t)$ is unitary and obeys $T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)$, yet $\langle e_0,T(t)e_0\rangle=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(t)$ is completely discontinuous at $t=0$ and has no Hamiltonian generator. Finite-displacement unitarity and composition therefore do not imply a generator on their own. Appendix E.1 rules out this pathology by adding continuity as an explicit axiom; the paper still needs a proof of strong continuity for its chosen representation of gravitational translations.

**A nonlinear change of canonical chart shifts Weyl ordering.** In a single canonical pair, let $V=\exp[-iq^4/(4\hbar)]$. On Schwartz functions, $VpV^*=p+q^3$. For the real classical symbol $h=p+q^3$, the Weyl star product (C.9) gives

$$
h\star h=h^2,\qquad h^2\star h=h^3-\frac{\hbar^2}{8}(\partial_p^2 h^2)(\partial_q^2 h)
=h^3-\tfrac32\hbar^2 q .
$$

Hence $Vp^3V^*=\mathcal Q(h^3)-\tfrac32\hbar^2 q$: substituting the transformed classical coordinates into the symbol and applying midpoint quantization shifts the operator by a noncentral term. Equation (4.2d) addresses additive shifts, which are unaffected by this calculation, but extending its change-of-variables logic to nonlinear canonical maps induced by a change of cut requires either a quantum symbol transformation law or a proof that the admissible cut changes avoid such ordering shifts. At the linear field-theory level there is a second representation hurdle: Torre and Varadarajan proved that for a free Klein–Gordon field on a flat toroidal spacetime of spatial dimension greater than one, canonical evolution between generic curved Cauchy surfaces is not unitarily implementable in the standard Fock representation. Although the field, boundary conditions, and representation differ here, their result flags a concrete implementability test for the gravitational radiative sector. [Torre and Varadarajan](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9811222)

**Open sewing does not control a closed cut.** Take $U=1$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ with $P_N$ projecting onto the first $N$ basis vectors. Every open matrix element converges as in (4.31), yet $\operatorname{Tr}(P_N U)=N$ diverges. Similarly, in §5.2 the positive lower bound $C_b^*C_b\ge c1$ forces $\operatorname{Tr}(P C_b^*C_b P)\ge c\operatorname{rank}P$. Before finite dimension is proved, strong convergence of exhaustive cut projections provides no control over unnormalized signed traces with an insertion $X$. Justifying the sign comparison in §5.2 requires an independently defined closed amplitude together with a convergence or sign-preserving gluing theorem for that closure.

## Tests that would address the gaps

1. **Continuum Hamiltonian limit.** Fix a regular family of caps with nonzero shear and an explicit gravitational state. Define regulated Hamiltonians $H_{c,\Lambda}(t)$ complete with ordering, corner terms, and any counterterms, and construct their propagators. Under explicit identifications of the cutoff Hilbert spaces, prove strong convergence of both propagators and their adjoints on a dense domain with time regularity sufficient for continuity and the evolution equation, and verify on a generating set that the limit agrees with the represented unitary (4.4). (Alternatively, give a direct algebraic construction of a regular positive state obeying the required Ward identities and gravitational dynamics on that set.)
2. **Cut independence.** Choose two distinct smooth intermediate caps sharing identical outer boundary data and a fixed relationally located source. Construct the boundary identifications independently, verify that they preserve the physical inner product and clock-form domains, and compare the two composed propagators with the source inserted. In a linearized Gaussian sector with fixed Fock complex structures, a first concrete check is computing the anti-linear Bogoliubov block $\beta$ and testing whether $\sum_{jk}|\beta_{jk}|^2<\infty$; full slicing independence also requires consistency (including endpoint or anomalous phases) around closed loops of cut changes.
3. **Spatial refinement and closed sewing.** Define the matched Hilbert space and joining map $\mathsf J_t$ with its gluing measure, and prove that it intertwines matched evolution with union evolution on a dense family of preparations. For the closed cut of §5.2, supply an independently justified closure functional on the relevant preparation together with an estimate controlling its finite-rank approximations (a trace-class bound on $C_b^*C_b$ would suffice and, paired with the claimed positive lower bound, would directly imply finite rank).

Until these tests are met, the manuscript establishes abstract finite-displacement unitarity, bounded-source dynamics, and fixed-representation open sewing; an interacting continuum gravitational propagator, arbitrary-cut independence, and closed-cut trace applications remain open.
