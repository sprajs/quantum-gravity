# Section 5.4 · Spatial topology and null states

**The physical question.** *If a causal diamond holds only finitely many independent quantum states, must extra spacetime handles become linearly redundant, and must connected two-boundary geometries factorize into separate boundary states?*

**Result.** The Gram-rank bound, the matrix-to-state identification, the fixed-area entropy formula, and the leading two-sided horizon correlators all reproduce under the stated Hilbert-space and semiclassical assumptions. The decisive physical assumption for topology is (5.71), which equates the physical norm of a connected filling with the Hilbert–Schmidt norm already accessible to separate boundary preparations. When cobordisms are defined as operators on the chosen cut, this is a valid gluing prescription. What is missing is an independent closed gravitational amplitude enforcing that equality for all fillings and ruling out additional orthogonal sectors across arbitrary topologies. ## One-boundary handles · (5.70)

For $m$ fillings in a *common* physical space $H$, define $V:\mathbb C^m\to H$ by $Ve_i=v_i$. Then $G=V^\dagger V$, $c^\dagger Gc=\|Vc\|^2$, and $\ker G=\ker V$. Thus, if $\dim H=d_{\mathscr D}$, at least $m-d_{\mathscr D}$ independent coefficient vectors have zero norm for $m>d_{\mathscr D}$. For example, the assignments $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(1,1)$ give a positive Gram matrix of rank two and the null combination $v_1+v_2-v_3$. Individual handles can still change every relevant overlap. This argument counts states for specified fillings. It gives no convergence estimate for a sum of amplitudes over infinitely many handle numbers.

Two upstream statements enter separately. Equation (5.39c) supplies a finite $d_{\mathscr D}$. Equation (5.20) proves density in the space obtained from a **chosen** GNS representation and clock projection. Using this space for every topological filling is another identification. The footnote after (5.20) leaves equivalence across choices of the state $\omega$ open. ## Two-boundary pairing · (5.71)–(5.74)

Take an orthonormal basis $u_i$ of $H$ and the matrix units $E_{ij}=|u_i\rangle\langle u_j|$. Then

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(E_{ij}^\dagger E_{kl})=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl},\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(E_{ij}^\dagger N)=\langle u_i|N|u_j\rangle .
$$

If full gluing assigns (5.71) to each pair of fillings, then for every representative of finite norm,
$$
\|\mathscr M\|^2=\operatorname{Tr}(N^\dagger N)=\sum_j\|Nu_j\|^2
=\sum_{ij}|N_{ij}|^2 .
$$
Independent boundary preparations supply the $E_{ij}$ as products $q_{ij}$. For any finite set $F$ of their indices, orthogonality gives
$$
\bigg\|\mathscr M-\sum_{(i,j)\in F}N_{ij}q_{ij}\bigg\|^2
=\|\mathscr M\|^2-\sum_{(i,j)\in F}|N_{ij}|^2 .
$$
Completing the sum gives (5.73). The assignments $q_{ij}\mapsto E_{ij}\mapsto u_i\otimes\overline{u_j}$ induce isometries onto the Hilbert–Schmidt operators and onto $H\otimes\overline H$ after completion. This proves (5.74) in the gluing construction. In infinite dimension the norm condition is essential. For example $N=1$ has infinite Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Given the finite-rank energy projections of (5.46), $P_INP_J$ is Hilbert–Schmidt for bounded $N$, and $\|P_I N P_J\|_2^2\le\min(\operatorname{rank}P_I,\operatorname{rank}P_J)\|N\|^2$. The AdS boundary algebra in (5.48) is obtained on the cyclic sector of one chosen ground state. **Test of the completeness inference.** In a two-boundary physical space $K$ with orthonormal products $q_{ij}$, let $P$ project onto their closed span. Product overlaps determine $P\mathscr M$. Bessel's inequality always gives $\sum_{ij}|N_{ij}|^2\le\|\mathscr M\|^2$. Equation (5.71) on the diagonal asserts equality. To see exactly what a one-boundary argument can miss, take $H=\mathbb C^2$, $K=\mathrm{HS}(H)\oplus\mathrm{HS}(H)$, and $q_{ij}=(E_{ij},0)$. The one-boundary matrix algebra acts by left multiplication on both copies and still has scalar center. For $v=(1_2,E_{11})$, all product overlaps are $\delta_{ij}$, their squared sum is $2$, and the squared norm in $K$ is $3$. The residual has norm squared $1$. This is a counterexample to inferring completeness from the one-boundary algebra and the product overlaps. Equation (5.71) excludes it by hypothesis. The example makes no assertion about which norm a four-dimensional gravitational path integral supplies.

Equation (4.29) inserts a complete basis when the two caps already realize a common Hilbert space and the amplitudes are operators on it. Determining completeness on a disconnected cut from geometric closed amplitudes requires the additional equality above, or a proof that the two separate cuts can always be made with no other sector. Appendix E.3 uses the same trace formula in (E.15b). A discriminating calculation would evaluate the closed double $\mathscr M^\dagger\mathscr M$ independently and compare it with the sum of squared separate-cap overlaps. The paper's own references illustrate this distinction. Marolf and Maxfield compute a residual squared norm $Z_\alpha-r$ in their example, where $r$ is the number of independent one-sided states. McNamara and Wang identify saturation of a thermal trace bound with surjectivity for a boundary and its conjugate, and formulate the full criterion for every pair of boundaries. ## Entropy at fixed area

For $N=c_{\mathscr A} U$ on an area eigenspace of finite dimension $d>0$ with $U$ isometric there, the unnormalized squared norm is $d|c_{\mathscr A}|^2$. In the outgoing basis $Uu_i$ and conjugate incoming basis $\overline{u_i}$ the normalized vector is, up to a common phase,
$$
d^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^d Uu_i\otimes\overline{u_i}.
$$
Each reduced density operator equals $1/d$ times the projection onto its support, so $S=\log d$ on either side. The scalar corner weight cancels. This calculation cannot determine $d$ from the area. For a general connected preparation, entanglement also depends on the singular values of $N$. A rank-one matrix gives a product state. [Fixed-area argument](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS4.p5.2)

## Hartle–Hawking example · (5.75)–(5.77)

1. **Oscillator pair.** Set $q=e^{-b\omega}$ with $b\omega>0$. The normalized pair state is $(1-q^2)^{1/2}\sum_{n\ge0}q^n|n,n^*\rangle$. Acting with $a_La_R$ yields
   $$
   (1-q^2)\sum_{n=1}^\infty n q^{2n-1}=\frac{q}{1-q^2}=\frac{1}{2\sinh(b\omega)}.
   $$
   A finite-dimensional cap cannot realize an exact oscillator ladder. The infinite sum in this example belongs to the quadratic approximation. Truncating at occupation $K$ discards probability $q^{2(K+1)}$.
2. **Affine-parameter kernel.** At the equilibrium wall temperature, the wall surface gravity is $\kappa_R=2\pi/(\hbar\beta_R)=\pi/b$, giving the boost frequency $\nu=\omega/\kappa_R$. Write $x=\log(u_L/u_R)$. For $f(x)=1/[4\cosh^2(x/2)]$, substitution $t=e^x$ gives
   $$
   \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ikx}f(x)\,dx
   =\int_0^\infty \frac{t^{-ik}}{(1+t)^2}\,dt
   =\Gamma(1-ik)\Gamma(1+ik)=\frac{\pi k}{\sinh(\pi k)}.
   $$
   Fourier inversion therefore gives precisely the cosine integral in (5.76), $f(x)=u_Lu_R/(u_L+u_R)^2$. Including the prefactor gives $-c_\perp/(u_L+u_R)^2$, including its sign and factor of four at $u_L=u_R$. This uses the stated near-horizon spectral measure and reflection-matched affine normalizations. Equation (D.19) parameterizes $c_\perp$ using transverse smearing and polarizations. An absolute number for the selected quadrupole would need its corresponding polarization and smearing normalization.
3. **Area derivatives.** For the transported area element $a=\sqrt{q_{S}}$, $a'=a\theta$ and $a''/a=\theta'+\theta^2$. The affine vacuum Raychaudhuri equation yields $a''/a=\theta^2/2-\sigma_{AB}\sigma^{AB}$, since $R_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b=0$. On the stationary shear-free horizon $\theta^{(0)}=\sigma^{(0)}=0$. The chosen odd-parity mode also has $\theta^{(1)}=0$. The quadratic area derivative is consequently $-\int dA_0\,{:}\sigma^{(1)}_{AB}\sigma_{(1)}^{AB}{:}$. For a single normalized real harmonic write $\sigma_{AB}^{(1)}=s(u)Y_{AB}$. The connected Wick contraction of the two normal-ordered squares is $2\langle s_Ls_R\rangle^2$, giving $2c_\perp^2/(u_L+u_R)^4$ in (5.77b). The correlator has two derivatives on each area. Recovering an area correlator itself would require the area and first-derivative data at reference cuts.

**Numerical cross-check.** Composite Simpson quadrature on $\nu\in[0,16]$ with 8,192 equal panels gives $0.250000000000000$, $0.222222222222222$, $0.082644628099174$ and $0.000998002996005$ for $u_L/u_R=1,1/2,1/10,1/1000$ respectively. Each unrounded result agrees with $f(x)$ to $6\times10^{-17}$ in absolute value. Summing the normalized oscillator series through $n=400$ at $b\omega=0.12,0.7,2$ gives $4.156683441195660$, $0.659123045733149$ and $0.137860282385892$, agreeing with (5.75) within $2\times10^{-15}$. These tests use the large-radius, Gaussian, single-angular-mode approximation, $\ell_P\ll u_L,u_R\ll r_h$. Matching the two local correlation functions verifies this perturbative example. The assertion of equality in the full physical Hilbert space still depends on (5.71).
