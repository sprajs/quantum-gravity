# Section 3.4 and Appendix B — Hamiltonian and boundary terms

**Physical question.** What Hamiltonian advances the spacelike cap in time, does the Komar surface charge alone generate that evolution when gravitational radiation crosses the boundary, and do the Appendix B constraint and lapse identities hold?

**Outcome.** On the classical constraints, the bulk first-order Hamiltonian reduces to a boundary term, equation (3.54) is exactly the integrated Komar charge for the coordinate translation $\xi=\partial_t$, the fixed-location unit corner boost integrates cleanly to $H_\xi=A/(8\pi G_N)+\mathrm{const}$, the radial lapse responds to interior shear with the printed sign, and nine Appendix B residuals vanish in a flat test family and the bulk density agrees with the direct four-dimensional Ricci calculation for arbitrary round cuts with zero shift in affine gauge (`checks/03-4.py`). A substantive gap remains for **translating** boundaries that meet radiation: the covariant Hamiltonian variation in (3.50b) includes a flux term $-i_\xi\theta$ in addition to $\delta Q_\xi$. Below, a Schwarzschild sphere calculation measures the missing half of $\delta M$ on a single cut, and a radiative variation shows that the fixed-embedding bulk charge variation is not even integrable at an external radiating cut. Section 3.4 and Appendix B.3 supply canonical change-of-variables and edge identities, but do not yet prove differentiability or flux cancellation for general cap evolution on the unrestricted phase space.

Source scope: equations (3.46)–(3.55) and (B.1)–(B.24), printed pages 20–21 and 59–64. [Section 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=20) · [Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=59) · [TeX](https://arxiv.org/src/2611.34924v1)

## Bulk split, coordinate charge and boost

Set $G=G_N$, $F=dt\wedge(\dot\omega_\Sigma-D_\Sigma\omega_t)+F_\Sigma$, and $B=dt\wedge B_t+B_\Sigma$. The symmetric multiplier contributes $-B_t\wedge\Phi B_\Sigma$ to the Lagrangian, and integrating the connection term by parts gives

$$
-B_\Sigma\wedge D_\Sigma\omega_t
=-d(B_\Sigma\omega_t)+(D_\Sigma B_\Sigma)\omega_t.
$$

Hence $16\pi G\Theta=\int B_\Sigma\wedge\delta\omega_\Sigma$, while $16\pi G H$ consists of the bulk constraints $-\int[B_t\wedge(F_\Sigma-\Phi B_\Sigma)+\omega_t D_\Sigma B_\Sigma]$ plus the boundary term $+\int_{\partial\Sigma}B_\Sigma\omega_t$. This confirms the signs in (3.46)–(3.48) for the bulk action; separate cap, null-side, and joint actions contribute to the complete generator.

For the metric (B.1), the inverse components are $g^{tv}=-1/N$, $g^{vv}=F/N^2$, $g^{vA}=V^A/N$, $g^{AB}=q^{AB}$, with volume density $\sqrt{-g}=N\sqrt q$. Taking orientation $dt\wedge dv\wedge d^2y$ and $\xi=\partial_t$, the dual covector is $(-F+q_{AB}V^AV^B,-N,q_{AB}V^B)$. Evaluating the Komar form $Q_\xi=-\star d\xi^\flat/(16\pi G)$ and pulling back to a constant-$t,v$ cut yields

$$
16\pi G\, Q_\xi|_C=\frac{\sqrt q}{N}
\big[F_{,v}-N_{,t}+V^A N_{,A}-q_{AB}V^A\partial_vV^B\big]d^2y.
$$

With $f=-\log N$, $\mu_c=(\partial_t-V^A\partial_A)f+F_{,v}/(2N)$, and $\Pi_A=\sqrt q\,q_{AB}\partial_v V^B/N$ in the stated angular coordinates, the outer-minus-inner integral reproduces (3.54) exactly.

For a unit normal-plane boost at the corner $S$, $\xi|_S=0$ and the squared binormal norm is $-2$. In the paper's oriented boost convention this gives $Q_\xi=\mu/(8\pi G)$. Holding the corner location fixed during the variation implies $\operatorname{tr}(\epsilon\,\delta\epsilon)=0$, so $Q_{\delta\xi}=0$, and $i_\xi\theta=0$ on $S$. Equation (3.50b) therefore integrates directly to $H_\xi=A/(8\pi G)+\mathrm{const}$.

## The distinction at a translating cut

Unlike a corner boost that vanishes at $S$, a time translation moves the cut itself, so the flux term $-i_\xi\theta$ in (3.50b) need not vanish. Two concrete tests show what happens.

**Single-cut Schwarzschild test.** On a sphere of areal radius $r$ in outgoing Schwarzschild coordinates, set $N=1$, $V=0$, and $F=1-2GM/r$. The Komar formula above gives $\int_C Q_{\partial_t}=r^2 F'/(4G)=M/2$. Under a mass variation $\delta M$, the metric perturbation has $h_{tt}=2G\delta M/r$, $h^{rr}=2G\delta M/r$, trace $h=0$, and $\nabla_b h^{rb}=2G\delta M/r^2$. Substituting into the paper's metric potential (A.6) with $\epsilon_{rt\theta\phi}=-r^2\sin\theta$ yields

$$
\int_C i_{\partial_t}\theta=-\frac{\delta M}{2},\qquad
\int_C(\delta Q_{\partial_t}-i_{\partial_t}\theta)=\delta M.
$$

These single-cut expressions hold at any regular radius (both endpoint contributions cancel on a stationary two-cut shell). Even in a stationary sector where the Hamiltonian variation is integrable, the Komar integral supplies only half of $\delta M$; the flux term provides the other half.

**Radiative integrability test.** A sharper obstruction appears on unrestricted radiating null data. Holding $\xi$ field-independent, the field-space exterior derivative of the proposed charge variation is $-\int_{\partial\Sigma}i_\xi\omega$, where $\omega=\delta\theta$. At one cut of a shear-free cone, choose the affine translation $\xi=\ell=\partial_u$ in fixed-normal characteristic gauge, and take two independent first-order metric variations $\delta_i q_{AB}=\rho^2 a_i(u)T_{AB}(y)$ for a smooth trace-free tensor $T_{AB}$ on the unit sphere. Because Raychaudhuri's shear source is quadratic, the area is unchanged at first order. From the shear term in (3.39), the radiative contribution (up to boundary orientation) is

$$
\int_C i_\ell\omega(\delta_1,\delta_2)
=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\int_C\mu\,T^{AB}T_{AB}
\big(a'_1a_2-a'_2a_1\big),
$$

with indices of $T$ raised by the unit-sphere metric. One can easily choose smooth profiles with $(a_1,a'_1)=(0,1)$ and $(a_2,a'_2)=(1,0)$ on this cut that vanish near the opposite cut, making the fixed-embedding bulk charge variation nonclosed. Across a shared *internal* boundary, matching fields and variations cancels the oppositely oriented currents. At an *external* radiating cut, however, obtaining a differentiable Hamiltonian requires either boundary conditions restricting the flux or an explicit calculation showing that the boundary fields and potentials supply a compensating current. The pullback and endpoint identities in §3.4 and Appendix B.3 do not perform that calculation, leaving open an extended-phase-space construction with the required compensation.

## Gravitational constraints and boundary motion

- **Lapse and transport, (3.55) and (B.1)–(B.7).** The nonaffine Raychaudhuri equation reads $\partial_v\theta=\kappa\theta-\theta^2/2-\sigma^2-R_{vv}$. Setting $R_{vv}=0$ and $\theta=2/v$ gives $\partial_v\log N=v\sigma^2/2=\partial_v\Xi$, so interior shear reduces $N(v)/N(1)$ on the expanding branch $v<1$. The nonaffine normal connection obeys $(\partial_v+\theta)\varpi_A=D_A\kappa+D_A\theta/2-D_B\sigma^B{}_A$; substituting $\varpi=w-Df/2$, $\kappa=-\partial_v f$, and $\Pi_A=-2\sqrt q\,w_A$ proves (B.4a), and differentiating (B.4b) recovers (B.2d). Taking the trace and trace-free parts of (B.2b,c) yields (B.6). Expanding $\partial_A(N\sqrt q\,\varpi^A)=N\sqrt q(D_A\varpi^A-\varpi^A D_A f)$ and using $2\varpi^2+2\varpi\cdot Df=2w^2-|Df|^2/2$ verifies (B.7). The angular divergence integrates to zero on a closed cut. The radial derivative still contributes the endpoint term when integrated along the cone.
- **Exact metric checks of the transverse equations.** For Schwarzschild–(A)dS with $q=r^2\gamma$, $N=1$, $V=0$, and $F=1-2GM/r-\Lambda r^2/3$, we have $\vartheta=-F/r$, $\mu_c=F'/2$, and $\eta=w=0$; equations (B.5a,c) reduce to $(rF)'=1-\Lambda r^2$ and $F''/2=(F-1)/r^2$. To test nonzero angular derivatives, shear, and shift simultaneously, start from flat cylindrical spacetime $ds^2=-dt^2-2dt\,dR+R^2dx^2+dy^2$ and substitute $R=a(t,x,y)v^2$ ($a>0$). Then $\sqrt q=R$, $N=R_{,v}$, $V^A=-q^{AB}R_{,B}$, and $F=1+2R_{,t}+q_{AB}V^A V^B$. For arbitrary smooth $a(t,x,y)$, the exact symbolic residuals of (B.3), both components of (B.4a), and all components of (B.5a–c) vanish identically. [Reproducible SymPy calculation](../checks/03-4.py)
- **Null-side graph, (B.8)–(B.15).** Put $p=v-b(t,y)$, $s=Ndp$, and $m=-s^\sharp$. The inverse metric gives $s^2=F+2N(b_{,t}-V^A b_{,A})+N^2|Db|^2$, with $m^t=1$ on $p=0$. Because $s^2=0$ everywhere along the graph, $d(s^2)|_{p=0}=\partial_v(s^2)\,dp$. Computing the acceleration of $-N\nabla p$ yields $\kappa_m=m(\log N)-\partial_v(s^2)/(2N)$, recovering (B.8) with the affine normalization sign. In the affine coordinate, contracting with the graph gives $L^r-b_{,t}L^t-b_{,A}L^A$, and eliminating $b_{,t}$ via $s^2=0$ yields (B.12). For $w=t-\tau$, the timelike covector $dw$ gives $n_a=-w_{,a}/W$, cap volume element $\sqrt q W\,dr\,d^2y$, and $K=\partial_a(\sqrt q n^a)/\sqrt q$; the final-minus-initial cap density is $L^a w_{,a}-2\sqrt q W K$, matching (B.13)–(B.15).
- **Bulk density, (B.9)–(B.11).** For any time-independent spherical $F(r)$ with $N=1$, the printed combination of first-derivative terms and divergence equals, per unit solid angle, $2-2F-4rF'-r^2F''-2\Lambda r^2=r^2(R[g]-2\Lambda)$. In the flat cylindrical family above, the affine substitution $R=r+a(t,x,y)$ makes the total density in (B.10) vanish identically at $\Lambda=0$ while its individual terms remain nonzero. These provide explicit checks in curved and angularly dependent metrics, short of a general off-shell tensor proof. Run `uv run checks/03-4.py` to reproduce all nine residuals with SymPy 1.14.0. [Symbolic check](../checks/03-4.py)
- **Embedding and endpoints, (B.16)–(B.24).** At fixed $t$, a cut tangent vector is $\partial_A+b_{,A}\partial_r$. Pulling $-\star d\chi^\flat$ back to these tangents for $\chi^t=0$ yields $\sqrt q[\partial_r\chi^r-q_{AB}(\partial_r V^A)\chi^B-D^A b\,\partial_r(q_{AB}\chi^B)]d^2y$, giving (B.17) upon dividing by $16\pi G$. The factor of two in the full unit boost comes from both normal directions. The extended on-shell potential (B.21) follows from $\theta(\mathcal L_\chi g)-i_\chi L=dQ_\chi$. The time component of the Einstein divergence, $\sqrt{-g}(g^{bc}\Gamma^t{}_{bc}-g^{tb}\Gamma^c{}_{bc})$, evaluates to $2\partial_u\sqrt q-\sqrt q\,\partial_u f$, explaining the interval integrand of (B.22): moving an upper endpoint adds its velocity times this integrand, and moving a lower endpoint enters with the opposite sign. Contracting the cotangent momentum with $\delta_\xi q=\mathcal L_\xi q$ and the Lie derivative of the area density recovers the angular generator (B.24) in the reference coordinates $\epsilon_0=d^2y$.

## The bulk density in curved metrics before imposing the field equations

Take $ds^2=-F(t,r)dt^2-2dt\,dr+b(t,r)^2d\Omega^2$ with two independent smooth functions and $b>0$. With $h$ the two-dimensional $(t,r)$ metric, the warped-product scalar curvature is $R_4=R_h+2/b^2-4\Box_h b/b-2(\nabla b)^2_h/b^2$. Here $R_h=-F_{rr}$, $\Box_h b=-2b_{tr}+F_r b_r+F b_{rr}$ and $(\nabla b)^2_h=-2b_t b_r+F b_r^2$. The four-dimensional Ricci density per unit solid angle becomes
$$
 b^2 R_4=2-b^2 F_{rr}+8b b_{tr}+4b_t b_r
              -4b b_r F_r-4F b b_{rr}-2F b_r^2 .
$$

An independent substitution into (B.11) uses $A=b^2$, vanishing shear and shift, and intrinsic curvature $2/b^2$. The four surviving densities per solid angle are $L_0=2-A_t A_r/A-2\Lambda A$, $\Delta L_0=A_r F_r+F A_r^2/(2A)$, $L^t=2A_r$ and $L^r=2A_t-A F_r-2F A_r$. Adding the first two and differentiating the last two recovers $b^2(R_4-2\Lambda)$ exactly. This is a check on the nondegenerate metric branch with its torsion-free connection. The independent variables $b,F$ need satisfy no Einstein equation. Angular shift or shear would turn on terms that this proof does not test. [Printed bulk densities (B.10)–(B.11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A2.E10)

Their controls give $R_4=12k$ for $b=r$, $F=1-kr^2$, and $R_{rr}=-4\alpha/b$ for $b=r+\alpha r^2$, $F=1$. ## What the reduced-action formula supplies

Given a canonical chart and a differentiable action for the boundary problem, the pullback $\iota_t^*\Theta_t=p_k\delta q^k+\delta\chi_t$ and velocity decomposition $\dot{\mathfrak h}=D\iota_t\dot\phi+\partial_t\iota_t$ immediately imply

$$
H_c=H\circ\iota_t-\Theta_t(\partial_t\iota_t)+\partial_t\chi_t.
$$

This establishes the sign of the embedding charge in (B.19). Adding an endpoint functional $[B(\mathfrak h,t)]$ shifts $\Theta$ by $\delta B$, $H$ by $-\partial_t B|_{\mathfrak h}$, and $\chi_t$ by $B\circ\iota_t$; the three shifts cancel in $H_c$ by the chain rule, as required. Neither (3.53) nor the cut formula (3.54), however, yields an explicit Hamiltonian flow for arbitrary radiating cap data without completing the boundary-flux analysis above. The classical shear-induced reduction of the radial lapse survives this qualification.
