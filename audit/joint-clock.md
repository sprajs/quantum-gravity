# The clock across a spacelike cap and a null sheet

In an exact four-dimensional vacuum spacetime, a narrowing gravitational wave crossing the cap rim can shift a spherical diamond's sharp affine clock by a finite amount even as its symplectic pairing with every fixed smooth metric perturbation tends to zero under the ordinary Einstein current with a shared boundary frame. This result separates two distinct questions about the classical bracket. Evaluating how the clock responds to **one** specified smooth gravitational probe is a weaker requirement than inverting the symplectic form to produce a single smooth Hamiltonian perturbation valid against **all** smooth test variations. The answer to both questions also depends on whether the cap and null sheet share one continued metric and boundary frame across their seam, or form an isolated, independently marked cap carrying relative corner degrees of freedom. These calculations establish a classical regularity obstruction for the ordinary shared-frame metric current, identify a **proof gap** for an isolated independently marked cap or distributional inverse, and leave the finite-dimensional quantum conjecture open. [Chandrasekaran, arXiv:2611.34924v1, Eqs. (3.18)–(3.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.E18)

## Why the clock detects the rim

Let $\rho$ be the area radius per unit solid angle along an expanding null generator in one affine parameter $u$, and write $p_b=(d\rho/du)_b$ for the optical expansion rate at a cut labeled by the area ratio $v=\rho/\rho_S=b$. The cap-calibrated affine reading between cuts $b$ and $c$ is $T_{bc}=p_b(u_c-u_b)$. It makes a structural difference **where the ray is calibrated**. On the expanding branch put $h(v)=v\|\partial_v\bar q\|^2/8$ and choose three ordered cuts $a<b<c$. The vacuum focusing equation gives the exact transfer law

$$
L_{ab}=p_a/p_b=e^{\int_a^b h},\qquad T_{ac}=T_{ab}+L_{ab}T_{bc}.
$$

Here all readings share the same generator, area density, and embedding. In $L_{ab}T_{bc}$, the functional coefficient of $\delta h(t)$ equals $L_{ab}T_{bc}$ for $a<t<b$ and $\rho_S L_{ab}\int_t^c\exp(\int_b^s h)\,ds$ for $b<t<c$. Because these coefficients match continuously at $t=b$, integrating by parts against a smooth polarization variation leaves no shear evaluation at that internal cut. For the cap-calibrated reading $T_{bc}$ alone, however, the coefficient jumps discontinuously from $0$ to $T_{bc}$ across $b$. Identifying the cap rim $C$ at $v=b$ and the waist $S$ at $v=c=1$, and holding $\rho_C$ and generator labels fixed, yields the rim contribution to its differential:

$$
\beta_C=-\frac{\rho_C(u_S-u_C)}{2}\,\sigma_{(\ell)}^{AB}\,\delta q^{\rm TF}_{AB}\big|_C\qquad(c=1).
$$

A spacelike observer at $C$ can reconstruct both pieces of this expression directly from cap Cauchy data: the normal–normal cap momentum and rim mean curvature determine the initial expansion. The tangential trace-free cap momentum and the trace-free part of the rim curvature determine the null shear $\sigma_{(\ell)}^{AB}$. Introducing an additional independent momentum conjugate to the expansion requires deriving its two-form and matching conditions directly from the gravitational action. ## Three calculations on spherical diamonds

| Preparation | Calculation and conclusion |
|---|---|
| **Flat-space second variation.** Take a unit-waist flat diamond ($R=1$) with matched-radius adjustment $M=\langle\rho_S^2\rangle/\langle\rho_S\rangle=R$ and a spherical cap at radius fraction $a=1/3$. Pair a fixed quadrupole wave setting unit radial optical-strain slope at $C$ with a narrowing smooth quadrupole wave of unit rim strain. | The **mixed second variation** (Hessian, not a Poisson bracket) of the matched-radius clock tends to **$-1/30$** as the second wave narrows; the first variation vanishes at flat space. On the spacelike waist disk, the narrowing wave's Cauchy data concentrate strictly inside the disk (near $r=1/3$) and pair to zero with every fixed smooth metric perturbation. This excludes a continuously differentiable ($C^1$) family of smooth Hamiltonian metric generators through flat space for this preparation and the ordinary shared-frame symplectic form. [Proof, optical waveforms and numerical check](joint-flat-hessian.md) |
| **Curved plane-wave first variation.** Place both smooth tips on the axis of an exact four-dimensional vacuum plane wave ($L=D=1$, cap fraction $\epsilon=1/2$), tracking the motion of both the waist intersection and the cap. Send a narrowing compensated pulse (zero total integral, unit integral on its earlier half) across the cap near the axial direction where its null coordinate $U$ attains a regular maximum. | The **first variation** of the full angularly averaged clock tends to **$-0.060534$** in the specified units. On a smooth spacelike ball spanning the waist (with common seam marks), the two opposite halves of the pulse cancel against every smooth metric perturbation. At the cap, angular integration near the regular maximum of $U$ samples only the earlier half. No smooth metric Hamiltonian generator represents the sharp clock at this curved background for the ordinary shared-frame symplectic form. [Exact cones, cap geometry and narrowing-pulse test](joint-focal.md) |
| **Kasner smooth probe and position average.** In a globally hyperbolic vacuum Kasner spacetime with compact spatial $T^3$ sections, hold the matched waist radius $R$ fixed, **average over emitter positions on the spatial torus**, and pair the clock with a smooth, gauge-invariant probe comparing the torus shape at nearby times. | Evaluating the clock against this single smooth probe yields a **nonzero classical Peierls bracket**: at cap fraction $\epsilon=1/2$, its leading coefficient in units of $R^5/T^4$ is **$-0.00036096$**, where $T$ is the launch time. Because the **spatial position average** and torus symmetries collapse the clock differential to a single Kasner-parameter derivative, this symmetric preparation also admits a smooth Hamiltonian tangent—a local spatial rescaling invisible to gauge-invariant probes supported inside a contractible patch and detected only by the global torus modulus. [Source, bracket and two-cone numerical integration](joint-jacobi.md) |

The first two calculations evaluate the ordinary Einstein symplectic current of a single metric continued smoothly across the seam with a common boundary frame. Current conservation allows the joined cap and null sheet to be deformed to a smooth spacelike disk sharing the outer waist boundary. Any smooth candidate generator $X$ must reproduce the clock differential through its symplectic pairing on that disk; instead, the two narrowing-wave sequences produce finite discrepancies—at second order in flat space and at first order on the curved plane wave. Every member of either sequence is a smooth vacuum perturbation at its own width; the limit tests the Hamilton equation directly without claiming a finite-amplitude wave of uniformly bounded curvature. In a separate test at zero cosmological constant, a smooth spacetime scaling probe gives another exact mixed Peierls bracket for suitably equivariant markings. ## Where the missing response goes in a solvable sector

A symmetry-reduced sector shows explicitly where the obstructed response moves when smooth metric tangents fail. In a polarized four-dimensional Einstein–Rosen vacuum spacetime with two Killing directions (toroidal cuts and fixed areal embeddings), one can evaluate the cap and sheet symplectic forms, transport both to ordinary spacelike initial data, and compute the causal Green function directly. At the worked radii ($a=1/4$, $b=1$), a smooth physical disturbance shifts the affine clock by **$-3/2$** entirely through its rim term. No smooth matched tangent represents the clock differential on the full radial wave sector; instead, the causal Peierls inverse acting on smooth probes vanishes in the open cap, approaches a nonzero sheet-side limit at the rim, and carries a **delta function in the initial canonical momentum at the rim**. Restricting to an exact two-parameter homogeneous family removes the independent radial waves and recovers a smooth canonical bracket. Conversely, in the full radial sector, approximating the same sharp reading by two distinct shrinking finite-resolution detectors yields order-dependent cross-bracket limits ($0$, $G/648$, or $25G/324$ at $\epsilon=3/4$), whereas identical detectors always have zero bracket with themselves. Because this sector uses toroidal cuts, fixed embeddings, and one polarization, it serves as a solvable junction model rather than a verdict on the full spherical diamond. ## What the isolated diamond must supply

Version 1 of Chandrasekaran's construction includes embeddings and normal frames alongside metric data. On a joined hypersurface sharing one smooth metric and a common boundary frame, identical displacements of the two faces have opposite orientations at the internal seam and cancel. On an **isolated, independently marked cap**, by contrast, a surviving **relative** cap frame can pair its angular displacement with the variation of the normal connection $\varpi_A$ (which measures the rotation or boost of the normal plane along the rim), while an independent relative cap boost can pair with the rim area density $\mu_C$. Holding the perturbation's angular labels fixed does not force this angular pairing to vanish: in the curved plane-wave sequence above, $\delta_h(\mu_C\varpi_i)$ produces an explicit nonzero limiting distribution at the axial maximum. A frame transported from the cap to the waist must likewise be varied consistently within that prescription. [Which geometric variables enter](joint-edges.md) · [Moving embeddings, Eq. (B.21)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A2.E21)

For the round-sphere quadrupole test ($R=1$, rim radius $r_c=aR=1/3$), an explicit candidate exists on the isolated side. In the orientation with one-sided angular endpoint potential $+(8\pi G_N)^{-1}\int_C\mu_C\,\varpi_A\,\delta F^A$, an independent angular displacement $V=G_N\,\nabla P_2(\cos\theta)$ at radius $1/3$ supplies precisely the missing **$-1/30$** in the mixed Hessian test through the normal-connection charge alone. This candidate, however, encounters a geometric **trace-free matching caveat**: at fixed angular labels, $V$ changes the local rim area density, and while an accompanying affine normal displacement can restore that area density at first order, both normal second fundamental forms of the round Minkowski rim are **pure trace**. No normal motion can remove the resulting trace-free angular distortion $2r_c^2(D_{(A}V_{B)})^{\rm TF}$ of the induced rim metric (with $D_A$ the unit-sphere covariant derivative). If both faces carry the same smooth metric perturbation, any relative motion preserving metric equality in the common marked chart must be conformal Killing—a condition the quadrupole $V$ violates. Removing that trace-free mismatch requires either a dynamical comparison map between the two faces (whose displacement must be included in the net relative $V$ and its charge) or independent metric perturbations on the two sides. Moreover, matching this single quadrupole projection of the boundary equations does not establish a solution to the full Hamilton equations against all admissible test variations. [Angular constraint and required compensation](joint-angular.md)

Completing an intrinsic bracket for the isolated diamond therefore requires specifying the pullback of all cap, sheet, and joint terms on the constrained domain, exhibiting one admissible response that solves the Hamilton equations for **every** test variation in that domain (including the Hamilton equations of any surviving relative frame), and recovering the finite coefficients above whenever the construction reduces to the corresponding shared-frame preparation. A distributional inverse is an alternative outcome, provided it comes with a domain on which the proposed pairings exist and agree on overlaps. Taken together, these calculations establish a classical regularity obstruction to smooth inversion under the stated shared-frame conditions, define a concrete **proof gap** for an isolated independently marked cap, and leave the quantum domain and finite-rank claim open.
