# Section 3.3 and Appendix A.2 · Symplectic form, corners and edge modes

**Physical question.** Which gravitational variables on the null walls and corner of a causal diamond are canonically conjugate, why does corner area generate boosts, and is the paper's reduced symplectic two-form for a finite cap actually closed (a prerequisite for the Jacobi identity and consistent Poisson brackets)?

**Outcome.** The radiative normalization, the corner boost charge $\mathcal A/(8\pi G_N)$, the smooth-vertex formula (3.42b) (closed by construction), the linearized focusing Green function, and the bulk jump of the radiative inverse all check out. At a **finite cap**, however, there is an explicit obstruction: on the free variations allowed by (3.18)—where the cap-to-corner area ratio $b=\epsilon$ stays fixed while the corner area $A$ varies—the displayed two-form (A.14) has a nonzero field-space exterior derivative localized at the cap rim ($d\omega=\tfrac{b^3}{2}dA\wedge dz'(b)\wedge dz(b)$, evaluated directly to $1/256$ at $b=1/2$). Adding the closed cap potential (A.18) does not remove this defect. Restoring closure requires either additional cap and embedding terms from a covariant reduction or narrower boundary conditions before (A.14) can be inverted into Poisson brackets. A secondary affine-frame correction also applies to (A.23b).

## Charges and the smooth cone · (3.32)–(3.45)

Use $\delta$ for the field-space exterior derivative, $A=\varrho_S^2$ for the local area per reference solid angle, $K=A/(8\pi G_N)$, and $\mathcal A=\int_S A\epsilon_0$ for total corner area. With the sign convention $\delta H=\Omega(\delta,X_H)$ and $\Omega=\delta p\wedge\delta q$, one has $X_p q=+1$. For an embedding displacement $\chi=\delta X\circ X^{-1}$, differentiating a pullback gives $\delta(X^*T)=X^*(\delta T+\mathcal L_\chi T)$, so a spacetime diffeomorphism acting simultaneously on fields and embeddings cancels. Note that (3.32) defines an actual Hamiltonian for a chosen displacement only when the one-form $\Omega(\cdot,\delta_\xi)$ is exact; establishing integrability for arbitrary moving cuts requires a separate flux and boundary-condition analysis.

1. **Internal Lorentz invariance.** Varying $B\wedge F$ gives $B_{IJ}\wedge\delta\omega^{IJ}/(16\pi G_N)$. Under an infinitesimal internal Lorentz rotation $\delta_\lambda\omega=-D\lambda$, the torsion-free Gauss equation reduces the bulk potential to $-d(B_{IJ}\lambda^{IJ})/(16\pi G_N)$, which is canceled by the improvement (3.35) on the oriented gravitational branch. Equivalently, the metric potential (A.6) vanishes on internal rotations because $h_{ab}=0$. This argument relies on the torsion-free sector. [De Paoli and Speziale](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09685)
2. **Radiative normalization.** On an adapted screen, $E^2\wedge\delta K^3-E^3\wedge\delta K^2=\mu\,E^A{}_i\delta K_A{}^i$, which reduces at fixed tetrad to $\mu\,\delta\Theta_{(\ell)}$. The longitudinal piece in (3.37) contributes $\mu\,\delta\kappa$, and their outgoing oriented sign $-\epsilon_{\mathscr H}$ in (A.2) cancels against the varied boundary action. With the null frame fixed, $q^{AB}\delta q_{AB}=2\delta\log\varrho^2$ and $\sigma^{AB}\delta q_{AB}=\tfrac12(\partial_u\bar q)^{AB}\delta\bar q_{AB}$, which turn (A.9) into (3.39). Here $(\partial\bar q)^{AB}$ raises both indices *after* differentiating, as noted before (3.25).
3. **Corner boost charge.** For a pure frame boost $\delta_\eta\Gamma=\eta(y)$ holding geometric normals and radiative data fixed, (3.41)–(3.42) yield $\Omega(\delta,\delta_\eta)=\delta\int_S\eta K\epsilon_0$. Under a normal rescaling $\ell\mapsto e^t\ell$, $n\mapsto e^{-t}n$ at fixed metric, $\Upsilon(\partial_t)=1$. Together these confirm both the normalization $1/(8\pi G_N)$ and the rapidity orientation; in particular, a constant boost has charge $\mathcal A/(8\pi G_N)$.
4. **Origin of $J$ on the smooth cone.** Set $r=\sqrt A$, $F=\partial_v u=r e^{\Xi}$ along a smooth vertex-normalized ray, and $D=\delta_v u$. At fixed affine coordinate, $\delta_u\bar q=\delta_v\bar q-(D/F)\bar q'$ and $\delta_u\log\varrho^2=2\delta\log r-2D/(vF)$. In the orientation of (3.39), the bulk contribution divided by $K$ becomes

   $$
   \alpha-\delta\log r +2\int_0^1(v/F)'D\,dv
   =\alpha-2\delta\log r-2\delta J+2D(1)/F(1),
   $$

   where $\int_0^1 v\Xi(v)dv=J$ follows by reversing the order of integration. The term $-2K\delta\log r$ is exact. This confirms the printed radiative and $J$ coefficients, while showing that the moving-cut terms must supply $-2K\,D(1)/F(1)$ for the cancellation asserted before (3.42a); the fixed-normal bulk piece alone leaves this endpoint term uncancelled. Taking $\delta$ of the reported total potential (3.42a) reproduces every term of (3.42b), including the $-2K\delta J$ contribution when $K$ varies.

## Double-null corner and change of cut · (A.11)–(A.22), (A.28)–(A.29)

Since $\varrho^2=A v^2$, holding physical area fixed during a variation forces $\delta v=-(v/2)\delta\log A$, giving the second subtraction in (A.13) while the first removes the change of angular chart. Under angular relabeling, $A$ transforms as a density relative to $\epsilon_0$, making its Jacobian essential in (A.17a). Differentiating $u(b,z)$ gives (A.17b), and differentiating $A b^2\epsilon_0$ gives (A.17c). At fixed angular labels, the same chain rule produces $X=\delta_u z+[\delta_v u-v\delta\log A/(2\partial_u v)]\partial_u z$ in (A.19). The antisymmetrized displacement–displacement term in (A.20) reduces to $|\partial_u z|^2(Y'_u\partial_u Y_u-Y_u\partial_u Y'_u)$ (where primes on $Y$ denote the second variation), and (A.22) is the differential of the capped clock definition.

The sum of the two twist densities in (A.12) equals $2A\,d\lambda$. Fixing the left angular chart leaves two independent components of the right density. The two equal corner pieces in (A.14) combine into $\delta\lambda\wedge\delta K$, and the right angular piece gives $\delta p_i\wedge\delta Y^i$. Under (3.32), positive $K$ generates decreasing $\lambda$; adding $K\delta\gamma$ yields $K\delta(\gamma-\lambda)$ in (A.28), so increasing relative frame rapidity carries positive charge. Note that this Darboux-looking local angular term still coexists with bulk–angular mixed terms. For opposite null normals $n_p=e^{-\eta}\ell_q$ and $n_q=e^{-\eta}\ell_p$, differentiating along the identified corner gives $\varpi_p+Y^*\varpi_q=d\log(-\ell_p\cdot\ell_q)=d\eta$, and the area equality in (A.29) follows by pullback of its two-form.

## Finite-cap closure test · (A.14), (A.17)–(A.18)

A valid symplectic two-form must satisfy $d\omega=0$ on its declared space of variations; otherwise its inverse violates the Jacobi identity. We test (A.14) directly on the fixed-ratio cap data of (3.18).

Hold the angular maps and $\lambda$ fixed, and work on an angular patch in the diagonal polarization $z^2=0$ of (4.21), writing $z=z^1$ (whose target-space line element is exactly $dz^2$). On one branch keep $b=\epsilon$ fixed as prescribed by (3.18) and let $A$ vary. Writing $d$ for the field-space exterior derivative, a prime for $\partial_v$, and suppressing $\epsilon_0/(16\pi G_N)$, the relation $\delta^o z=dz-vz'dA/(2A)$ turns (A.14) into

$$
\omega=A\int_1^b v^2 dz'\wedge dz\,dv+dA\wedge B,
\qquad
B=-\tfrac12 z'(1)dz(1)+\tfrac12\int_1^b v^2\{v z' dz'-(z'+v z'')dz\}\,dv.
$$

Integrating by parts gives

$$
d_z B=\int_1^b v^2 dz'\wedge dz\,dv-\tfrac{b^3}{2}dz'(b)\wedge dz(b),
\qquad
\boxed{d\omega=\tfrac{b^3}{2}\,dA\wedge dz'(b)\wedge dz(b).}
$$

The explicit mixed corner term at $S$ cancels the lower-limit contribution at $v=1$, leaving the uncancelled remainder at the outer cap $v=b$, where both $z$ and $z'$ can vary freely under the fixed-ratio condition (3.18). On the three-parameter test family $z=x(1-v)^2+y(1-v)^3$, the corner value and corner derivative remain fixed; evaluating $d\omega$ on $(\partial_A,\partial_x,\partial_y)$ gives $b^3(1-b)^4/2$, which equals $1/256$ at $b=1/2$. Both shape variations can be localized inside the same angular patch and set to zero on the other branch, respecting corner matching of the displayed data. This obstruction vanishes only at a smooth vertex ($b=0$), when $dA=0$ pointwise, or when endpoint restrictions force $dz'(b)\wedge dz(b)=0$. Holding only the *total* corner area fixed still allows compensating local variations of $A$ outside the patch. On this restricted sector, adding $+(b^3/2)dA\wedge z'(b)dz(b)$ restores closure, though a genuine covariant repair must supply the complete cap and embedding terms and define the exact space of admissible data. Notice that the exterior derivative of the cap-joint potential (A.18) is already closed and cannot cancel $d\omega$ by itself. Pulling back to the image of a tighter solution or boundary-data map might remove the offending variations, but the paper would need to construct that map explicitly. By contrast, Reisenberger's original double-null bracket calculation fixes outer area data and specifies which endpoint variations are allowed. [Reisenberger, Eqs. (5)–(8)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.2541)

## Affine displacement and Green function · (A.23)–(A.27)

Let $p=\partial_u v>0$ and $\kappa=-p'/p=v\langle z',z'\rangle/4$. First hold the affine origin and normalization fixed so that $f=p\,\delta_v u$, and set $h=\delta_u z$. Differentiating gives $\delta_v z=h+fz'$ and $\delta\log p=-f'-\kappa f$; varying both expressions for $\kappa$ then yields

$$
f''+\kappa f'+\kappa'f
=\tfrac v2\langle z',\nabla_v h\rangle+2\kappa f'+(\kappa'-\kappa/v)f,
$$

which proves (A.24) under this fixed affine frame. Using the homogeneous solution $f=v$ and reduction of order gives $v\int_1^v dw/(w^2p(w))$. The printed kernel $G$ obeys $G(w,w)=0$, $\partial_v G(w,w)=1$, and the homogeneous equation off the diagonal, verifying the initial-value Green formula including its sign for $v<1$. Equation (A.23a) also enforces $f(1)=0$ for ordinary variations of the displayed variables.

A subtle correction appears once the affine frame itself is allowed to vary. Writing $u_c=u(1)$ and $s=e^{-\Gamma}(u-u_c)$, the chain rule with the literal fixed-$u$ derivative of (A.19) gives

$$
\delta_v z=\delta_u z+f z'+[\delta u_c+(u-u_c)\delta\Gamma]\,\partial_u z.
$$

For example, rescaling $u\mapsto e^t u$ while holding the area-coordinate data unchanged has $f=0$ and $\delta_v z=0$, yet $\delta_u z=-u\partial_u z$ for unit $\dot t$. Thus (A.23b)—and the forcing terms in (A.24) and (A.26)—require replacing $\delta_u^y z$ with the variation at fixed $s$, or restricting variations to a fixed affine frame. The stated homogeneous kernels are unaffected.

## Inverting the radiative form

At fixed $A$, the bulk term in (A.14) is proportional to $\int v^2(\langle\nabla_v h,k\rangle-\langle\nabla_v k,h\rangle)dv$. Setting $r(v)=\sqrt A\,v$ and integrating $\Omega(h,X)$ by parts produces the interior operator $-r\nabla_v(rX)/(8\pi G_N)$ together with an endpoint pairing. Differentiating $\operatorname{sgn}(v-w)$ gives $2\delta(v-w)$, verifying both the jump and the factor $-4\pi G_N/[r(v)r(w)]$ in (A.36). Transported homogeneous solutions $r^{-1}P(v,v_0)c$ remain free; their coefficients—and even the existence of a Hamiltonian vector field for a given source—depend on the cap and corner boundary conditions. For instance, on a single diagonal fixed-area branch with both endpoint values held fixed, the bulk equation admits a solution only if the weighted source has zero integral.

Equation (3.45) defines a pullback, not a proof of invertibility. Establishing nondegeneracy, the cap domain of the inverse, and a global canonical chart for the smeared observables remains open. In particular, (A.36) explicitly leaves an undetermined homogeneous kernel, and the final paragraph of Appendix A.3 invokes the full (A.14), which is directly affected by the finite-cap closure defect above. The local boost-charge normalization and the bulk jump of the Green function survive that obstruction.
