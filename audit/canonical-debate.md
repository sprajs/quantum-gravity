# What does it mean to quantize gravity canonically?

In his 26 November 2026 critique of Venkatesa Chandrasekaran’s null-cone paper ([arXiv:2611.34924v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1)), Jonathan Oppenheim writes that “suspicion should be raised immediately, because the paper works within a framework called ‘canonical quantisation’,” noting that the original programme of quantizing the spatial metric directly stalled in the 1980s. He acknowledges loop quantum gravity as a developed canonical approach and correctly notes that Chandrasekaran solves the bulk constraints before quantizing. His substantive technical objections target microstate counting, diffeomorphism invariance, operator ordering, time, and the classical limit. [Oppenheim’s article](https://superposer.substack.com/p/has-ai-come-for-quantum-gravity) · [Chandrasekaran, version 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1)

Deepak Vaid’s visible reply highlights that opening “suspicion” sentence and writes: *“Ok Jonathan. I think at this point I do have to say that I don't think you've understood what canonical quantization, in the context of 3+1 GR, is. What you're repeating is an old myth first spread by Susskind in his paper on the same topic.”*

Two balanced points follow immediately:

1. **On the narrow historical framing, Vaid has a valid objection.** A warning about Wheeler–DeWitt quantization of the spatial metric on a spacelike slice does not automatically carry over to characteristic quantization on null cones (where constraints become ordinary differential equations along light rays), and “suspicion” is a triage heuristic rather than a no-go theorem. No theorem proves that every canonical approach to four-dimensional gravity is impossible.
2. **On the paper’s technical claims, Vaid’s visible reply is silent.** The visible excerpt supplies no technical argument and does not address Oppenheim’s three concrete checks on state counting, cut invariance, or continuum dynamics. Nor does it name a specific Susskind paper (and Oppenheim’s article does not cite Susskind), so the historical attribution remains unverified. For Vaid’s own published account of canonical variables and quantum geometry, see his work with Sundance Bilson-Thompson. [Bilson-Thompson and Vaid](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3586)

## Which choices does “canonical” leave open?

“Canonical quantization” is an umbrella covering several independent choices:

- **Choice of variables.** In DeWitt’s 1967 spacelike formulation, the configuration variable is the three-dimensional spatial metric and its conjugate momentum is built from extrinsic curvature. In 1986 Ashtekar replaced them with a complex connection and a densitized triad; Barbero later introduced a real-variable version. Chandrasekaran instead starts from first-order variables restricted to intersecting null cones.
- **When constraints are solved.** In *Dirac quantization*, classical constraints become operator equations annihilating physical states. In *reduced phase-space quantization*, one solves the constraints and quotients by gauge transformations classically, then quantizes the remaining physical degrees of freedom. (Version 1 is in fact a hybrid: it solves bulk null constraints classically while imposing corner-charge matching on quantum states in §4.4.)
- **Symmetry restrictions.** Reducing the constraints can retain gravitational waves of arbitrary shape within its patch; imposing a symmetry such as cylindrical symmetry is an independent restriction on the waves themselves. [DeWitt](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.160.1113) · [Ashtekar](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.2244) · [Barbero](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9410014)

Two well-known $3+1$-dimensional results illustrate both the reach and the open problems of canonical methods:

- **Evolution in a symmetry-reduced sector.** In 1971 Karel Kuchař treated cylindrical Einstein–Rosen waves by both Dirac and reduced methods. Using cylindrical radius and constant Einstein–Rosen time slices, the quantum constraint equations reproduce the reduced evolution across infinitely many wave degrees of freedom—illustrating how a constraint annihilating a state can still encode evolution relative to a physical clock (though cylindrical symmetry is essential). Ashtekar and Pierri later analyzed the boundary conditions and regularization of this model. [Kuchař](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.4.955) · [Ashtekar and Pierri](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9606085)
- **Kinematical geometry before physical dynamics.** In loop quantum gravity, Ashtekar and Lewandowski constructed self-adjoint area operators with discrete spectra on the kinematical Hilbert space. Constructing physical transition amplitudes that recover Einstein evolution remains harder: for example, Lewandowski and Marolf showed that on a specified space of test states, the commutator of two Hamiltonian constraints vanished for a class of proposed operators even where spatial diffeomorphisms acted nontrivially, underscoring how sensitive dynamics are to operator and domain choices. [Area operators](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9602046) · [Constraint commutators](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9710016)

**Note on the unverified Susskind reference.** One *candidate* paper containing the phrase “canonical quantum gravity” is Susskind and Uglum’s 1994 black-hole entropy paper—though there is no evidence it is the paper Vaid meant. In §3, their Euclidean calculation links the leading horizon-entropy divergence of quantum fields to the renormalization of Newton’s constant, expressly conditioning any inference of infinite entropy on the renormalized gravitational coupling vanishing. Goroff and Sagnotti’s two-loop ultraviolet divergence of perturbative pure Einstein gravity is a separate, established result under perturbative assumptions. Neither calculation rules out every nonperturbative canonical representation. [Susskind and Uglum, §3, p. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9401070v3#page=15) · [Goroff and Sagnotti](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(86)90193-8)

## What would settle the issue for the null-cone proposal?

Historical debates about canonical gravity cannot decide whether version 1 works; three concrete, checkable calculations can:

1. **Intermediate-cut (refoliation) invariance.** Classically, version 1 shows that moving the metric and its boundary reference frame together leaves a dressed observer’s reading unchanged (§3.5). At the quantum level, §5.2 and Appendix E.1 require amplitudes to remain invariant when an *intermediate cut* moves while external preparations and sources stay fixed. Computing one regulated amplitude across two genuinely distinct internal cuts—and tracking its continuum limit—directly tests that stronger quantum claim. (Changing the external frame alone changes the experiment.) [Paper §§3.2–3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS2) · [Closed-cut audit](04-gluing.md) · [Refoliation test](refoliation-test.md) · [Appendix E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A5.SS1)
2. **Anomaly budget and reduction independence.** Rather than treating strict Dirac quantization of the null constraint (A.30b) as an automatically available benchmark, one must first compute the central charge of the null reparametrization constraint algebra (including the area-density sector): if the total central charge is nonzero—as reported for null-surface quantization in [Ciambelli, Freidel, and Leigh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11132)—no nonzero state can be annihilated by every constraint mode. Independently, comparing quantizations built from different classical reductions of the same null phase space (such as area gauge versus affine parameter) tests whether the quantum theory depends on the choice of reduction. [Reduction comparison](reduction-test.md)
3. **Continuum Einstein evolution.** Section 6.1 follows smooth, sharply peaked data formally via Weyl symbols, while Appendix C.2 proves a time-ordered exponential only for bounded, norm-integrable generators. Constructing the interacting gravitational evolution with a controlled continuum limit—and proving that expectation values of dressed observables follow classical Einstein solutions over a stated time interval, plus matching low-energy graviton self-interactions—would directly settle the dynamical critique. [Paper §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S6.SS1) · [Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS2) · [Evolution audit](evolution.md) · [Scattering audit](scattering.md)
