# Section 6.2 · Graviton fluctuations and backreaction

**Physical question.** How do quantum graviton fluctuations curve the average spacetime metric at order $\hbar$, and does the paper's fixed-smearing expansion produce a renormalized, local one-loop semiclassical Einstein equation?

**Outcome.** For any smooth family of vacuum solutions with a finite, centered Gaussian fluctuation covariance, equations (6.12)–(6.16) hold with the printed factor of two, plus sign, conservation of the quadratic source on linearized solutions, and retarded metric response. An independent exact calculation on a vacuum plane wave in Rosen coordinates confirms both sides of (6.13) directly. What remains open is promoting this fixed-smearing identity into a renormalized, local one-loop equation for the continuum theory: the paper explicitly keeps field smearings fixed in (6.14) and never takes the differentiated coincidence limit. As a flat-space test shows below, an individual smeared graviton-derivative variance diverges as $\hbar/(8\pi^2\ell^4)$ when the smearing width $\ell\to 0$, so smearing *before* multiplying differs fundamentally from defining a renormalized local composite operator on the diagonal. Completing the continuum one-loop equation requires a Hadamard-type condition on high-frequency mode widths, a covariant parametrix subtraction with local Ward-identity counterterms, and a renormalization prescription for the closed-time-path effective action and boundary terms.

## Reconstruction and the mean metric

1. **Equations (6.6)–(6.8).** Linearizing the torsion and simplicity equations around an invertible tetrad recovers the Levi-Civita connection variation. Holding $\Lambda$ fixed and differentiating the trace-reversed vacuum equation gives $\delta R_{ab}=\Lambda\delta g_{ab}$, which is equivalent to $E'[\delta g]=0$ on an Einstein background. For the relationally dressed field, applying $\delta'$ to $\delta(X^*T)=X^*(\delta T+\mathcal L_\chi T)$ produces all five terms of (6.8), including $\mathcal L_{\delta'\chi}g$ and the ordered $\mathcal L_{\chi'}\mathcal L_\chi g$. Diffeomorphism covariance ensures $E[X^*g]=X^*E[g]=0$ wherever $X$ defines coordinates.
2. **Equations (6.9)–(6.10).** Write $h_I=\partial_I g(\phi_0)$ and $k_{IJ}=\partial_I\partial_J g(\phi_0)$ in the *same* relational coordinates. Taylor-expanding in centered initial fluctuations yields $\Delta g=\tfrac12 k_{IJ}\Sigma^{IJ}+O(\hbar^2)$ and $\mathcal G(p,p')=h_I(p)\Sigma^{IJ}h_J(p')+O(\hbar^2)$, because odd centered Gaussian moments vanish and fourth moments are $O(\hbar^2)$. Restoring half the commutator adds the $+i\hbar J^{IJ}/2$ term in (6.10). This holds at a fixed mode cutoff (or for convergent smooth data) over an interval of smooth classical evolution, assuming Weyl symbols match the classical Hamiltonian and observable with no $O(\hbar)$ subprincipal term; mode-by-mode Moyal counting does not by itself supply a uniform continuum remainder.
3. **Equations (6.11)–(6.13).** At $g_0$, set $E'=DE$ and $E^{(2)}=\tfrac12 D^2 E$. Differentiating the exact identity $E[g(\phi)]=0$ twice gives

   $$
   E'[k_{IJ}]+2E^{(2)}[h_I,h_J]=0.
   $$

   Contracting with $\Sigma^{IJ}/2$ and using $E[g_0+\Delta g]=E'[\Delta g]+O(\hbar^2)$ yields (6.13) with its plus sign. Because this identity follows for *any* smooth ensemble of classical solutions sharing those first two moments, the quantum input enters strictly through the choice and normalization of $\Sigma$, the commutator, and the assumed Weyl evolution; (6.13) alone does not distinguish different quantizations that agree on those data to $O(\hbar)$.
4. **Equations (6.15)–(6.16).** Writing $B_g$ for the metric-dependent divergence operator, the $\varepsilon^2$ coefficient of the Bianchi identity $B_{g_0+\varepsilon h} E[g_0+\varepsilon h]=0$ is $B_0 E^{(2)}[h,h]+B'_0[h]E'[h]+\tfrac12 B''_0[h,h]E[g_0]$. The last two terms vanish on linearized vacuum solutions, and polarization extends conservation to off-diagonal contractions $h_I,h_J$. Applying a retarded right inverse $G_R$ of $E'$ on the constrained, gauge-reduced source space then gives $\Delta g=\Delta g_0-G_R\langle E^{(2)}\rangle$ to this order, with the minus sign fixed by (6.13) and the homogeneous piece $\Delta g_0$ fixed by boundary data. Note that causality of $G_R$ refers to source propagation in the fixed reference coordinates; because a relationally dressed observable also depends on its boundary frame and the geodesic path defining $X$, confining physical dependence strictly to the causal past of its endpoint requires controlling that path and anchor as well. ## Independent check on a vacuum plane wave

To verify both sides of (6.13) independently on a single smooth radiative mode, take $\Lambda=0$ in Rosen coordinates on a regular patch:

$$
ds^2=-2\,du\,dv+a_s(u)^2dx^2+b_s(u)^2dy^2,\qquad
a_s=\cos(\sqrt s\,u),\quad b_s=\cosh(\sqrt s\,u).
$$

Both $a_s$ and $b_s$ are real-analytic in signed $s$ around $s=0$. Since $a_s''=-s a_s$ and $b_s''=s b_s$, the sole potentially nonzero Ricci component vanishes identically: $R_{uu}=-a_s''/a_s-b_s''/b_s=0$, while tidal curvature is nonzero for $s\ne 0$. Expanding in a small-variance centered Gaussian ensemble in $s$ with $\langle s^2\rangle=v$ gives

$$
g_{xx}=1-su^2+\tfrac13 s^2u^4+O(s^3),\qquad
g_{yy}=1+su^2+\tfrac13 s^2u^4+O(s^3).
$$

Through order $v$, the mean metric shift is $\Delta g_{xx}=\Delta g_{yy}=v u^4/3$, giving linearized curvature $E'[\Delta g]_{uu}=-\tfrac12(\Delta g_{xx}+\Delta g_{yy})''=-4vu^2$. Meanwhile, retaining only the linear fluctuation $X=1\pm su^2$ inside $-(\sqrt X)''/\sqrt X=-X''/(2X)+(X')^2/(4X^2)$ and summing over $x,y$ yields $E^{(2)}_{uu}[sh,sh]=4s^2u^2$. Taking the expectation cancels the linearized mean curvature exactly. This confirms the differential equation and its factor of two on an exact nonlinear family; because only one mode fluctuates, there is no ultraviolet divergence to renormalize.

## The coincidence limit and the physical source

**Flat-space test of (6.14).** Consider one canonically normalized helicity amplitude $H$ of a free massless graviton in the Minkowski vacuum, smeared spatially with the Gaussian Fourier multiplier $e^{-\ell^2|\boldsymbol k|^2/2}$. Its derivative variance is

$$
\langle(\partial_t H_\ell)^2\rangle
=\frac{\hbar}{2}\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}|\boldsymbol k|e^{-\ell^2|\boldsymbol k|^2}
=\frac{\hbar}{8\pi^2\ell^4}.
$$

Restoring metric normalization multiplies by a constant proportional to $G_N$, and tensor contractions adjust the component coefficients. This checks a single derivative contraction of the type appearing in (6.14) without presupposing the value of the full sum in $E^{(2)}$. Halving the smearing length $\ell$ multiplies this contraction by sixteen. Keeping both field legs smeared yields a finite, resolution-dependent observable, whereas smearing a *local* composite source after multiplication requires first renormalizing the product on the diagonal $p'=p$. Even in flat space, those two procedures do not agree.

In a continuum tadpole calculation, one instead specifies a physical state with the proper short-distance singularity and a covariant renormalization rule. Schematically, the renormalized bulk source takes the form $[D_{ab}(\mathcal G-H_{\rm sing})]_{p'=p}+C_{ab}$ (with parallel transport of primed indices), where $D_{ab}$ is the bilinear differential operator from $E^{(2)}$, $H_{\rm sing}$ is the state-independent singular parametrix in the chosen gauge, and $C_{ab}$ collects local terms fixed by the Ward identity and renormalization conditions. A reduced-phase-space computation must also match the gauge and measure terms of the covariant calculation. The smooth-mode Bianchi identity verified above is a useful check, but conservation before regularization does not guarantee conservation after parametrix subtraction. Allen, Folacci, and Ottewill carry out the graviton stress-tensor construction on vacuum spacetimes with $\Lambda$, and Fröb and Taslimi Tehrani derive graviton parametrices and Ward identities across a family of gauges. [Allen, Folacci and Ottewill](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.38.1069) · [Fröb and Taslimi Tehrani](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00444)

Equation (6.6) bounds the variance of each fixed smearing, while the Gaussian representation of §4 allows wide freedom in choosing mode widths. A continuum Hadamard property requires a collective constraint on high-frequency widths. Appendix D.2 does subtract a vacuum singularity for the shear two-point function near a shear-free corner—a useful local check—but that subtraction supplies neither the general dressed metric parametrix nor the complete composite tensor in (6.13). A clean, tractable continuum test would compare two admissible states sharing the same short-distance singularity, background, and dressing, so that the common bulk subtraction cancels in the difference of their sources. [Manuscript, Gaussian widths](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS2.p2.1) · [Manuscript, shear subtraction](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS2.p3.1)

**One-loop effective action.** Appendix D.1 sets up the formal Gaussian determinant with the reduced measure and boundary Hessian on a closed time path to obtain a real, retarded expectation equation. However, the closed-time-path normalization $Z[j,j]=1$ constrains only equal sources on the two branches; it still allows local counterterms $S_{\rm loc}[g_+]-S_{\rm loc}[g_-]$ whose variation on a *single* branch at $g_+=g_-$ is nonzero. A normalized $\operatorname{Tr}\log$ therefore still requires a renormalization prescription for its source derivatives, functional measure, local bulk counterterms, and boundary or corner terms. In four dimensions, candidate bulk terms include cosmological-constant and Einstein–Hilbert renormalizations together with curvature-squared variations (some curvature-squared variations vanish on smooth 4D Einstein backgrounds, and the Euler term is a boundary variation, so merely listing candidate terms does not prove a nonzero bulk shift in a particular case). Likewise, the familiar one-loop on-shell finiteness of pure gravity at $\Lambda=0$ does not define a local composite stress tensor, and for $\Lambda\ne 0$ the standard one-loop on-shell divergence includes a volume term. [Manuscript, D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A4.SS1) · [Calzetta and Hu](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.35.495) · [’t Hooft and Veltman](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPA_1974__20_1_69_0/) · [Christensen and Duff](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(80)90423-X)

**Range of the result.** Formally, the effective graviton source is $t_{ab}^{\rm grav}=-\langle E^{(2)}_{ab}\rangle/(8\pi G_N)$, and the split between this source and the mean metric shift depends on the reference frame. Under a first-order coordinate change, $h\mapsto h+\mathcal L_\xi g_0$ while the second-order coefficient transforms as $k\mapsto k+\mathcal L_\xi h+\tfrac12\mathcal L_\xi^2 g_0$; covariance preserves only the *sum* $E'[k]+E^{(2)}[h,h]$ on a linearized solution, making the paper's common relational frame essential for comparing the two terms. Unlike Isaacson's gauge-invariant gravitational-wave stress tensor—which relies on short-wavelength averaging—this equation describes vacuum graviton and edge fluctuations in a chosen coherent preparation with no matter stress tensor. Testing the claimed continuum equivalence would require a regulator-matched curved-background computation of a dressed tadpole under the paper's null and corner boundary conditions. [Isaacson, high-frequency gravitational radiation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.166.1272)
