# Section 3.2 and Appendices A.1, A.3 · Null initial data, clocks and constraints

**Physical question.** What free gravitational data live on the null boundary of a causal diamond, how does gravitational shear turn the light-cone clock into an upper bound on corner area, and where does the paper's area-radius coordinate break down?

**Outcome.** The vacuum focusing equation, the exponential clock formula on expanding generators, and the classical upper bound on corner area at fixed clock reading all hold—indeed, an explicit affine integral shows that the classical area ceiling survives even across a smooth turning point where expansion changes sign. Three limitations qualify the single-diamond quantization built on these formulas: (1) the phase space defined in (3.18)–(3.19) permits generators whose expansion vanishes before any caustic or crossing, where the area-ratio coordinate $v=r/r_S$ stops being single-valued (demonstrated below on an exact vacuum plane wave); (2) classically, the clock constrains radial shear along each ray without limiting arbitrarily rapid transverse angular oscillations, so any quantum state-count bound needs further input; and (3) in Appendix A.3, while the interior Green functions and coupled differential equations verify, equation (A.32) drops a field-space exact term (harmless for $\delta\Theta$) and equation (A.36) leaves the endpoint homogeneous kernel $\mathsf k_\partial$ implicit rather than fixed on a concrete test-function domain.

## Optical constraint and clock

Along a torsion-free, hypersurface-orthogonal affine null congruence, commuting the angular coordinate vectors with the null tangent gives $K_{AB}=\tfrac12\partial_u q_{AB}$. On the oriented tetrad branch, $\star_\eta B=-e\wedge e$, so contracting the $uA$ component with $n\cdot\ell=-1$ and $n\cdot E_A=0$ recovers $E_A^\mu$ as in (3.17). Writing $q_{AB}=r^2\bar q_{AB}$ with $r=\varrho$ and fixed conformal determinant gives $\operatorname{tr}(\bar q^{-1}\partial_u\bar q)=0$ and

$$
\Theta=2r'/r,\qquad \sigma_{AB}=\tfrac12r^2\partial_u\bar q_{AB},\qquad
\sigma_{AB}\sigma^{AB}=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}[(\bar q^{-1}\partial_u\bar q)^2].
$$

The trace is nonnegative because the endomorphism is self-adjoint with respect to the positive cut metric. Note a convention detail: the raised derivative in (3.25f) means the derivative of the covariant metric with indices raised afterward (differentiating the inverse metric directly carries a minus sign). Using vacuum $R_{\ell\ell}=\Lambda g_{\ell\ell}=0$, the Raychaudhuri equation reduces to $r''=-r\sigma^2/2$, verifying all coefficients in (3.25). Near a smooth vertex, Jacobi separation scales as $u$ times the initial angular separation, so the boundary condition $r'(0)=1$ requires matching the fiducial solid-angle form and affine normalization in the same local frame.

On a strictly expanding generator, set $v=r/r_S$ and $p_\epsilon=r'(u_0)>0$. Rewriting the affine focusing equation in terms of $v$ yields

$$
\frac{d\log r'}{dv}=-\frac v8\operatorname{tr}[(\bar q^{-1}\partial_v\bar q)^2]=-\Xi'(v),
\quad
r'=p_\epsilon e^{-\Xi(v)},\quad
u_*=p_\epsilon(u_S-u_0)=r_S\int_\epsilon^1 e^{\Xi(v)}dv .
$$

This reproduces (3.26)–(3.27). An affine reparametrization $u\mapsto au+b$ scales $p_\epsilon$ by $a^{-1}$ and the interval by $a$, leaving $u_*$ invariant. The clock still depends, however, on the fiducial solid-angle form $\epsilon_0$ chosen to define the angular boundary frame: rescaling $\epsilon_0\mapsto h(y)\epsilon_0$ at fixed geometry rescales $r_S$ and $p_\epsilon$ by $h^{-1/2}$, whereas a simultaneous angular diffeomorphism pulls everything back consistently. On the future cone, applying the positive-slope formula literally requires orienting the affine parameter from the future cap back to $S$; for the future-directed $\ell_q$ of Fig. 7, both the slope and the affine interval are negative in flat space, so their product stays positive and the logarithmic equation uses $\log|r'|$.

A direct affine integration checks the area bound even across a smooth turning point. Orienting $u$ from the cap to the corner and integrating $r''=-r\sigma^2/2$ twice gives

$$
u_*=(1-\epsilon)r_S+
\frac12\int_{u_0}^{u_S}(u_S-s)\,r(s)\sigma^2(s)\,ds .
$$

This identity requires only a smooth positive-area congruence and the cap ratio $r(u_0)=\epsilon r_S$; it implies $u_*\ge(1-\epsilon)r_S$ even if $r'$ changes sign along the ray. From (3.28), setting $a=\int_S r_S\epsilon_0$ with $\int_S\epsilon_0=4\pi$ yields $\bar u_*\ge(1-\epsilon)\mathscr A/a\ge(1-\epsilon)\sqrt{\mathscr A/(4\pi)}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Equality in both inequalities holds precisely for zero shear along every generator and constant $r_S$, realized by spherical vacuum data.

This is a classical area ceiling at fixed clock reading. Because $\Xi$ integrates only *radial* derivatives of $\bar q$, it does not directly restrain transverse angular oscillations: in a local angular frame, the family $\bar q=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2h},e^{-2h})$ with $h=g(v)\chi(y)\sin(ny^1)$ (for fixed smooth bumps $g,\chi$) keeps the radial derivative squared in $\Xi$ uniformly bounded in $n$ while angular derivatives grow without bound. Any quantum bound on the number of states therefore requires input beyond this classical focusing inequality.

## Where the area chart ends

The phase-space conditions in (3.18) require positive cross-sectional area and no generator crossings, both of which can persist past the first point where the expansion turns around ($r'=0$). An explicit vacuum plane wave shows this directly:

$$
ds^2=-2du\,dw+dx^2+dy^2+(x^2-y^2)du^2 .
$$

Because the transverse Laplacian of $x^2-y^2$ vanishes, $R_{uu}=0$ and the metric is Ricci-flat. Along the central null ray from a point vertex, the transverse geodesic equations give the Jacobi matrix $J=\operatorname{diag}(\sinh u,\sin u)$ for $J(0)=0$, $J'(0)=I$, so $r=\sqrt{\sinh u\sin u}$. The first positive zero of $r'$ solves $\coth u+\cot u=0$ at $u=2.36502037$, well before the first caustic ($\det J=0$) at $u=\pi$. Explicitly, $r(1)=0.99443336$, $r(2.5)=1.90286070$, and $r'(2.5)=-0.30929176$, with cap ratio $r(1)/r(2.5)=0.52259914$. For every $1<v<r(2.36502037)/r(2.5)$, two distinct affine points on the ray carry the same area ratio $v=r/r(2.5)$.

Thus a zero of $r'$ terminates the area chart before a caustic ($\det J=0$) occurs, and distinct generators can also cross even where $\det J\neq 0$. The manuscript distinguishes these events on p. 16. Restricting (3.19) to strictly expanding sheets, introducing overlapping charts, or quantizing the direct affine clock formula above would define distinct versions of the single-region problem. Covering a smooth vacuum spacetime with sufficiently small diamonds supplies a local classical description past an earlier caustic: in (3.29) Cauchy uniqueness governs the shared cap's domain of dependence, while extending beyond that domain in (3.30)–(3.31) relies on the additional overlap identifications, triple-overlap compatibility, and the stipulated smooth manifold. That classical patching does not by itself supply a spectral identification for the original diamond's clock or a quantum measure relating different covers. The classical null initial-data construction likewise separates caustics from crossings. [Reisenberger, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.3880v1)

## Tetrad, normalizations and symplectic potential

In the metric (B.1) with $\ell=\partial_v$, the normalization $n\cdot\ell=-1$ yields $\beta_v=\partial_v\log N$ and $\beta_A=(\partial_A N-q_{AB}\partial_v V^B)/(2N)$, reproducing (A.1) for this generator choice. In commuting coordinates, the tangential Cartan equation gives $D_\ell E_A=D_A\ell+T_{\ell A}$; taking its screen trace gives $\mathcal L_\ell\boldsymbol\mu=(\Theta+E^A_\mu T^\mu_{\ell A})\boldsymbol\mu$. This explains why (A.2) cancels against the boundary variation *before* torsion is set to zero, and why the improved action reduces to the inaffinity term in (A.3)–(A.4). Varying $De=0$ and $F=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega$ recovers (A.5), and metric variation of Einstein–Hilbert gives the boundary divergence $\nabla_b h^{ab}-\nabla^a h$ in (A.6).

For (A.7)–(A.10), hold the hypersurface and its defining function $t$ fixed. Keeping the surface null throughout the variation imposes $h_{\ell\ell}=0$ there, which gives $\upsilon^a=-h^{ab}\ell_b$, $q^{ab}h_{ab}=h-2n\cdot\upsilon$, and $\delta\boldsymbol\eta=(h/2-\delta\psi)\boldsymbol\eta$. Holding $\psi$ fixed first, expanding the exact pieces and (A.9) gives $16\pi G_N\theta/\eta=2\delta\kappa+\mathcal L_\ell h+\Theta h/2+\sigma^{ab}h_{ab}+\operatorname{div}_\eta\upsilon+(2\kappa-\Theta)n\cdot\upsilon-2\varpi\cdot\upsilon$, matching eq. (6.8) of Chandrasekaran and Flanagan. For a pure normalization shift $\delta\psi=s$ at fixed metric, $\delta\kappa=s\kappa+\mathcal L_\ell s$, and the three contributions to $16\pi G_N\theta/\eta$ are $2\mathcal L_\ell s$, $-2(\mathcal L_\ell s+\Theta s)$, and $2\Theta s$, whose sum vanishes. Differentiating $\ell_a=-e^\psi\partial_a t$ gives $\kappa=\mathcal L_\ell\psi-\tfrac12 n^a\partial_a(\ell^2)$ on the null surface, so varying produces the transverse derivative in (A.10), which can remain nonzero even when $h_{\ell\ell}=0$. [Chandrasekaran and Flanagan, §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03871v2#S6.SS3)

## Area variable, Green functions and the endpoint inverse

Write $A=r^2$ for the **local** area density relative to $\epsilon_0$, and let $t=\dot\zeta$ denote the target-space velocity. In affine gauge, $\mu=r'/r$ and $|z'|^2=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}[(\bar q^{-1}\partial_u\bar q)^2]$, so the left side of (A.30b) becomes $2rr''+A|z'|^2/2=A\mathfrak C_\ell$. In nonaffine parameter $v$, the relation $V''/V'=\mu$ gives $A''-\mu A'=(V')^2\ddot A$, proving (A.31) with $D=\partial_V^2+|t|^2/2$. In affine parameter $u$ this means $dV/du=cr$ with $c$ constant along each generator. Integrating $\int fA''$ by parts twice yields the endpoint concomitant $[Af'-fA']$ with the sign used when improving the constraint generator.

**Exact-term correction in (A.32).** Substituting into (A.30a) uses $-\mu\delta A=A\delta\mu-\delta(A\mu)$. Integrating $A\delta\mu=A\partial_v(\delta V'/V')$ by parts produces the displayed cut terms and the area part of the bulk constraint, while the radiative shift $\delta z=\delta\zeta+\dot\zeta\,\delta V$ supplies the shear part. With the same $\Theta_\partial$ and fixed $v$ integration bounds, the literal identity is

$$
\Theta_{\rm(A.30a)}
=\mathrm{RHS}_{\rm(A.32)}
-\frac{1}{8\pi G_N}\,\delta\int A\mu\,dv .
$$

Because the omitted term is field-space exact, it changes only the choice of symplectic potential representative and drops out of $\delta\Theta$. The reduced bulk two-form and the homogeneous-data Green function therefore survive this correction.

Indeed, wherever $A>0$ and $DA=0$, the kernel $G(V,s)=A(V)A(s)\int_s^V A(t')^{-2}dt'$ satisfies $G(s,s)=0$, $\partial_V G(s,s)=1$, and $D_V G=0$ away from the diagonal. Linearizing $DA=0$ gives $D\delta A=-A\delta|t|^2/2$, reproducing (A.33) for fixed initial area density and slope.

At fixed $A$, the differential operator acting on radiative variations is $-r\mathsf g\nabla_v(r\,\cdot)/(8\pi G_N)$. Parallel transporting the tangent index and using $\partial_v\operatorname{sgn}(v-w)=2\delta(v-w)$ verifies the interior jump and normalization of (A.34)–(A.36). The homogeneous kernel consists of transported constants divided by $r$. On a finite interval, inverting a first-order operator depends on endpoint conditions: the displayed sign kernel verifies the interior jump, while (A.36) leaves $\mathsf k_\partial$ unspecified as an explicit kernel, referring back to (A.14) to fix it. Unrestricted existence and uniqueness of the inverse on a concrete space of test functions and cut variations therefore do not follow from the jump calculation alone.

For variable area, put $a_U=\delta_U A$, so $Da_U=-A\,t\cdot\nabla_V U$ and $Db=t\cdot U$. The adjoint of the initial-data Green function satisfies homogeneous final conditions, so integrating $-\int a_\eta\,t\cdot U$ by parts yields $-\nabla_V(Atb)$ in (A.38). Defining $w=U-bt/2$, $y=a_U-b\dot A/2$, and $f=\mathcal D_z U$, direct substitution gives

$$
(2A\nabla_V+\dot A)w=f-ty,\qquad
\ddot b=t\cdot w,\qquad
\ddot y=-t\cdot f/2.
$$

Differentiating $y$ at the initial cut with $a_U=\dot a_U=0$ reproduces (A.42), confirming the interior reduction (A.37)–(A.42). Computing the mixed Poisson brackets for arbitrary cut motions still requires explicit endpoint and corner equations together with their functional domain.
