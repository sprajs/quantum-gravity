# Appendix E — Lorentzian amplitudes and the Euclidean cylinder

**Physical question.** When does a Lorentzian gravitational theory with a lower-bounded Hamiltonian define an equivalent Euclidean path-integral theory with a finite cylinder trace—and does first-order null-cone gravity satisfy the axioms needed for that equivalence?

**Outcome.** The operator-theoretic core is sound: analytic continuation between a lower-bounded continuous unitary group and a positive Euclidean cylinder semigroup works cleanly, and under Axioms 1–5, equations (E.6)–(E.12) reconstruct the Hilbert algebra, tracial weights, and finite-rank energy bands on a fixed cut. Three gaps separate this framework from an unconditional equivalence for gravity. First, Axiom 2's reflection-positivity phrasing must be restricted to canonical reflected pairings rather than arbitrary single-sided boundary permutations. Second, identifying an independently defined geometric Euclidean path integral with the Lorentzian reconstruction in (E.13)–(E.14) requires matching amplitudes across non-cylinder topologies, which cylinder operators and thermal traces do not fix (as a two-dimensional Euler-characteristic deformation illustrates). Third, the first-order gravity realization in (E.15)–(E.18) verifies operator identities *inside* an assumed Hilbert-space trace, importing the dimension and AdS heat-trace conclusions of §5.2 rather than proving closed-cut sewing independently. [Manuscript, pp. 76–80](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=76) · [Manuscript, TeX lines 3596–3770](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A5)

## E.1. What the axioms require

Axioms 1–5 postulate cut invariance for a designated class of configurations, reflection positivity, multiplicativity on disjoint closed manifolds, strongly continuous unitary time evolution, and finite pairings with compact energy filters. Each is substantial physical input: knowing a one-parameter translation group on a fixed spatial cut does not by itself tell one how to sum amplitudes across topologies. Within these axioms, substituting the spectral resolution of $T(t)=e^{-itH/\hbar}$ into (E.4) reproduces $F_g=g(H)$ with the printed Fourier normalization, and choosing a smooth cutoff equal to $1$ on a bounded interval $I$ verifies $\nu(I)<\infty$ via (E.5). [Manuscript, pp. 76–77](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=77)

**Which pairings are positive matters.** Axiom 1 allows cross-gluing matching boundary components, and Axiom 2 asserts positivity of the resulting Gram form for every allowed pairing. Read literally to permit permuting two identical boundaries on only one side of the Gram form, this fails already in two dimensions. Take two identical cuts, each with orthogonal preparable states $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$, and form the antisymmetric vector

$$
v=(|0\rangle\otimes|1\rangle-|1\rangle\otimes|0\rangle)/\sqrt{2}.
$$

Its canonical reflected double has $\langle v,v\rangle=1$, whereas swapping the two boundaries on just the ket gives $\langle v,Sv\rangle=-1$ (in the basis $|01\rangle,|10\rangle$, the swap matrix has zeros on the diagonal and ones off-diagonal, so one eigenvalue is $-1$). Relabeling both halves simultaneously, as in E.3, preserves positivity of the ordinary Gram matrix. If "allowed pairing" in Axiom 2 already means the canonical reflected identification of labels, this is purely a wording clarification: a precise statement allows cross-gluings as matrix elements or new preparations, while requiring positivity of the Gram matrix built from each preparation and its own adjoint—matching the cited Euclidean reflection axiom. [Manuscript, axioms 1–2 and (E.16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=77) · [Colafranceschi, Dong, Marolf and Wang, §2.2, Axiom 3, and §2.3, Definition 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02189#page=10)

## E.2. Reconstruction and its range

1. **Trace and Hilbert space, (E.6)–(E.7).** Closing a pair of multiplications cyclically defines a positive tracial form. The four-boundary exchange inequality—using positivity of canonical reflected Gram forms on the larger space—gives $\|ab\|_2^2\le\|a\|_2^2\|b\|_2^2$, letting multiplication descend modulo null vectors and act by bounded left and right operators. Compact exterior energy supports satisfy $e_m a=a e_m=a$ for large $m$, so products span the dense algebra and its Hilbert-algebra completion carries a normal semifinite trace $\tau$. Spectral functions supported below $E_0$ have zero Hilbert norm and zero left/right action, so the reconstructed generators are bounded below. For positive $A$, equation (E.7a) evaluates $\tau(A^{1/2}\chi_m(H)^2A^{1/2})$, whose argument increases monotonically to $A$, giving (E.7a) by normality and (E.7b) by spectral approximation. Note that $\tau$ acts on the *left* algebra: a rank-one left projection on an infinite-dimensional physical Hilbert space still acts on infinitely many right indices in its Hilbert–Schmidt representation. [Manuscript, pp. 77–78](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=77) · [Colafranceschi et al., §§3.2, 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02189#page=20)
2. **Positive Euclidean time and the inverse construction, (E.8)–(E.12).** By the spectral theorem, $C(s)=e^{-sH/\hbar}$ satisfies $C(s)=C(s/2)^*C(s/2)$, the printed norm bound, strong convergence $C(s)\to 1$ as $s\downarrow 0$, and (E.9) because the integrand has finite-trace support. Conversely, a positive bounded cylinder semigroup with the assumed continuity and dense short-cylinder ranges has trivial joint kernel: if $C(s_0)v=0$, then $C(s)^k v=0$ for $ks>s_0$ by the semigroup law, so positivity forces $C(s)v=0$ for all $s>0$. Taking rational powers and using continuity yields (E.11), and $\log C(s_0)$ has a dense domain even when $0$ lies in the continuous spectrum of $C(s_0)$. Whenever $\tau(C(s_0))<\infty$, the spectral bound $1_{(-\infty,R]}(H)\le e^{s_0 R/\hbar}C(s_0)$ proves (E.12), and the cited direct sum of type I factors with integer trace weights implies finite total physical multiplicity below $R$. [Manuscript, p. 78](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=78)
3. **Closed cylinders need their stated finiteness hypothesis.** Compact-band finiteness alone does not ensure finite unfiltered cylinder traces at all lengths. In units $\hbar=1$, a diagonal Hamiltonian with integer energies $n\ge 1$ and multiplicity $2^n$ has finite rank in every bounded energy window and finite networks of energy-filtered operators, yet its unfiltered heat trace $\sum_n 2^n e^{-sn}$ converges only for $s>\log 2$. One finite length $s_0$ suffices for (E.12), whereas a theory with finite closed cylinders at every $s>0$ requires control at all positive lengths; longer open cylinders and energy-filtered cylinders obey the exact properties stated in (E.8)–(E.9).
4. **Matching different theories, (E.13)–(E.14).** The identity $L(F_{g_s})=g(H_L)C_L(s)$ holds inside the Lorentzian construction. Identifying the resulting matrix elements with those between $\iota(a)$ and $\iota(b)$ in an independently defined geometric Euclidean path integral requires a specified correspondence on insertions that preserves all those amplitudes—taking $s\downarrow 0$ proves isometry only *after* that identification is granted. (Defining the Euclidean expressions *by* the Lorentzian products builds the identity into the generated sector, but does not prove equivalence with an independent geometric integral.) Likewise, the density proof covers finite-norm preparations built from the chosen insertions and positive cylinders without showing that every allowed Euclidean source geometry admits such a presentation or that distinct decompositions agree—a consistency condition imposed explicitly in the sentence after (E.9). Because the cited Euclidean framework works with smooth closed boundary-source manifolds and product collars, connecting it to E.1's closed spacetime cobordisms requires an explicit geometric dictionary. [Manuscript, pp. 78–79](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=78) · [Colafranceschi et al., §§2.1–2.3 and 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02189#page=14)

A non-gravitational example illustrates why cylinder agreement leaves other topologies undetermined. Multiply the amplitudes of compact oriented two-dimensional surfaces by $e^{\lambda\chi(M)}$ for any real $\lambda$. Since the Euler characteristic $\chi$ is additive under gluing along circles and under disjoint union, every reflected Gram matrix is conjugated by positive diagonal factors $e^{\lambda\chi(M_i)}$, preserving positivity. Cylinders and closed tori have $\chi=0$ and stay completely unchanged, whereas disks acquire $e^\lambda$ and spheres acquire $e^{2\lambda}$. Identical cylinder operators and thermal traces can therefore coexist with distinct non-cylinder amplitudes; matching non-cylinder preparations is genuine extra input to (E.14).

**Replica weights.** To see precisely where multi-boundary gluing enforces integer dimensions, take an admissible projection $p$ of finite trace $d$ and glue $n$ disjoint copies with all permutations of their left boundaries. Because each cycle contributes one factor of $\operatorname{Tr}(p)=d$, the canonical reflected Gram matrix has entries $d^{\#\mathrm{cycles}(\sigma^{-1}\pi)}$. Evaluating it on alternating signs gives $n!\,d(d-1)\cdots(d-n+1)$; positivity for every $n\ge 1$ forces $d$ to be a nonnegative integer. Passing to general projections in the completed algebra uses the approximation arguments in the cited Euclidean theorem (or their Lorentzian analogues), and integer weights transfer whenever a trace-preserving identification holds on a common sector. [Colafranceschi et al., §4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02189#page=42) · [Manuscript, (E.14)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=79)

## E.3. What first-order gravity realizes

**Closure and positivity, (E.15)–(E.16).** Whenever admissible fillings are represented by operators $\hat a$ on a common cut Hilbert space and geometric closure is given by the ordinary operator trace, the canonical reflected Gram form is $\operatorname{Tr}(\hat a^*\hat b)$, immediately yielding positivity, cyclicity on trace-class products, and tensor-product factorization. Conjugating a clock operator and its domain by a unitary conjugates its spectral projections and proves (E.16), establishing covariance under a shared boundary transformation (with (C.1) implementing changes of boundary variables). What is not proved here is that all closed gravitational spacetime amplitudes equal that operator trace across topologically distinct decompositions. [Manuscript, (C.1) and (E.15)–(E.16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34924v1#page=79)

Two elementary tests highlight the gluing hypotheses that must be tracked explicitly:

- **Intermediate clock projections.** With a clock projection $P$ on the external cuts, the paper itself notes $PXYP=(PXP)(PYP)+PX(1-P)YP$. Imposing $P$ on a newly inserted intermediate cut alters the amplitude whenever the second term is nonzero: for $P=|0\rangle\langle 0|$ and $X=Y$ equal to the swap on $\mathbb C^2$, the uncut product is $P$ whereas the projected intermediate product is $0$. Cut invariance therefore requires keeping careful track of which Hilbert space sits on each intermediate slice. [Manuscript, Appendix C, TeX lines 3166–3172](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#A3.SS3.p2.2)
- **Factorization and connected fillings.** In §5.4, product overlaps determine the matrix entries of $N_M$, and setting the squared norm of a connected filling equal to $\operatorname{Tr}(N_M^*N_M)$ makes the residual orthogonal to product states vanish by Parseval's identity. Product overlaps alone determine only the orthogonal projection onto the span of products—any orthogonal component would add to the squared norm—so (E.15) supplies the decisive extra assumption; factorization of disjoint *closed* amplitudes (E.2) does not by itself prove completeness of product states. [Manuscript, §5.4, TeX lines 2112–2141](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS4.p3.1)

**Finiteness and density, (E.17)–(E.18).** Assuming the finite-dimensional clock subspace of (5.39c) makes the cutoff equal to the identity and yields $|\operatorname{Tr}a|\le d_{\mathscr D}\|a\|$ immediately. In AdS, (E.17) similarly imports both a lower-bounded boundary Hamiltonian and the thermal trace estimate (5.45), which relies on unconditional convergence of the signed closed double sum (5.44). As an explicit test, take $H=0$ on $\ell^2$ ($w_i=1$) and a normalized two-state diamond with equal weights $p=1/2$: each $i$-row of (5.44) contains $+1/4$ and $-1/4$, so summing complete rows gives $0$, yet the unrestricted net over arbitrary finite subsets diverges along with $\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}=\infty$, even though every fixed-vector open sewing amplitude on $\ell^2$ converges by Cauchy–Schwarz. Unconditional convergence of the closed sum would indeed force the claimed thermal finiteness, but open sewing does not supply that premise. [Open sewing (TeX lines 1265–1271)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS4.p3.1) · [Convergence (lines 1370–1378)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S4.SS4.p4.1) · [AdS trace (lines 1865–1892)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S5.SS2.p18.1)

Finally, *given* the finite-rank energy windows and cyclic boundary sector of (5.47)–(5.48), the density statement (E.18) is sound: two-sided compressions $P_\Lambda a P_{\Lambda'}$ converge to $a$ in Hilbert–Schmidt norm by $\sum|a_{mn}|^2<\infty$, and strong operator approximation on finite windows yields (E.18) by a finite sum. Note the domain distinction between the two formulas in (E.15): on $\ell^2$, $a=\operatorname{diag}(1/n)$ is Hilbert–Schmidt (so (E.15b) is finite) yet not trace class (so $\operatorname{Tr}a=\infty$, outside the domain of (E.15a)). All of these realizations also depend on the earlier choice of pure state extension, whose uniqueness or equivalence across choices the manuscript explicitly leaves open.
