# What a referee should ask of the null-cone proposal

**Assessment of arXiv:2611.34924v1, 26 November 2026.** The paper poses an unusually concrete question. Can one start with the gravitational data on the lightlike boundary of a laboratory, define that laboratory's exact quantum dynamics, and deduce how many distinguishable preparations it admits? There is a classical construction, a quantum proposal, and a set of exact calculations for chosen states and sources. The inference from these results to a finite-dimensional four-dimensional theory remains open. Nothing in this report physically refutes finite-dimensional diamonds; the findings are proof gaps, defects in the construction as written, and model counterexamples. The following questions are ordered by logical dependence and foreground calculable or testable criteria ahead of broader interpretive debates. A failure of a particular proof in version 1 would leave other quantizations and the underlying physical conjecture open. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1) · [Observable tests](OBSERVABLES.md)

## 1. Which classical diamonds and variations does the construction cover?

* **Is the clock coordinate a coordinate on the entire stated phase space?** Positive cross-sectional area and an absence of crossings permit an area maximum. The area radius then labels two points on the same ray. A smooth vacuum plane wave exhibits this before its first conjugate point. Specify a strictly expanding patch, add transition maps across turning points, and rederive the clock where its inverse coordinate fails. The affine focusing inequality itself survives a turn. The generality of the single-chart quantization is at issue. [Calculation](audit/03-2.md)
* **Which data must agree to glue two solutions?** Matching the induced metric, both null second fundamental forms and normal connection on the corner matches its intrinsic and embedding data. The radiation profile away from that cut is freely specifiable. Flat spacetime and a vacuum plane wave can agree in all these corner data and differ in curvature there. State the complete characteristic or compatible cap data and the domain-of-dependence theorem being used. Matching the solutions on an open overlap as separately stipulated in §3.6 avoids this counterexample. [Explicit metrics](audit/03-5-6.md)
* **Is the finite-cap two-form closed?** The cap prescription in (3.18) fixes the ratio of two area elements. On the variations permitted by that condition, differentiating the displayed (A.14) leaves an endpoint three-form. For two simple polarization variations and one area variation its coefficient is nonzero even on a shear-free background. The exact polynomial test gives $1/256$ at cap fraction $1/2$ in the audit normalization. Re-derive the potential with moving caps and all joints before inversion, or specify the narrower boundary conditions. Then check that the repair preserves the area variations used in §5. A closed covariant potential for the full system is compatible with a term having been lost in its reduction. [Derivation](audit/03-3.md) · [Independent endpoint calculation](audit/cap-crosscheck.md)
* **On which phase space can the sharp clock generate a flow?** Two vacuum packets leave the local data at the cap rim and the waist unchanged to first order. The mixed clock Hessian of their signed combination with a fixed reference wave tends to $-3/4$. On the usual smooth matched Einstein phase space admitting these tests, local angular, boost and displacement charges cannot supply a $C^1$ generator through flat space for the matched-radius clock. The same conclusion holds at varying waist area if the exterior frame carries the total-area boost. If an alternative uses frames determined by bulk data, derive their response to arbitrary radial quadrupole profiles and check the finite-rank obstruction. For a distributional generator, specify its domain and verify its brackets with smooth probes. [Rim matching and scope](audit/rim-matching.md) · [Packet and rank tests](audit/match-packets.md) · [Varying area](audit/match-area.md)
* **Is the surface charge the Hamiltonian for all the claimed translations?** Deriving the Komar form is only part of the variation. The flux term in the Hamiltonian variation can contribute. For a single round Schwarzschild sphere the charge for unit asymptotic time is $M/2$, while the Hamiltonian varies by $\delta M$. Compute the field-space curl with the allowed radiative data, including both caps, and demonstrate cancellation or specify the boundary conditions or additional boundary degrees of freedom. Invert the corrected two-form including its homogeneous endpoint kernel. These decisions feed directly into the quantum generator. [Hamiltonian calculation](audit/03-4.md) · [Symplectic calculation](audit/03-3.md)

## 2. Does the algebra give an interacting quantum theory?

* **Which representation and which amplitudes are physically selected?** Finite collections of canonical pairs have Gaussian wavefunctions and ladder operators. Infinitely many pairs admit inequivalent choices of vacuum widths. The choice of a pure extension gives an irreducible representation and hence a type I factor. Type I includes ordinary quantum mechanics on an infinite-dimensional space. Construct the full regular representation, specify smearing classes, and calculate matrix elements between different areas. Show uniqueness where claimed, or describe which observables distinguish the choices. [Gaussian representation](audit/04-2.md)
* **Does the full Hamiltonian actually generate a unitary group?** Assigning a unitary to an admitted action deformation gives identities in an algebra. To predict the result of a gravitational experiment one needs to identify that unitary with evolution under the reduced interacting Hamiltonian. Give a common dense domain, self-adjointness or a well-posed time-dependent propagator, and cutoff-independent matrix elements. Verify two different decompositions of the same region with the same external preparations. For nonlinear changes of canonical variables, derive the quantum ordering correction or the intertwining operator. The tested additive shift identities and bounded-source Dyson expansions survive these questions. [Evolution](audit/evolution.md) · [Ordering and source algebra](audit/04-1.md)
* **Does the focusing clock exist on any physical quantum state when the regulators come off?** Positive bounded truncations can converge to a form whose domain contains only zero. Equation (5.16) uses a classical Gaussian phase-space probability to estimate a function of noncommuting operators. An exactly soluble, unnested one-pair counterexample has Gaussian tails tending to zero in phase space while every quantum vector is excluded from the limiting form domain. This tests the inference rule, not the gravitational operator directly: nesting shifts the quadratic threshold from $1$ to $1.19968$, and a path-ordered exponential preserves a dense domain at every coupling, offering a concrete constructive option to test. The gravitational case requires a bound for its own variables. Start with one normalized vector of finite limiting form and nonzero overlap with the proposed packet. Stronger results would control nearby coherent states, all angular and radial modes, and strong commutation of the clock with the area. [Existence and sufficient criterion](audit/05-1.md) · [Clock and sewing review](reviews/fable-clock-sewing.md)
* **What defines the square of the quantum shear?** Local fluctuations have a singular two-point function. The positive bare square enters a clock exponential, while the semiclassical focusing estimate uses a vacuum-subtracted product. Vacuum subtraction can change positivity. A linearized radial-mode calculation with the paper's example widths gives a mean derivative square growing like the square of the mode cutoff; in two regulated quadratic models, even the state-independent lowest clock eigenvalue diverges with resolution, emptying any fixed window. Whether the full nonlinear two-polarization clock behaves the same way remains open. Give a regulated prescription for the composite observable and prove both its physical short-distance limit and the lower bound on which the state count relies. Test the result against different regulators and against the two gravitational polarizations. [Boundary operators](audit/04-3.md) · [Shear test](audit/shear-uv.md)

## 3. Can a bounded clock really leave only finitely many states?

**The most consequential question is whether closing a cut has been defined independently of the number of states.** For a specified initial and final state, the two factors in the intermediate sum are components of vectors of finite norm. Cauchy–Schwarz proves absolute convergence. For a closed cut one has to sum over initial states as well. On the clock-selected space put $s=A/(4G_N\hbar)$. If the proposed clock-area bound holds, $0\le s\le s_*$. For any fixed Gaussian area filter the reflected weight $W=C_b^\dagger C_b=e^{-s}f_b(s)$ has a positive lower bound $c$. Therefore, for every finite-rank projection $P$,

$$
c\,\mathrm{rank}(P)\le \mathrm{Tr}(PW)\le \mathrm{rank}(P).
$$

Finiteness of the ordinary unnormalized trace is already equivalent to finite dimension. This is why a proof of convergence for a different contraction cannot by itself supply the missing result. [Open sewing](audit/04-gluing.md) · [Closed sewing](audit/05-2.md)

The paper offers a more subtle argument than simply postulating this finite trace. It predicts a change in a two-sided local measurement when the right-hand frame is boosted. It groups intermediate basis states into cycles whose areas are mostly close together. The boost then acts almost as a common phase in each truncated preparation. After subtracting a constant the cut and uncut answers have opposite signs. The finite-projection estimates work. What remains to prove is that every such exhaustion evaluates the *same exact closed observable* as the uncut calculation. In an explicit two-area example with infinite multiplicities, two exhaustive orders yield different boost contrasts. Both recover the identity strongly and obey open sewing. The example isolates the additional gravitational assertion. [Sign argument and example](audit/dimension-crosscheck.md)

Three lines of attack could distinguish the possibilities.

1. **Work directly with the spectrum.** Construct the limiting positive clock $T$ and prove a coercive form inequality controlling all physical radial, angular and edge excitations by an operator of compact resolvent. Finite rank of every bounded spectral projection follows. A sequence of orthonormal physical states with uniformly bounded clock-form expectation would refute compact resolvent. Respect gauge equivalence and cap constraints in constructing the sequence. The angular budget used in Appendix D.3 needs a derivation from the actual projector. [Focusing and state count](audit/D3.md)
2. **Prove closed sewing for the boost observable directly.** For the specified bounded boost-sensitive observable, give a uniform estimate showing convergence to the uncut result for all exhaustive cut projections. Given the cycle family of (D.25), the unnormalized signed insertion carries a strictly negative margin per state, so bounded partial sums along those cycles are already equivalent to finite rank—even without using the sign of the uncut answer. A proof based on a normalized measurement would need its own independent, regulator-uniform control. Compute the uncut signal in the exact filtered preparation, with an error bound preserving its sign. The freely falling vacuum ratio $4e^\eta/(1+e^\eta)^2$ follows, but a Gaussian filter of area alone does not force every polarization into its local vacuum. [Graviton correlator](audit/D2.md) · [Independent sewing test](audit/compactness-stress.md)
3. **Use replicas with a controlled error.** Establish a positive lower bound on the exact regulated ratio between the cyclic two-copy trace and the product of the single-copy traces, uniformly as the cutoffs are removed. With the positive lower weight above, this is a rank diagnostic. The regular one-loop saddle supplies a candidate contribution. Control all remaining sectors, zero modes and measures before interpreting it as a bound on a Hilbert-space trace. A measure integral over continuous area values can be finite even though multiplication by the same function has infinite Hilbert-space trace. [Appendix D.3](audit/D3.md)

If finite dimension is established, the clock-bounded area operator has finitely many distinct eigenvalues. Their values and multiplicities remain separate outputs. A bounded operator on its own can have continuous spectrum or eigenvalues with infinite multiplicity. There is also an operational question about how the continuum canonical observables act after projection. An exact canonical pair cannot act internally on a finite-dimensional space because the trace of its commutator is zero. The paper allows insertions to leave the subspace between projections. Compute those matrix elements and quantify the range of observables for which semiclassical field theory emerges. [Boundary compression](audit/04-3.md)

## 4. How much of the black-hole entropy is derived?

The boost generator gives an especially clean check. Its magnitude is $A/(8\pi G_N)$. Continuing a half-turn to $i\pi$ and pairing it with its reflection yields a weight $e^{-s}$. For $Z=\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-s})<\infty$, the entropy identity $S=\langle s\rangle+\log Z$ follows. At a single sharp area with multiplicity $d$, normalization simply gives $S=\log d$. The boost determines the weight of each state. The number of states with that weight still has to be computed. [Entropy derivation](audit/05-3.md)

* **Is the trace dominated by the assumed large Schwarzschild family?** The clock-scaling calculation needs controlled average delay, a stable ratio of average to maximum reading, and a consistent family of nested cutoffs and traces. Moreover, the surface integrand $R[q]+2\Theta_{(\ell)}\Theta_{(n)}$ integrates to $+8\pi$ on a marginal round sphere and $-8\pi$ on a flat round sphere, so (5.67c) is conditioned on a marginal horizon saddle that the clock trace does not by itself select. Establish these properties in the constructed quantum ensemble. They are stronger than the classical observation that a round shear-free cone saturates a bound.
* **What ensemble does the determinant actually count?** A fixed area, a Gaussian window whose width grows as the square root of area in Planck units, and a density per unit mass carry different logarithms. For instance a boost-weighted density proportional to $s^p ds$ gives a packet trace proportional to $s_b^{p+1/2}$. Derive the collective-coordinate measure, gauge and ghost factors, zero modes, cap and joint boundary conditions, and weight for the area packet. Then calculate its determinant directly and transform to fixed mass with angular momenta summed. The intermediate clock-ensemble power $257/90$ (which belongs to the clock trace and is not directly comparable with Sen's fixed-mass numbers), the fixed-mass power $106/45$, and the match to Sen's spin-summed ensemble are arithmetically consistent given the paper's assignment of the packet power. Because both calculations use the same local heat-kernel coefficient $53/45$, this checks the ensemble conversion rather than supplying an independent state count. There is no demonstrated universal half-unit error; there is an unverified ensemble identification. [Ensemble cross-check](audit/ensemble-crosscheck.md) · [Sen comparison](audit/sen.md)
* **Which horizons share the result?** A corner boost can be attached to a smooth marginal surface under the paper's conditions. The large-area logarithm is calculated about a Schwarzschild saddle with pure gravity. For a dynamical marginal surface, rotating hole, or additional massless fields the state, zero modes, determinant and physical ensemble must be specified afresh. [Section 5.3](audit/05-3.md)

## 5. Do the proposed dynamics pass independent physical tests?

* **Interacting gravity.** The §4.4 pair correlator gives a clean example of a nonuniform expansion. It can be reproduced with uncoupled oscillators and a deliberately coupling-dependent pulse separation. Compute a gauge-invariant observable that requires gravitational self-interactions. A particularly discriminating target is the universal scale dependence of the two-loop four-graviton amplitude, using a controlled large-diamond/flat-space limit and matched treatment of infrared radiation. A finite answer alone is insufficient. It must also reproduce the known long-distance coefficients and state normalizations. [Pair reproduction](audit/04-pair.md) · [Scattering comparison](audit/scattering.md)
* **Classical limit and backreaction.** The starting reduced phase space already satisfies the vacuum Einstein equations. Recovering them tests preservation of the input dynamics. The one-loop Taylor expansion about a classical solution gives the expected form of backreaction at fixed smearing. Derive the renormalized local stress contribution on a specified curved background with the stated cap conditions, verify its conservation and gauge/dressing dependence, and compare a relational observable with effective field theory. The coincident derivative contraction requires a subtraction and renormalization prescription. [Classical saddle](audit/06-1.md) · [Independent plane-wave and flat-space tests](audit/06-2.md)
* **Topology, factorization and AdS.** Prove completeness of one-boundary preparations on the two-boundary space before inferring that residual topology states have zero norm. In the AdS extension, prove unconditional closed thermal sewing or establish heat-trace finiteness independently. For the paper's centered insertion the absolute sum equals $2p(1-p)Z_\beta$. A cancellation in each row is compatible with an infinite thermal trace. Appendix E's Euclidean correspondence also requires identifying the allowed preparations and geometric source insertions beyond a single continued cylinder. [Topology](audit/05-4.md) · [AdS test](audit/area-ads.md) · [Axioms](audit/E.md)

## Two concrete attempts to falsify finite dimension

**Can the shape of the corner carry arbitrarily many distinguishable states?** Keep the total corner area fixed and smear its local area density with a nonconstant smooth function $h$ on the sphere. In a classical shape chart $f=q h+f_0$, differentiating the smeared area with respect to $q$ gives a positive multiple of the variance of $h$ in the local area distribution. Classically, in the smooth-vertex form (3.42a), the smeared local area density generates a shift of the relative boost angle $\Gamma(y)$ alone and leaves the clock-bounded region invariant. Thus a continuous shape coordinate can encode information even at a single total area. Transferring this to the quantum theory hinges on three decisive steps: establishing that the physically marked charge survives the gauge quotient and is realized cleanly in the canonical chart of (4.7), proving that its spectral projections preserve the ordered physical clock window, and verifying that its spectrum in at least one allowed state has a continuous component. If all three hold, partitioning that continuous component produces arbitrarily many nonzero orthogonal states with the same clock bound. The proof in the paper controls total area; the stronger commutation and spectral statements for local area remain to be established. [Corner and gauge conditions](audit/finite-kinematics.md) · [Fable's corner-charge review](reviews/fable.md)

**Can a family of gravitational waves fit under a fixed clock bound?** Fix the radius, cap ratio, total area, physical clock limit and units while raising the radial and angular mode cutoffs. For an orthonormal set of ambient one-graviton states $e_j$, calculate the true acceptance probabilities $p_j=\|P_\ell e_j\|^2$. For any finite family, $\sum_j p_j\leq\mathrm{rank}(P_\ell)$ by Bessel's inequality. Divergence of these sums for physical modes at fixed clock would falsify finite rank, even when the projected packets overlap. Small acceptance for the few states simulated does not prove finite rank. Proving finite rank requires an upper bound covering the untested modes, constraints and boundary variables. A shear-free corner model provides a control with a known infinite-rank answer against which to calibrate finite-matrix numerics. [Regulated investigation](audit/regulator-program.md)

## Scope of the objections

Two public AI-assisted technical reports checked finite calculations without external specialist peer review, and one also raised the closed-trace objection. Separately, Jonathan Oppenheim published a specialist critique of the microstate count, diffeomorphism invariance, and recovery of Einstein dynamics; his essay also reports a model-assisted 21-item canonical-quantization check of which he reviewed selected parts. Each argument here is assessed against the equations and the specific measurement it would affect. [Public responses](audit/context.md) · [Assessment of Oppenheim's three checks](audit/oppenheim-obstacles.md) · [Canonical methods in four dimensions](audit/canonical-debate.md)

The proof gaps above bear on claims explicitly made in v1. Including matter, obtaining a radiation Page curve, describing passage through a curvature singularity, and predicting a measured spectral line are different projects. An equilibrium two-sided correlator gives no collapse-to-evaporation channel. A discrete spectrum without energy gaps, couplings and transition rates gives no line prediction. A regular null patch gives no continuation through a curvature singularity. Those distinctions set the stakes of further work without counting every attractive application as a flaw. [Physical implications and possible tests](audit/implications.md)

For an efficient research sequence, I would first repair the finite-cap phase space and construct a regulated clock on it. Next I would try both to prove compactness and to defeat it with angular or edge excitations. Either outcome would be informative. In parallel, a direct determinant in a specified ensemble, an interacting low-energy amplitude, and the concrete measurement checks in [OBSERVABLES.md](OBSERVABLES.md) would supply independent tests that do not require the state-count conjecture to be true.
