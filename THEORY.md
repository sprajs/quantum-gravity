# The next theoretical calculations

The strongest test of the original four-dimensional claim is to construct the clock on the **physical state space at fixed diamond size**. This requires the cap and corner variables that enter its brackets. A simultaneous large-size limit can make quantum fluctuations look small while concealing failure of the clock at any fixed size. The assessment below concerns version 1. [Verification by section and appendix](audit/verification-scope.md) · [Versioned manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1)

## Which gaps have narrowed?

The calculations below resolve specific classical checks and isolate sharp operator criteria, while underscoring why classical boundary recovery and model clock constructions remain distinct from establishing finite rank in nonperturbative four-dimensional quantum gravity.

**Bulk action.** For the general affine metric with arbitrary angular metric, shift and metric function $F$ at affine normalization $N=1$, the Einstein–Hilbert density equals the complete expression in (B.10)–(B.11), including all three total-derivative currents. A geometric curvature decomposition and an independent exact identity in arbitrary metric derivatives agree without imposing Einstein’s equations. This closes the earlier bulk-density check. [General proof](audit/bulk-identity.md)

**Boundary dynamics.** Differentiating the characteristic shear potential with the physical endpoints allowed to move supplies exactly the missing rim term for both polarizations in the fixed-label sector, repairing that specific classical nonclosure defect without incorporating the spacelike cap Cauchy data or constraining the quantum state count. The [joint cap and sheet calculation](audit/joint-clock.md) tests how the repaired form acts on a sharp clock. On the isolated null block, a concrete clock variation depends on a cap value that no square-integrable interior source can represent, and in a radiating region the cap swept out in time carries symplectic flux. [Rim derivation](audit/rim-potential.md) · [Inverse and clock example](audit/endpoint-inverse.md) · [Charge and radiative flux](audit/radiative-flux.md)

**Clock existence and dimension.** Two continuum model constructions give decisive answers within their own assumptions without deciding the full interacting four-dimensional rank. A bare clock in a fixed-background commutator model has a divergent lower spectral bound that empties every fixed window. A clock with fixed detector smoothing in linearized gravity accepts the Fock vacuum with probability bounded away from zero and accepts infinitely many independent directions above its vacuum mean. There is also an instability on a smooth fixed-density null sheet. A third, later pulse supplies a canonical pair in its induced symplectic form. When that restricted family is quantized, an arbitrarily small prescribed second polarization destroys the dense exponential form domain. Realizing the same pair and incompatible clock weights after quantizing the full constrained theory remains unproved. A comparison of coordinate-defined vacua on a curved null cone supplies a separate test of how the state and source would have to transform under a change of cut. [Bare model](audit/clock-spectrum.md) · [Constructive model](audit/clock-construction.md) · [Nonlinear domain test](audit/clock-domain-jump.md) · [State transport](audit/cut-anomaly.md)

**Testing the vacuum state count.** The area in a marked part of the corner can obstruct finite rank if its physical angular transformations and commutation with the clock survive quantization. A second method asks how accurately every normalized boundary preparation can be approximated within a common finite-dimensional space. A verified uniform error below the derived threshold would give an upper bound on the physical rank without presupposing a closed trace, whereas checking only a restricted collection of preparations can give a false positive. [Corner area and its transformations](audit/corner-commutant.md) · [Finite-rank approximation and detector criteria](audit/sewing-repair.md)

**Gravity beyond a Gaussian overlap.** Two crossing vacuum waves give an exact classical transfer law to first order in the weaker wave and all orders in the stronger one before focusing. A separate Schwarzschild calculation selects the horizon as the unique marginal radial graph on a fixed ingoing sheet and tests how its clock changes when the cut is deformed. These results supply classical and geometric targets that constrain a future derivation of interacting quantum evolution and of the horizon ensemble. [Crossing-wave calculation](audit/interacting-test.md) · [Horizon and determinant](audit/entropy-determinant.md)

## A first matter model

Take the simplest local field with propagating modes in four dimensions, a real, minimally coupled massless scalar. In units $c=\hbar=1$ its action is $S_\phi=-\tfrac12\int d\phi\wedge *d\phi$. Variation gives the wave equation $d*d\phi=0$ and the boundary potential $\Theta_\phi=-\delta\phi\,*d\phi$. In a fixed embedding the scalar symplectic current is
$$
 \omega_\phi(\delta_1,\delta_2)=\delta_1\phi\,\delta_2(*d\phi)
       -\delta_2\phi\,\delta_1(*d\phi).
$$
The metric variation of the Hodge star matters in the coupled problem. A moving cap also changes the integration surface. Deriving the full reduced brackets and matching both sheets with these terms is still necessary. Restricting to a constant scalar with $\delta\phi=0$ recovers the original vacuum cap test, so just appending a scalar to the action cannot cancel its defect.

Three calculations specify targets for the extension.

* **Exact classical geometry.** An exact homogeneous Einstein–scalar solution gives a diamond with two regular null sheets, both caps, a common waist and two calculable affine readings. Even without gravitational shear, the scalar's positive null stress increases both clock readings above a flat diamond matched to the same waist area. A candidate coupled theory should reproduce the readings and their small-diamond limit with these boundary data. [Exact diamond and control values](audit/matter-diamond.md)
* **Semiclassical mean metric (quantum sign).** Prepare the same normalized spherical scalar packet in a coherent superposition of zero and two particles. Reversing the relative sign preserves its particle-number probabilities and total mean energy, yet flips the sign of the leading semiclassical mean-metric clock excess (relative to the matched physical waist radius, for $R\gg\ell_P$) on **both** sheets. For the past-tip reading the two coefficients are $-0.0114211$ and $+0.0159152$ in units of $\ell_P^2/R$. This tests the proposed matter coupling and renormalized stress prescription on the mean metric (omitting graviton fluctuations); predicting single-run fluctuations requires a renormalized quantum clock operator. [States, integrals and future-tip result](audit/matter-null-energy.md)
* **Conditional scalar rank.** Decide what it means to measure the scalar's absolute value at a relationally specified boundary. If a noncompact scalar shift is an exact physical symmetry preserving a nonempty clock window and translating such a readout, finite rank is impossible. Fixing the boundary value, gauging the shift, compactifying the scalar, or breaking the symmetry changes the hypotheses and what states it makes sense to count. [Rank theorem and characteristic-data model](audit/matter-state-count.md)

These results supply exact classical, semiclassical mean-metric, and conditional symmetry benchmarks for a four-dimensional matter extension. A derivation of the **interacting quantum matter theory** still requires its physical inner product, its cap and overlap matching, and its renormalized observables. None of these benchmarks by itself decides finite dimension in vacuum gravity.

A matched two-parameter Einstein–scalar family goes beyond a single cone. Its two capped sheets, shift charge and scalar calibration are all specified. Quantizing the induced homogeneous phase space provides an explicit infinite-rank example with both clocks bounded when the shifted exterior is admitted. Embedding that sector in the physical quantum theory is a separate question. [Two-sheet shift charge and quantization](audit/matter-boundary.md)

## The joint cap and sheet

The sharp clock calibrates affine distance at the cap. In a symmetric vacuum plane wave, fix one freely falling emitter's boost by a local tidal reading. At this background the clock's first variation is gauge invariant. Propagating its source in the surrounding spacetime gives mixed brackets approaching $-0.060534$ against smooth gravitational probes in the half-radius experiment. The same limit holds when the return is set by proper duration or adjusted to keep the weighted waist radius fixed. The ordinary joined current with any fixed smooth metric response tends to zero in the same pulse test. In a different preparation, averaging over emitter positions in a Kasner spacetime gives a smooth generator at the symmetric background. [Single-emitter source and causal response](audit/weak-curved.md) · [Explicit tensor Green function](audit/weak-rosen-green.md) · [Curved-rim calculation](audit/match-curved.md) · [Kasner probe](audit/joint-jacobi.md)

**Flat diamond with independently marked cuts.** In the single-packet experiment a one-sided angular charge can reproduce $-1/30$. Two packets on opposite sides of the rim leave all local cut data unchanged to first order on an admissible lift. The mixed clock Hessian of their signed combination with a fixed reference wave tends to $-3/4$, while its Einstein pairing with each fixed smooth response tends to zero. Thus on a regular matched-radius reduction admitting those tests, even independent local angular and boost marks cannot supply a $C^1$ family of smooth generators through flat space. If the outer waist retains its uniform area boost, the obstruction applies to one sharp averaged clock with freely varying waist area and shape. [Comparison maps](audit/match-reduction.md) · [Wave packets and hypotheses](audit/match-packets.md) · [Exterior waist charge](audit/match-area.md)

A frame transported through the cap interior can detect a packet missed by the local cut data. A further test rules out any fixed finite number of independent linear responses to the radial quadrupole profiles as a repair under the same smooth bulk assumptions. A proposed extension must identify which profiles its boundary data retain or which packet variations its boundary conditions exclude. [Matching results and remaining cases](audit/rim-matching.md) · [Finite-rank test](audit/match-packets.md#a-rank-test-for-frames-determined-by-cap-data)

**Distributional response of the flat spherical clock.** Fix a smooth reference perturbation of the round diamond. The mixed derivative of the clock excess against a second wave integrates that wave's linearized Weyl curvature over the marked cone. The causal gravitational propagator takes the resulting conserved source to an explicit distributional metric field on Minkowski space. On the filled diamond, its data on the closed waist disk give the same answer as first adding the smooth cap and sheet currents and then removing the source regulator. Tests are vacuum solutions smooth up to the boundary, with one metric and common marks at the seam. In a radial quadrupole sector an explicit inverse in the master wave variable recovers both packet limits. [Spherical generator](audit/spherical-generator.md) · [Curvature source](audit/weak-tidal.md) · [Closed-disk pairing](audit/weak-boundary.md)

For the positive-frequency Minkowski graviton vacuum, nonzero reference shear at the rim gives a logarithmically divergent squared one-particle norm in the radial quadrupole sector. This rules out representing that linear response as a one-particle displacement in that representation. The full quantum clock and the rank of its bounded window remain open. [Fock norm and hypotheses](audit/weak-fock.md) · [Three-patch quantum domain test](audit/clock-domain-jump.md) · [Rank criteria](audit/sewing-repair.md)

Translate the reference quadrupole and the emitter together by a proper time $\tau$. For unit rim slope and a vanishing vertex jump, the bracket between the two mixed clock responses approaches $+G_N/30$ from positive delays and $-G_N/30$ from negative delays at $R=1$, $a=1/3$. Identical timing windows give zero. Two windows with the same center and variance can instead approach $-G_N/150$. The timing profile therefore enters a sharp coincidence limit even after fixing those two moments. This is a statement about the linearized response.
